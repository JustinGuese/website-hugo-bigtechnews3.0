Tim Andrews, 66, was too weak to walk when he received a genetically modified [pig kidney in January](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/uCUbXb4VsLP7mexke8sjjw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMDQtdGVjaHxhLXBpZy1raWRuZXktYm91Z2h0LW5pbmUtbW9udGhzLW9mLWxpZmV8fGh0dHBzOi8vd3d3Lm55dGltZXMuY29tLzIwMjYvMDkvMDMvd2VsbC9waWcta2lkbmV5LWJyaWRnZS10cmFuc3BsYW50Lmh0bWw_dW5sb2NrZWRfYXJ0aWNsZV9jb2RlPTEuLWxBLmItUlcuckhWX1ZaaFdrMXBwJnNtaWQ9dXJsLXNoYXJlJnV0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ); he lived with it for nine months before a human donor kidney became available. Surgeons have been implanting pig organs for a couple of years, but this is the first case where the xenograft was explicitly used as a bridge rather than a destination. For health-adjacent engineers, the relevant pattern is the same one transplant programs have been chasing for decades: matching patients to a finite donor pool in real time, only now with a backup organ that can hold the patient while they wait.

[Benedict Evans argues](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/4jfFRVoKwglW7iyeW-6YbA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMDQtdGVjaHx0d28tdmlld3Mtb2Ytd2hhdC1haS1kb2VzLXRvLXNvZnR3YXJlfHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5iZW4tZXZhbnMuY29tL2JlbmVkaWN0ZXZhbnMvMjAyNi85LzMvYWktdG9vbHMtYW5kLXRyYW5zZm9ybWF0aW9uP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) that AI will not "sweep away" the long tail of corporate software, because most knowledge workers do not think in terms of building tools, and the value of software is institutional adoption, not generation. Large companies already run hundreds to thousands of apps, scripts, and shadow IT assets, and the bottleneck is recognising you need a tool, not producing one. [Frontier AI makes a complementary point](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/dYUyfqCxdbBOLq82DbkeUA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMDQtdGVjaHx0d28tdmlld3Mtb2Ytd2hhdC1haS1kb2VzLXRvLXNvZnR3YXJlfHxodHRwczovL2Zyb250aWVyYWkuc3Vic3RhY2suY29tL3AvbGxtcy1hcmUtYmVjb21pbmctY29tbW9kaXRpZXMtZGUxP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ): model quality is converging (Claude 3 Opus on par with GPT-4 Turbo on Elo, Llama 3 within ~4%, Gemini 1.5 Pro within 1.5% of GPT-4o), GPT-4o cut cost and latency 2x versus GPT-4 Turbo in six months, and differentiation is moving from the model to the harness. The practical synthesis for builders: pick a model provider on price and latency, then fight over onboarding, templates, data, and workflow integration.

<h2 id="grok-bot-designed-around-persistent-agents"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/F00NSHqld-EzKaYhj7TlZA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMDQtdGVjaHxncm9rLWJvdC1kZXNpZ25lZC1hcm91bmQtcGVyc2lzdGVudC1hZ2VudHN8fGh0dHBzOi8veC5haS9uZXdzL2Rlc2lnbmluZy1ncm9rLWJvdD91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Grok Bot, designed around persistent agents</a></h2>

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/F00NSHqld-EzKaYhj7TlZA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMDQtdGVjaHxncm9rLWJvdC1kZXNpZ25lZC1hcm91bmQtcGVyc2lzdGVudC1hZ2VudHN8fGh0dHBzOi8veC5haS9uZXdzL2Rlc2lnbmluZy1ncm9rLWJvdD91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: x.ai ↗</a></p>

xAI's [Grok Bot design rationale](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/F00NSHqld-EzKaYhj7TlZA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMDQtdGVjaHxncm9rLWJvdC1kZXNpZ25lZC1hcm91bmQtcGVyc2lzdGVudC1hZ2VudHN8fGh0dHBzOi8veC5haS9uZXdzL2Rlc2lnbmluZy1ncm9rLWJvdD91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) is a useful concrete read for anyone building agent products. The interface is built on five primitives: Bots (persistent agents with identity, memory, runtime, and tools), Chats (the conversational surface), Prompts (one-off, saved as Skills, or auto-triggered as Routines), Tools (APIs, shell, computer use), and Artifacts (durable outputs like docs and code). The interesting decision is what to hide: models, context windows, and sandboxes are deliberately not in the user-facing vocabulary. That split is the most copyable idea in the post.

<h2 id="zoom-is-sniffing-macos-unified-logging-to-find-google-meet"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/rJJlqeK4dQGJ07uUvFwSMQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMDQtdGVjaHx6b29tLWlzLXNuaWZmaW5nLW1hY29zLXVuaWZpZWQtbG9nZ2luZy10by1maW5kLWdvb2dsZS1tZWV0fHxodHRwczovL3h1c2hlbmcuZGV2L3Bvc3RzL3JldmVyc2luZy96b29tX215X25vdGVzX2RldGVjdG9yL21haW4vP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Zoom is sniffing macOS Unified Logging to find Google Meet</a></h2>

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/rJJlqeK4dQGJ07uUvFwSMQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMDQtdGVjaHx6b29tLWlzLXNuaWZmaW5nLW1hY29zLXVuaWZpZWQtbG9nZ2luZy10by1maW5kLWdvb2dsZS1tZWV0fHxodHRwczovL3h1c2hlbmcuZGV2L3Bvc3RzL3JldmVyc2luZy96b29tX215X25vdGVzX2RldGVjdG9yL21haW4vP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: xusheng.dev ↗</a></p>

Reverse-engineering of Zoom Workplace 7.1.5 (84650, arm64) on macOS shows that [Zoom's "My Notes" feature](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/rJJlqeK4dQGJ07uUvFwSMQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMDQtdGVjaHx6b29tLWlzLXNuaWZmaW5nLW1hY29zLXVuaWZpZWQtbG9nZ2luZy10by1maW5kLWdvb2dsZS1tZWV0fHxodHRwczovL3h1c2hlbmcuZGV2L3Bvc3RzL3JldmVyc2luZy96b29tX215X25vdGVzX2RldGVjdG9yL21haW4vP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) detects third-party meetings (Google Meet, Teams, Slack) by tailing the macOS Unified Logging stream for the `com.apple.controlcenter` sensor-indicators subsystem and parsing lines like `Sorted active attributions from SystemStatus update: [mic] Google Chrome (com.google.Chrome)`. On Windows, it reads the per-user Capability Access Manager microphone records in the registry. Neither path requires admin rights or Zoom's own microphone permission, which is the privacy hook: any engineer shipping a Mac or Windows app that wants to know what peripherals other apps are using can do the same thing without the user ever granting access.

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/GDud1k3N3Ib2LTUa0CEvxA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMDQtdGVjaHxtZXRhcy10d28tdGllci1hcGktYW5kLXRoZS1wcmljZS1vZi10cmFpbmluZy1kYXRhfHxodHRwczovL3RvbXR1bmd1ei5jb20vdGhlLWFkcy1tb2RlbC1mb3ItcHJvbXB0cy12ZXJ0aWNhbGx5LWludGVncmF0ZXMtYWk_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: tomtunguz.com ↗</a></p>

[Tom Tunguz breaks down](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/GDud1k3N3Ib2LTUa0CEvxA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMDQtdGVjaHxtZXRhcy10d28tdGllci1hcGktYW5kLXRoZS1wcmljZS1vZi10cmFpbmluZy1kYXRhfHxodHRwczovL3RvbXR1bmd1ei5jb20vdGhlLWFkcy1tb2RlbC1mb3ItcHJvbXB0cy12ZXJ0aWNhbGx5LWludGVncmF0ZXMtYWk_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t) Meta's "Muse Spark" launch, which introduced a two-tier API: a Standard tier (`muse-spark-1.3`) at $1.25/M input and $4.25/M output tokens with zero data retention, and a Contributor tier (`muse-spark-1.3-contributor`) at $0.10/M input and $0.20/M output, in exchange for Meta training on customer data, a roughly 92% discount. The ~$1.24/M token spread is Meta's implicit valuation of training data, and the move unbundles the $20/month consumer subscription by making the data-for-discount barter explicit per token. The precedent matters: tiered API pricing tied to data rights rather than compute is now a pattern other providers can copy.

[PostgreSQL 19 Beta 3](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/Q3ZuSEHnNRsXj0l7A8kSBA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMDQtdGVjaHxwb3N0Z3Jlc3FsLTE5LWxhbmRzLXNvb24td2l0aC1tb3JlLWJyZWFrYWdlcy10aGFuLXVzdWFsfHxodHRwczovL3RhcG91ZWgub3JnL2Jsb2cvMjAyNi8wOS9nZXR0aW5nLXJlYWR5LWZvci1wb3N0Z3Jlc3FsLTE5Lz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) shipped on August 13, with GA expected in the September/October window. Test those paths before you schedule the upgrade window.
