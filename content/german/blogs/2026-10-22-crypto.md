<h2 id="occ-issues-final-genius-act-rule-for-stablecoin-issuers">OCC Issues Final GENIUS Act Rule for Stablecoin Issuers</h2>

The OCC finalized its regulatory framework under the Trump-signed GENIUS Act, taking effect on January 18. The rule governs domestic bank issuance, foreign stablecoin recognition, non-bank registration pathways, and federal custody reserves. The OCC held firm on banning issuers from directly paying yields to holders. However, it scrapped a contested clause from the draft proposal that presumed third-party reward relationships were unlawful, conceding that no evidence exists of widespread abusive structures.

Instead of a blanket ban, third-party distribution rewards will face case-by-case scrutiny. That leaves room for fintechs, exchanges, and custodial neobanks to distribute yield on stablecoin deposits as long as the arrangements do not constitute obvious regulatory evasion. Reserve assets must still be strictly segregated on a one-to-one basis, but the concession on partner yields removes an existential barrier for consumer-facing crypto rails.

<h2 id="utah-sues-kalshi-and-market-makers-over-unlicensed-betting">Utah Sues Kalshi and Market Makers Over Unlicensed Betting</h2>

Utah's attorney general filed a sweeping lawsuit targeting five Kalshi entities, alongside trading firms Susquehanna International Group and Susquehanna Government Products. The state claims Kalshi operates an unlawful gambling business, arguing that labeling wagers as CFTC-supervised "event contracts" does not bypass state criminal gambling statutes. 

Critically, this complaint goes after liquidity providers. By suing Susquehanna, Utah opened the first state enforcement action against the dedicated market makers quoting order books on prediction platforms. Utah estimates Kalshi extracted between $30.6 million and $61.2 million in fees from state residents through October 19. The state wants an injunction halting all contracts tracking sports, political races, macro data, and cultural mentions, alongside strict geofencing, full fee restitution, and civil damages.

<h2 id="celestia-deploys-fibre-upgrade-to-mainnet">Celestia Deploys Fibre Upgrade to Mainnet</h2>

Celestia activated its Fibre upgrade on mainnet, marking the network's most significant protocol change since its 2023 rollout. The upgrade scales data availability (DA) capacity to a theoretical 3.07 Tb/s throughput. TIA traded up 59% over the month preceding the launch, topping out at a yearly high of $0.68.

Fibre targets the growing blobspace bottleneck faced by modular rollups, decentralized sequencers, and agentic platforms executing continuous micro-settlements. Celestia uses Data Availability Sampling (DAS), allowing light clients to verify block data integrity by downloading only small cryptographic samples rather than full blocks. Increasing the underlying pipeline capacity lets modular Layer 2s and sovereign rollups offload data storage overhead from monolithic Layer 1 execution environments while preventing fee spikes during periods of high blob contention.
