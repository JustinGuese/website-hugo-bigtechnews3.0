They get stable URLs, require Devin sign-in, and can be shared within an organization.

The architecture is worth noting. Each app runs on a single machine using memory snapshots, sleeping when idle and waking on access. Billing is based on awake time, not wall-clock time. That means an engineer can spin up an internal dashboard that has access to existing MCPs, integrations, and codebases without standing up a whole deployment pipeline.

The catch: this is not for high-traffic production services. A single machine that sleeps when idle is fine for an approval queue or a monitoring dashboard, not for something serving millions of requests. But for internal tooling, it collapses the time from "I need a dashboard" to "here's a URL" down to a single prompt.

[Thinking Machines](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/dYED4IJA1gUMST1fvBWdpw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTYtYWl8ZW5kLXRvLWVuZC00LWJpdC10cmFpbmluZy10aGUtc3BlZWRlcnJvci10cmFkZW9mZnx8aHR0cHM6Ly90aGlua2luZ21hY2hpbmVzLmFpL2Jsb2cvZW5kLXRvLWVuZC00LWJpdC10cmFpbmluZy8_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t) published a deep dive into training language models end-to-end at 4-bit precision on NVIDIA's Grace Blackwell Ultra (GB300). The piece covers three numerical formats: BF16, MXFP8, and NVFP4.

NVFP4 is the headline. It uses 4.5 bits per value and runs up to 6x faster than BF16 - 15 PFLOPS versus 2.5 PFLOPS on GB300. But it introduces significantly more quantization error. The trick is that this tradeoff isn't uniform across the training pipeline. Pretraining forward/backward passes, SFT, and RL each have different priorities when it comes to minimizing error, bias, and memory footprint.

For engineers actually training models, this is the difference between a 6x speedup that breaks your model and a 6x speedup that works. The guidance here is to be surgical about where you apply low precision. Apply it in the wrong stage and you'll watch your eval scores crater; apply it where error is tolerable and you're saving real money on compute.

[Quark](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/R_Tj_mQXJKw5_isltWdIGg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTYtYWl8cXVhcmstYS1mdWxsLXRyYWluaW5nLXN0YWNrLWZvci00MjAwfHxodHRwczovL2VtcGVyby5vcmcvd3JpdGluZy9xdWFyaz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) is a 165M-parameter language model (140M without embeddings) with 128K context, trained end-to-end from raw data through pretraining, long-context training, RL, evaluation, and release. The entire thing cost about $4,200 of compute, roughly 1,300 H100-hours.

The architecture is 16 layers with 12 local layers attending to the last 256 tokens and 4 global layers, plus multi-head gated value residuals. One version, Quark-150M-128K-Zero, learned agentic tasks from RL alone - no SFT, no distillation. All four checkpoints are released under Apache-2.0.

This matters because it validates the whole pipeline before you spend serious money. The team explored every part of the stack - data, architecture, pretraining at scale, context extension, RL, evaluation - on a budget that's less than a single large training run. If you're planning to train something bigger, this is the proof-of-concept playbook.

In experimental science, and in most real-world workflows, the surrounding systems are just too slow.

The author notes that Claude Code + Opus 4.5 in December 2025 made agents feel genuinely useful, but predictions of rapid white-collar unemployment were wrong. The world has seen only 2% GDP per capita growth for nearly 150 years, despite electricity, communication, computers, transportation, and agriculture all going "quasi-infinite." Intelligence is just another curve, and it's hitting the same wall of everything else being slow.

This is a useful corrective for anyone who's been assuming that a model that beats humans on benchmarks will immediately translate to economic output. The model isn't the bottleneck. The rest of the organization is.

[Embla 1](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/Ra4UibDDewRbGPPgViAnEg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTYtYWl8ZW1ibGEtMS1hLXJlYXNvbmluZy1tb2RlbC10aGF0LXJ1bnMtb24teW91ci1waG9uZXx8aHR0cHM6Ly9zZXZyZW4uYWkvYmxvZy9lbWJsYS0xP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) is a reasoning model designed to run on phones - private, offline, and free per request. At 4 bits, it averages 57.4 on BFCL, IFBench, AA-Omniscience, GPQA, and MATH-500, with a 1.77-second end-to-end response on an iPhone 18 Pro.

The architecture is 32 layers of width 2048 with 8 of 96 routed experts, using Sevren Sparse Attention - ReLU² instead of softmax, with roughly 95% zero weights - plus shared K/V caches and an Engram hash table. For engineers, this is the first credible sign that on-device reasoning is viable. If a model of this quality runs offline on a phone, a lot of the privacy and latency arguments for cloud-only AI start to weaken.
