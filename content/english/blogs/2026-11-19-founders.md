The pre-seed market just did something weird: it raised the most money ever while making the fewest deals on record. Carta's Q3 2026 data shows $3.58B flowing into 11,726 SAFEs and notes, on pace to finish the year above $4.48B across 14,600+ instruments. That's the largest quarter by capital and the smallest by deal count simultaneously - a concentration that tells you exactly where the money is going, and who is getting left out.

The numbers behind that split matter more than the headline. Median post-money caps rose across every tier, but the big jump was in large rounds: deals above $2.5M saw caps climb 33% year-over-year, from $30M to $40M. Meanwhile the Western U.S. pulled in 68% of pre-seed dollars, up from 58%, with the Bay Area alone raising $1.7B. If you're a founder outside that geography, the data says you're competing for a shrinking slice.

> Only 29% of seed rounds convert below the cap, meaning most SAFE investors get their minimum ownership.

<h2 id="pre-seed-caps-are-the-price-not-the-ceiling">Pre-Seed Caps Are the Price, Not the Ceiling</h2>

A SAFE defers pricing, but the cap is where most investors actually end up paying. Carta's data shows only 29% of seed rounds convert below the cap - meaning for 71% of SAFE holders, the cap is the effective price, not a ceiling that gets negotiated down. Seed valuations land above the cap often enough that founders should treat the cap as the likely outcome when negotiating.

The 33% jump in caps for larger deals changes the dilution math. A founder raising $2.5M at a $40M cap gives up roughly 6.25% before any discount; at last year's $30M cap that same check cost 8.3%. The trade-off is that the bar for what counts as a "larger" pre-seed round keeps rising, and the AI-driven concentration of capital means non-AI founders face a tougher market with caps that haven't moved as much.
