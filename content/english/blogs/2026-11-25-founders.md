The AI business model just flipped. ICONIQ's data shows consumption-based pricing among AI software companies jumped from 35% to 42% between Q4 2025 and Q2 2026, and Kyle Poyar found 76% of AI-native companies now use variable or hybrid pricing versus 49% of SaaS. Investors aren't just tolerating this - they prefer usage-based revenue growth over traditional ARR. That's a structural change in how AI products get built, metered, and sold, and it's the story underneath most of today's news.

The other big number: customer service AI is buying nothing. Only 4% of service leaders report expected ROI despite a 38% increase in AI spending. Gartner says customers are three times more likely to use third-party tools like ChatGPT than company-built chatbots, and a 2019 field experiment found disclosed chatbots reduced purchase rates by 79.7%. Companies are spending more to make customers angrier.

> Coding agents read more than 150 tokens for every one they wrote, and that reading is what's burning enterprise budgets.

The shift to usage-based pricing looks simple until you ask who pays for a failed run. An operator building an AI research product [lays out the three options](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/ASjLBs3R2dsVEOpDlo5PjQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjUtZm91bmRlcnN8dGhlLXBheS1wZXItam9iLWVjb25vbWljcy1ub2JvZHktd2FudHMtdG8tdGFsay1hYm91dHx8aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuZm9jdXNlZGNoYW9zLmNvL3AvaW5zaWRlLXRoZS1wYXktcGVyLWpvYi1haS1idXNpbmVzcz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20): charge for effort, charge for delivered output, or charge for successful outcome. Each shifts risk differently, and the cost unit rarely matches what customers think they're buying.

Candor bills per synthetic interview while buyers think in studies of 15 to 24 interviews - the unit of sale and the unit of cost diverge. True gross profit per paid job must include retries and free trials alongside model costs and human review. And repeat demand follows a natural cycle, so you can't judge a cohort until that full window closes.

For engineers this changes architecture. Usage-based pricing means you need metering built in from day one, and it shifts cost risk to the vendor when usage grows. The product has to fail cheap and retry smart, because every retry is now a line item on someone's invoice.

A growth firm reanalyzed 280 user tests and found [actual understanding explained only 3% of the variation](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/CGz1EOMRXPaaHHslyySXWg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjUtZm91bmRlcnN8Y29uZnVzaW5nLWFkcy13b3JrLWFuZC10aGF0LXRlbGxzLXlvdS1zb21ldGhpbmctdW5jb21mb3J0YWJsZXx8aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuc3lzdG0uY28vcG9zdC93aHktY29uZnVzaW5nLWFkcy1zdGlsbC13b3JrP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) in wanting to learn more. The practical audit: count sentences about your company versus sentences about the customer's situation. Then show the message to a small group of target customers and ask who they think it's for. If they say "someone like me," you're winning - even if they can't explain what you do.

[Elliot Smith's argument](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/Zjl7zAj_TtoJJ9EcSo5W2Q/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjUtZm91bmRlcnN8c2Fhcy10aGF0LXN1cnZpdmVzLXZpc2liaWxpdHktYmVhdHMtdXRpbGl0eXx8aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuZWxsaW90Y3NtaXRoLmNvbS9zYWFzLXRoYXQtc3Vydml2ZXMvP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) is that software existing only to fill a "software skill gap" is doomed - AI makes it easy for users to build their own tools, putting downward pressure on prices. What survives is software providing visibility: seeing the forest for the trees.

Strava is the example. Its value isn't logging runs; it's comparing your performance to others. Products that aggregate and contextualize data across users create durable competitive advantage. Single-user utilities are commodities.

Menlo Ventures estimates enterprise AI spending is at a [$176.8 billion annual pace by September 2026](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/kSGBuJAQuj04YJC4kp9Ptg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjUtZm91bmRlcnN8eW91cmUtb3ZlcnBheWluZy1mb3ItYWktYmVjYXVzZS1hZ2VudHMtcmVhZC10b28tbXVjaHx8aHR0cHM6Ly9jcmV3YWkuY29tL25ld3NsZXR0ZXIveW91cmUtb3ZlcnBheWluZy1mb3ItYWktaGVyZXMtd2h5P3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ), up from $33 billion in 2025. Most of that bill isn't generation - it's agents rereading context. A Stanford/Michigan study found coding agents read more than 150 tokens for every one they wrote.
