An AI has proved the Aaronson-Ambainis Conjecture, a 2009 open problem in quantum complexity theory, according to [Scott Aaronson's blog](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/Pqt1Tky7Pg3mAoPy1827PQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHxhaS1wcm92ZWQtdGhlLWFhcm9uc29uLWFtYmFpbmlzLWNvbmplY3R1cmUtcXVhbnR1bS1jb21wdXRpbmctanVzdC1nb3QtY2xlYXJlcnx8aHR0cHM6Ly9zY290dGFhcm9uc29uLmJsb2cvP3A9MTAyOTUmdXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t). The conjecture states that every degree-d polynomial with constant variance has a variable with influence at least 1/poly(d). The consequence: no superpolynomial quantum advantage over classical query complexity for decision problems on most Boolean inputs. This does not kill quantum supremacy - it eliminates a theoretical possibility that most experts already doubted.

The proof comes with Lean certificates, which is why the community is treating it as credible despite human validation lagging. For engineers, the practical takeaway is about expectations: when you hear about quantum speedups, this result narrows where they can appear. It rules out a broad class of decision problems where quantum was theoretically promising. That is useful information for anyone planning research around quantum algorithms or budgeting for quantum hardware. The rate of AI-produced proofs is now high enough that open problems in math are falling regularly, and the bottleneck is human verification, not generation.

LLMs are bad at designing software systems that use LLMs, and [Sean Goedecke's analysis](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/g0TUy3q-RJg1A54jrTZNrQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHxsbG1zLWFyZS10ZXJyaWJsZS1hdC1kZXNpZ25pbmctYWktc3lzdGVtcy1oZXJlcy10aGUtcHJvb2Z8fGh0dHBzOi8vd3d3LnNlYW5nb2VkZWNrZS5jb20vbGxtcy1hcmUtdGVycmlibGUtYXQtZGVzaWduaW5nLWFpLXN5c3RlbXMvP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) gives a concrete example: an AI agent tasked with building a memory contradiction checker built a symbolic logic system instead of using LLM calls, despite knowing the symbolic approach was impossible. The likely reasons: most training data is pre-LLM code, and RLVR environments don't reward calling LLMs.

This is a practical limitation you will hit if you let agents design systems end-to-end. The fix is human oversight at the architecture level - let the LLM write the code, but make the design decisions yourself. Agents default to the patterns in their training data, and those patterns predate the tools they are supposed to use.

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/9PnSW5D8II0152D2QpBBww/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHxwcm9ncmFtbWluZy1pcy1tb3JlLXRpcmluZy1ub3ctdGhhdHMtdGhlLXBvaW50fHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5uYXRlbWV5dmlzLmNvbS9wcm9ncmFtbWluZy1hbmQtZmF0aWd1ZS8_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: natemeyvis.com ↗</a></p>

[Nate Meyvis argues](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/9PnSW5D8II0152D2QpBBww/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHxwcm9ncmFtbWluZy1pcy1tb3JlLXRpcmluZy1ub3ctdGhhdHMtdGhlLXBvaW50fHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5uYXRlbWV5dmlzLmNvbS9wcm9ncmFtbWluZy1hbmQtZmF0aWd1ZS8_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t) that programming is more tiring with AI because AI handles the repetitive parts, leaving humans with higher-judgment tasks. He compares it to online poker, where juggling many threads is normal, and argues the fatigue is healthy, not burnout. He admits this is optimistic and conjectural.

The counterpoint: if the fatigue is from constant context-switching across many AI threads rather than deep work, it is not healthy - it is cognitive overload. The distinction matters for how you structure your workflow. If you are tired because you are making real decisions, that is value. If you are tired because you are babysitting agents, that is a tooling problem.

<h2 id="signal-collapse-is-everywhere-all-at-once"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/P5RHkBTakV3w2EzGnTFDxA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHxzaWduYWwtY29sbGFwc2UtaXMtZXZlcnl3aGVyZS1hbGwtYXQtb25jZXx8aHR0cHM6Ly90aGVsYXN0c29mdHdhcmVlbmdpbmVlci5zdWJzdGFjay5jb20vcC9haS1pcy1jYXVzaW5nLXNpZ25hbC1jb2xsYXBzZS1ldmVyeXdoZXJlP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Signal collapse is everywhere, all at once</a></h2>

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/P5RHkBTakV3w2EzGnTFDxA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHxzaWduYWwtY29sbGFwc2UtaXMtZXZlcnl3aGVyZS1hbGwtYXQtb25jZXx8aHR0cHM6Ly90aGVsYXN0c29mdHdhcmVlbmdpbmVlci5zdWJzdGFjay5jb20vcC9haS1pcy1jYXVzaW5nLXNpZ25hbC1jb2xsYXBzZS1ldmVyeXdoZXJlP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: thelastsoftwareengineer.substack.com ↗</a></p>

AI can now cheaply fake almost every signal used to judge quality - GitHub stars, grades, peer review - causing "signal collapse" across open source, education, and research, per [The Last Software Engineer](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/P5RHkBTakV3w2EzGnTFDxA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHxzaWduYWwtY29sbGFwc2UtaXMtZXZlcnl3aGVyZS1hbGwtYXQtb25jZXx8aHR0cHM6Ly90aGVsYXN0c29mdHdhcmVlbmdpbmVlci5zdWJzdGFjay5jb20vcC9haS1pcy1jYXVzaW5nLXNpZ25hbC1jb2xsYXBzZS1ldmVyeXdoZXJlP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ). The author prefers "signal collapse" over "signal erosion" because the change is fast and widespread. The result is a "market for lemons" where good work is devalued because buyers cannot distinguish it from fakes.

For engineers, this hits hiring and open source directly. GitHub stars used to be a rough proxy for quality; now they are a metric that can be gamed with generated PRs and issues. The fix is not obvious - it likely involves verified contribution history, cryptographic attestation, or human review networks. Until then, trust signals degrade.

<h2 id="the-jevons-paradox-has-a-second-half-cheaper-software-means-more-software"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/769o-uVVe29FOJ4Du1se9g/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHx0aGUtamV2b25zLXBhcmFkb3gtaGFzLWEtc2Vjb25kLWhhbGYtY2hlYXBlci1zb2Z0d2FyZS1tZWFucy1tb3JlLXNvZnR3YXJlfHxodHRwczovL2hyaWguc2kvYXJ0aWNsZXMvdGhlLWpldm9ucy1wYXJhZG94LWhhcy1hLXNlY29uZC1oYWxmLz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">The Jevons paradox has a second half: cheaper software means more software</a></h2>

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/769o-uVVe29FOJ4Du1se9g/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHx0aGUtamV2b25zLXBhcmFkb3gtaGFzLWEtc2Vjb25kLWhhbGYtY2hlYXBlci1zb2Z0d2FyZS1tZWFucy1tb3JlLXNvZnR3YXJlfHxodHRwczovL2hyaWguc2kvYXJ0aWNsZXMvdGhlLWpldm9ucy1wYXJhZG94LWhhcy1hLXNlY29uZC1oYWxmLz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: hrih.si ↗</a></p>

The author of [this piece](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/769o-uVVe29FOJ4Du1se9g/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHx0aGUtamV2b25zLXBhcmFkb3gtaGFzLWEtc2Vjb25kLWhhbGYtY2hlYXBlci1zb2Z0d2FyZS1tZWFucy1tb3JlLXNvZnR3YXJlfHxodHRwczovL2hyaWguc2kvYXJ0aWNsZXMvdGhlLWpldm9ucy1wYXJhZG94LWhhcy1hLXNlY29uZC1oYWxmLz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) built 22 programs in three weeks with an AI agent, including a tax calculator for three countries and a child support calculator - things that would have been too costly at 2020 prices. This is the Jevons paradox: cheaper software leads to more software being built, not fewer jobs. The article traces the paradox from 1865 coal use to modern software, arguing AI will increase demand for software.

The developer jobs that grow will be the ones machines made faster. The demand for software can be bottomless and the work can still move somewhere else. If you are a developer, the question is not whether AI takes your job - it is whether you are building the things that become cheaper to make, or the things that become more valuable because they are cheaper to make.

<h2 id="ai-broke-cybersecurity-alex-stamos-says-were-in-a-dark-age"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/Cxp9MXbYxwn4Cs_Oubm71w/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHxhaS1icm9rZS1jeWJlcnNlY3VyaXR5LWFsZXgtc3RhbW9zLXNheXMtd2VyZS1pbi1hLWRhcmstYWdlfHxodHRwczovL3d3dy50aGV2ZXJnZS5jb20vcG9kY2FzdC8xMDI5MDM4L2FpLWN5YmVyc2VjdXJpdHktaGFja2Vycy1kYXRhLWJyZWFjaC1kYXJrLWFnZS1hbGV4LXN0YW1vcz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">AI broke cybersecurity. Alex Stamos says we&#x27;re in a dark age</a></h2>

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/Cxp9MXbYxwn4Cs_Oubm71w/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHxhaS1icm9rZS1jeWJlcnNlY3VyaXR5LWFsZXgtc3RhbW9zLXNheXMtd2VyZS1pbi1hLWRhcmstYWdlfHxodHRwczovL3d3dy50aGV2ZXJnZS5jb20vcG9kY2FzdC8xMDI5MDM4L2FpLWN5YmVyc2VjdXJpdHktaGFja2Vycy1kYXRhLWJyZWFjaC1kYXJrLWFnZS1hbGV4LXN0YW1vcz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: theverge.com ↗</a></p>

Alex Stamos, CSO at Cognition, says AI has broken the cybersecurity world, with AI-boosted attacks from private and state actors exploiting vulnerabilities at scale, per [The Verge](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/Cxp9MXbYxwn4Cs_Oubm71w/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHxhaS1icm9rZS1jeWJlcnNlY3VyaXR5LWFsZXgtc3RhbW9zLXNheXMtd2VyZS1pbi1hLWRhcmstYWdlfHxodHRwczovL3d3dy50aGV2ZXJnZS5jb20vcG9kY2FzdC8xMDI5MDM4L2FpLWN5YmVyc2VjdXJpdHktaGFja2Vycy1kYXRhLWJyZWFjaC1kYXJrLWFnZS1hbGV4LXN0YW1vcz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20). He describes a "dark age" as the industry adapts, comparing it to the frontier feel of the 1990s internet. The multibillion-dollar cybersecurity industry is "overdue" for disruption.

AI accelerates both attack and defense. Attackers get automated vulnerability discovery and exploit generation; defenders get automated detection and response. The asymmetry is that attackers only need one hole, while defenders must close them all. Expect breach rates to rise before the defense catches up.

<h2 id="the-great-software-ip-writedown-is-real"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/ugLiNtanFEvuMBqJ0D5EEA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHx0aGUtZ3JlYXQtc29mdHdhcmUtaXAtd3JpdGVkb3duLWlzLXJlYWx8fGh0dHBzOi8vbWFydGluYWxkZXJzb24uY29tL3Bvc3RzL3RoZS1ncmVhdC1zb2Z0d2FyZS1pcC13cml0ZWRvd24vP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">The great software IP writedown is real</a></h2>

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/ugLiNtanFEvuMBqJ0D5EEA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHx0aGUtZ3JlYXQtc29mdHdhcmUtaXAtd3JpdGVkb3duLWlzLXJlYWx8fGh0dHBzOi8vbWFydGluYWxkZXJzb24uY29tL3Bvc3RzL3RoZS1ncmVhdC1zb2Z0d2FyZS1pcC13cml0ZWRvd24vP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: martinalderson.com ↗</a></p>

[Martin Alderson argues](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/ugLiNtanFEvuMBqJ0D5EEA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHx0aGUtZ3JlYXQtc29mdHdhcmUtaXAtd3JpdGVkb3duLWlzLXJlYWx8fGh0dHBzOi8vbWFydGluYWxkZXJzb24uY29tL3Bvc3RzL3RoZS1ncmVhdC1zb2Z0d2FyZS1pcC13cml0ZWRvd24vP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) that software's high profit margins were driven by intellectual scarcity - a shortage of people able to build software - not monopolies or network effects. With AI making software cheaper to build, that scarcity is ending. IP is worth roughly what it costs to rebuild, which is not very much anymore.

For engineers, this is the uncomfortable version of the Jevons argument: software value will decline even as volume increases. The work that survives is the work machines cannot do - judgment, architecture, and domain knowledge.

<h2 id="fighting-the-ai-brain-fry-with-a-git-based-priority-queue"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/v23FbTZFI3Qgzb9zYUKWrw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHxmaWdodGluZy10aGUtYWktYnJhaW4tZnJ5LXdpdGgtYS1naXQtYmFzZWQtcHJpb3JpdHktcXVldWV8fGh0dHBzOi8vc2VyZ2V5a3VobmUuY29tL2Jsb2cvZmlnaHRpbmctYWktYnJhaW4tZnJ5P3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Fighting the AI Brain Fry with a git-based priority queue</a></h2>

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/v23FbTZFI3Qgzb9zYUKWrw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHxmaWdodGluZy10aGUtYWktYnJhaW4tZnJ5LXdpdGgtYS1naXQtYmFzZWQtcHJpb3JpdHktcXVldWV8fGh0dHBzOi8vc2VyZ2V5a3VobmUuY29tL2Jsb2cvZmlnaHRpbmctYWktYnJhaW4tZnJ5P3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: sergeykuhne.com ↗</a></p>

[Sergey Kuhne describes](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/v23FbTZFI3Qgzb9zYUKWrw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHxmaWdodGluZy10aGUtYWktYnJhaW4tZnJ5LXdpdGgtYS1naXQtYmFzZWQtcHJpb3JpdHktcXVldWV8fGh0dHBzOi8vc2VyZ2V5a3VobmUuY29tL2Jsb2cvZmlnaHRpbmctYWktYnJhaW4tZnJ5P3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) a system to manage the cognitive load of many AI coding threads: a git-based priority queue where tickets are markdown files. An agent cycles through the board, building safe tickets, running tests, and verifying deploys, while human decisions land in an "owner inbox" with a Claude artifact viewer. The key is closing each item after a decision, freeing mental context.

This is a practical pattern worth stealing. The conventional way of working with AI has sequencing and context issues; organizing work like a priority queue gives you a single source of truth and a way to close loops.

<h2 id="working-with-designers-one-is-a-blessing-multiple-is-a-curse"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/Q9chxnbFxHCDTnQn1zqtVg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHx3b3JraW5nLXdpdGgtZGVzaWduZXJzLW9uZS1pcy1hLWJsZXNzaW5nLW11bHRpcGxlLWlzLWEtY3Vyc2V8fGh0dHBzOi8vd3d3LnNlYW5nb2VkZWNrZS5jb20vd29ya2luZy13aXRoLWRlc2lnbmVycy8_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Working with designers: one is a blessing, multiple is a curse</a></h2>

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/Q9chxnbFxHCDTnQn1zqtVg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHx3b3JraW5nLXdpdGgtZGVzaWduZXJzLW9uZS1pcy1hLWJsZXNzaW5nLW11bHRpcGxlLWlzLWEtY3Vyc2V8fGh0dHBzOi8vd3d3LnNlYW5nb2VkZWNrZS5jb20vd29ya2luZy13aXRoLWRlc2lnbmVycy8_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: seangoedecke.com ↗</a></p>

[Sean Goedecke](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/Q9chxnbFxHCDTnQn1zqtVg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHx3b3JraW5nLXdpdGgtZGVzaWduZXJzLW9uZS1pcy1hLWJsZXNzaW5nLW11bHRpcGxlLWlzLWEtY3Vyc2V8fGh0dHBzOi8vd3d3LnNlYW5nb2VkZWNrZS5jb20vd29ya2luZy13aXRoLWRlc2lnbmVycy8_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t) loves working with designers but warns that having more than one on a team causes conflict. Designers are like "Domovoy" spirits - one protects, multiple fight. His worst experience was with three designers on one product.

The engineering takeaway: design is a bottleneck that gets worse with more cooks. Structure your team so one designer owns the product vision, and route everyone else through them.

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/tC1SlGim5pzSsjJ2JfTa8A/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHx0aGUtam9icy1udW1iZXJzLXNheS1haS1oYXNudC1lYXRlbi13aGl0ZS1jb2xsYXItd29yay15ZXR8fGh0dHBzOi8vd3d3Lndzai5jb20vZWNvbm9teS9qb2JzL2lzLWFpLXRha2luZy13aGl0ZS1jb2xsYXItam9icy13aGF0LXRoZS1sYXRlc3QtbnVtYmVycy10ZWxsLXVzLWRkMzFiMTE1P3N0PVVZSkJyZiZhbXA7cmVmbGluaz1kZXNrdG9wd2Vic2hhcmVfcGVybWFsaW5rJmFtcDt1dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: wsj.com ↗</a></p>

U.S. professional and business services added 70,000 jobs in October, the largest monthly increase since July 2022, per [the Wall Street Journal](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/QwBvMbH8xaKqdsEe3Xr6Iw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDktdGVjaHx0aGUtam9icy1udW1iZXJzLXNheS1haS1oYXNudC1lYXRlbi13aGl0ZS1jb2xsYXItd29yay15ZXR8fGh0dHBzOi8vd3d3Lndzai5jb20vZWNvbm9teS9qb2JzL2lzLWFpLXRha2luZy13aGl0ZS1jb2xsYXItam9icy13aGF0LXRoZS1sYXRlc3QtbnVtYmVycy10ZWxsLXVzLWRkMzFiMTE1P3N0PVVZSkJyZiZyZWZsaW5rPWRlc2t0b3B3ZWJzaGFyZV9wZXJtYWxpbmsmdXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t). That is not a collapse - it is growth. The AI job displacement story is not showing up in the aggregate data yet, either because the displacement is concentrated in specific roles or because the Jevons effect is generating enough new work to offset it. Either way, the numbers say the white-collar apocalypse is not here.
