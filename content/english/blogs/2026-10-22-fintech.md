Ramp just made its bid to escape the partner-bank sandbox. Fresh off a $1.85 billion round at a $60 billion valuation, the corporate spend platform submitted an application to the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) for a national trust bank charter. If approved, the charter gives Ramp direct federal oversight to manage fiduciary investments and custody digital assets without cobbling together state-by-state trust licenses or renting charter rails from sponsor institutions. 

The regulatory move mirrors a broader operational pivot across fintech: companies are building hard infrastructure to cut out middle-tier intermediaries. That push spans card acquiring, corporate treasury consolidation, and automated protocol rails designed to let LLMs transact directly with payment networks. 

While the legal battle over non-depository fintech charters heats up in Washington, the payment networks are racing to deploy the authentication plumbing for autonomous agents. Here is the operational breakdown of what shipped across fintech today.

Ramp's [OCC application](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/_l5lDMd0Eh20NcJJiLfUrg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMjItZmludGVjaHxyYW1wLWdvZXMtYWZ0ZXItYW4tb2NjLXRydXN0LWNoYXJ0ZXItYW5kLWFjcXVpcmVzLXZlc3RvfHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5iYW5raW5nZGl2ZS5jb20vbmV3cy9yYW1wLWFwcGxpZXMtbmF0aW9uYWwtdHJ1c3QtYmFuay1jaGFydGVyLW9jYy1maW50ZWNoLWljYmEvODMzNDM0Lz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) seeks a limited-purpose national trust bank charter. Under this structure, Ramp will not take retail or commercial deposits and will not originate commercial loans. That distinction matters: it avoids FDIC deposit insurance mandates and standard Bank Holding Company Act restrictions while granting fiduciary investment powers and digital asset custody under uniform federal supervision. 

Traditional banking trade groups like the ICBA have historically opposed the OCC granting national trust charters to fintech and crypto firms, arguing it allows tech platforms to bypass standard prudential safeguards. For Ramp, the upside is architectural: holding assets directly under a federal framework reduces reliance on third-party trust companies, removes 50-state regulatory fragmentation, and lets it custody yield-bearing instruments directly for its corporate clients.

Ramp immediately paired the regulatory filing with the [acquisition of Vesto](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/iIVt0vpUQUTGmM2Bh70Tsg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMjItZmludGVjaHxyYW1wLWdvZXMtYWZ0ZXItYW4tb2NjLXRydXN0LWNoYXJ0ZXItYW5kLWFjcXVpcmVzLXZlc3RvfHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5maW50ZWNoZnV0dXJlcy5jb20vbS1hL3JhbXAtYWNxdWlyZXMtdmVzdG8_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t). Vesto provides an aggregation layer connecting accounts across more than 12,000 financial institutions, providing real-time cash position monitoring and liquidity forecasting. Instead of operating as a pure card-issuing and expense-management tool, Ramp is integrating Vesto's multi-bank data feed directly into its native checking and investment products, turning the software into an automated corporate treasury management system.

Card networks are rapidly commoditizing autonomous machine-to-machine checkout. [Mastercard opened early access to Agent Pay for Machines](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/m7NlhsWqTX__bZU8SNY6Lg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMjItZmludGVjaHxuZXR3b3Jrcy1zaGlwLWFnZW50aWMtcGF5bWVudHMtbWFzdGVyY2FyZC1hZ2VudC1wYXktYW5kLXZpc2FzLXRydXN0ZWQtYWdlbnQtcHJvdG9jb2x8fGh0dHBzOi8vbGlua3MudGxkcm5ld3NsZXR0ZXIuY29tL09zdXFUcj91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20), a suite of APIs, software development kits, and sandbox environments designed to let autonomous agents execute payments within deterministic budget constraints. In pilot testing with Coinbase, Base, Cloudflare, BVNK, and Ant International, an AI agent completed nine autonomous transactions using Coinbase’s x402 HTTP payment protocol with onchain settlement on Base. Mastercard and BVNK acted as the payment orchestration layer, bridging fiat rails and stablecoin liquidity to clear transactions without requiring human intervention.

Concurrently, [Visa, BILL, and Billtrust launched a live pilot](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/N_--0XTCVcBEXdWSun353Q/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMjItZmludGVjaHxuZXR3b3Jrcy1zaGlwLWFnZW50aWMtcGF5bWVudHMtbWFzdGVyY2FyZC1hZ2VudC1wYXktYW5kLXZpc2FzLXRydXN0ZWQtYWdlbnQtcHJvdG9jb2x8fGh0dHBzOi8vaW52ZXN0b3IudmlzYS5jb20vbmV3cy9uZXdzLWRldGFpbHMvMjAyNi9WaXNhLUJJTEwtYW5kLUJpbGx0cnVzdC1MYXVuY2gtRmlyc3QtTGl2ZS1QaWxvdC1mb3ItVHJ1c3RlZC1BSS1BZ2VudC1QYXltZW50cy1CZXR3ZWVuLUJ1eWVyLWFuZC1TdXBwbGllci1QbGF0Zm9ybXMvZGVmYXVsdC5hc3B4P3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) targeting accounts payable operations. Today, AP automation often stalls at supplier portals, requiring accounts payable clerks to manually solve CAPTCHAs and key in commercial card numbers. Visa's approach relies on its Trusted Agent Protocol and tokenized credentials. The pilot allows buyer-authorized agents to authenticate directly against supplier portals, bypass CAPTCHA gating via cryptographic attestation, and deliver single-use tokenized card numbers for already-approved invoices.

The consumer side of autonomous checkout is also hitting production. Duet hooks into roughly 6,000 marketplace integrations, checking connected services asynchronously while users are offline. For procurement, it routes transactions through Shopify Shop Pay and Stripe Link. Stripe Link issues dynamically generated virtual cards to obscure underlying account numbers, though Anthropic still enforces a human-in-the-loop confirmation modal before the payment clears.

The industry consensus on how to authenticate these operations is hardening around authorization mandates. As [Vladyslav Kolodistyi noted on passkey implementations](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/1lMaZw1pBXwUBPksMDc-lQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMjItZmludGVjaHxuZXR3b3Jrcy1zaGlwLWFnZW50aWMtcGF5bWVudHMtbWFzdGVyY2FyZC1hZ2VudC1wYXktYW5kLXZpc2FzLXRydXN0ZWQtYWdlbnQtcHJvdG9jb2x8fGh0dHBzOi8vbGlua3MudGxkcm5ld3NsZXR0ZXIuY29tL2pIeHcyWj91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20), authenticating an agentic purchase cannot happen at the point of sale. Instead, authentication shifts to the issuance of a cryptographically signed mandate (via FIDO2/WebAuthn passkeys) where the user sets strict merchant, spend, and TTL boundaries for the agent's delegation token. Adoption, however, remains cautious: [Money20/20 panel data](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/Qg_-i3cqAq1DPP23vjHFig/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMjItZmludGVjaHxuZXR3b3Jrcy1zaGlwLWFnZW50aWMtcGF5bWVudHMtbWFzdGVyY2FyZC1hZ2VudC1wYXktYW5kLXZpc2FzLXRydXN0ZWQtYWdlbnQtcHJvdG9jb2x8fGh0dHBzOi8vZmluYWluZXdzLmNvbS9wYXltZW50cy8yMy1vZi11cy1jb25zdW1lcnMtY29tZm9ydGFibGUtd2l0aC1hZ2VudGljLXB1cmNoYXNlcy8_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t) shows only 23% of US consumers currently feel comfortable letting AI agents manage payment checkouts autonomously.
