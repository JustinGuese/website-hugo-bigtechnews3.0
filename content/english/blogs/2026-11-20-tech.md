[PostgreSQL 19](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/PpngGNOHTI5kHqTvaijZRQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjAtdGVjaHxwb3N0Z3Jlc3FsLTE5LXJlcGFjay1jb25jdXJyZW50bHkta2lsbHMtdmFjdXVtLWZ1bGx8fGh0dHBzOi8vd3d3LnBvc3RncmVzcWwub3JnL2Fib3V0L25ld3MvcG9zdGdyZXNxbC0xOS1yZWxlYXNlZC0zNDQ5Lz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) is out, and the headline feature is `REPACK (CONCURRENTLY)`. It reclaims table bloat and reorders data without blocking reads or writes, replacing the dreaded `VACUUM FULL` and `CLUSTER` commands that locked tables and forced maintenance windows. For anyone running multi-terabyte databases in production, this removes a major operational constraint.

The release also adds the `pg_plan_advice` extension for stabilizing query plans, plus support for "read-your-writes" consistency on standbys and "get or create" behavior. The read-your-writes feature is significant for applications that route reads to replicas: it eliminates the race condition where a user writes data and immediately reads stale state from a standby. This is the kind of release that doesn't make flashy headlines but saves someone a 2 a.m. incident call.

Engineers should read this as what it is: a technology demonstration. Watch this for the networking and thermal challenges, not as a migration target.

The [AI forecasting agenda](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/da7MmsKlQkzXPLS9rrlzrA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjAtdGVjaHxhaS1mb3JlY2FzdGVycy1iZWF0LWh1bWFucy0tLWJ1dC1jYW50LWRvLWNvbmRpdGlvbmFsLXF1ZXN0aW9uc3x8aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuYXN0cmFsY29kZXh0ZW4uY29tL3AvdGhlLWFpLWZvcmVjYXN0aW5nLWFnZW5kYT91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) has a clear result: specialized models from startups like FutureSearch and Mantic now match or beat top human performance on traditional forecasting questions. Foundation models like ChatGPT and Claude trail by about six months. This could replace expensive human superforecaster teams and prediction markets.

The unsolved problem is conditional forecasting - "if X happens, what's the impact on Y?" - which is more relevant to policy outcomes but harder to implement. That's the gap between a model that predicts election results and one that advises on what happens if a specific regulation passes. Until conditional forecasting works, human judgment still has a seat at the table.

<h2 id="the-epoch-capabilities-index-one-number-to-track-ai-progress"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/vFu94VRtWFO5Su2cPtOVAA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjAtdGVjaHx0aGUtZXBvY2gtY2FwYWJpbGl0aWVzLWluZGV4LW9uZS1udW1iZXItdG8tdHJhY2stYWktcHJvZ3Jlc3N8fGh0dHBzOi8vYXN0ZXJpc2ttYWcuc3Vic3RhY2suY29tL3AvdGhlLW1vc3QtaW1wb3J0YW50LW51bWJlci1pbi1haT91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">The Epoch Capabilities Index: One Number to Track AI Progress</a></h2>

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/vFu94VRtWFO5Su2cPtOVAA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjAtdGVjaHx0aGUtZXBvY2gtY2FwYWJpbGl0aWVzLWluZGV4LW9uZS1udW1iZXItdG8tdHJhY2stYWktcHJvZ3Jlc3N8fGh0dHBzOi8vYXN0ZXJpc2ttYWcuc3Vic3RhY2suY29tL3AvdGhlLW1vc3QtaW1wb3J0YW50LW51bWJlci1pbi1haT91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: asteriskmag.substack.com ↗</a></p>

The [Epoch Capabilities Index (ECI)](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/vFu94VRtWFO5Su2cPtOVAA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjAtdGVjaHx0aGUtZXBvY2gtY2FwYWJpbGl0aWVzLWluZGV4LW9uZS1udW1iZXItdG8tdHJhY2stYWktcHJvZ3Jlc3N8fGh0dHBzOi8vYXN0ZXJpc2ttYWcuc3Vic3RhY2suY29tL3AvdGhlLW1vc3QtaW1wb3J0YW50LW51bWJlci1pbi1haT91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) from Epoch AI is a single number summarizing AI model performance across benchmarks, built using item response theory - the same methodology as IQ tests. It's designed to be continuously updatable as benchmarks saturate or new ones are introduced, allowing trends to be tracked over years.

The honest caveat: the assumption of a single underlying capability is "not literally true but empirically close." For engineers tracking model progress, the ECI is becoming a standard reference point, but it's a summary statistic, not a specification. Use it to spot trends, not to pick a model for production.

<h2 id="the-gig-economys-dark-side-180hour-for-doctors-750hour-for-tagalog"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/7MocVs9u2TvvkpwSVMi2AQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjAtdGVjaHx0aGUtZ2lnLWVjb25vbXlzLWRhcmstc2lkZS0xODBob3VyLWZvci1kb2N0b3JzLTc1MGhvdXItZm9yLXRhZ2Fsb2d8fGh0dHBzOi8vcmVzdG9md29ybGQub3JnLzIwMjYvYWktam9icy1za2lsbHMtZ2lnLXdvcmsvP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">The Gig Economy&#x27;s Dark Side: $180/Hour for Doctors, $7.50/Hour for Tagalog</a></h2>

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/7MocVs9u2TvvkpwSVMi2AQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjAtdGVjaHx0aGUtZ2lnLWVjb25vbXlzLWRhcmstc2lkZS0xODBob3VyLWZvci1kb2N0b3JzLTc1MGhvdXItZm9yLXRhZ2Fsb2d8fGh0dHBzOi8vcmVzdG9md29ybGQub3JnLzIwMjYvYWktam9icy1za2lsbHMtZ2lnLXdvcmsvP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: restofworld.org ↗</a></p>

The [AI data work economy](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/7MocVs9u2TvvkpwSVMi2AQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjAtdGVjaHx0aGUtZ2lnLWVjb25vbXlzLWRhcmstc2lkZS0xODBob3VyLWZvci1kb2N0b3JzLTc1MGhvdXItZm9yLXRhZ2Fsb2d8fGh0dHBzOi8vcmVzdG9md29ybGQub3JnLzIwMjYvYWktam9icy1za2lsbHMtZ2lnLXdvcmsvP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) is stratified in ways that should give every engineer pause. Platforms like Scale AI's Outlier pay up to $180/hour for U.S. physicians but as low as $7.50/hour for Tagalog work in the Philippines. The pattern is "extractivism": pay well to build a workforce, then cut rates or shut down operations - as seen with Remotasks closing in Kenya, Nigeria, and Pakistan in 2024.

This is the labor that trains the models we deploy. The quality of your training data is directly tied to the conditions of the people producing it. When rates get cut, quality follows, and so does model performance. It's worth asking who's annotating your eval sets.
