The [kernel.dev analysis](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/WIovGBt4VOC2r73b83gipw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTktYWl8Z2VtaW5pLTQtY2FyYm9uLXNsb3dlci1iZWNhdXNlLWl0LWRvZXMtbW9yZS1ub3QtYmVjYXVzZS1pdHMtYnJva2VufHxodHRwczovL2tlcm5lbC5kZXYvYmxvZy93aHktaXMtZ2VtaW5pLTQtY2FyYm9uLXRha2luZy1zby1sb25nLz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) of Gemini 4 Carbon is the most useful engineering read of the day. On a real coding task, Carbon took 22m21s where Opus 5.5 took 9m33s. The slowness splits into slower inference (16-30% extra time vs Astra, 45-49% vs Opus) and dramatically longer trajectories: Carbon takes about seven times as many steps as Astra and eleven times as many actions, processing more than sixteen times as much input.

The authors ruled out harness and reasoning-level mismatches. The extra time comes from Carbon doing significantly more work per task - more steps, more actions, more tokens consumed. That's a different failure mode than "slow inference." It means Carbon is exploring more of the solution space, and if your tasks are well-scoped, that exploration is wasted. If your tasks are genuinely hard and underspecified, the extra steps might be exactly what you need. Know which you're running before you pick the model.

Edgee's Agent Gateway treats routing as both a tier map (which model per difficulty) and a switching policy, with Self-Learning Routing replaying past sessions to recommend the cheapest capable combination. If you're building a multi-model agent, this is the cost model you're missing.

[This investigation](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/y6dejj40zDSvGYCXyBhZ8Q/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTktYWl8YW50aWdyYXZpdHktZWF0cy1xdW90YS1iZWNhdXNlLWl0cy1jYWNoZS1kaWVzLWluLTE1LXNlY29uZHN8fGh0dHBzOi8vYXN1YmVkaS5jb20ubnAvYmxvZy93aHktYW50aWdyYXZpdHktZWF0cy13ZWVrbHktcXVvdGEtZmFzdC8_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t) into why Google Antigravity burns through weekly quota is a masterclass in binary-searching a black box. The author disassembled the 193 MB Go binary (v2.33.0), discovered a dormant keep-alive engine, and narrowed the cache eviction cliff down to a 15-second window.

The mechanics: Gemini 4 models rely on implicit caching, where cached tokens get a 90% discount but cache misses pay 100% on the entire prefix. Even a 5-word prompt starts with 13,800 to 16,800+ tokens of system instructions, MCP schemas, and skills. If your code-review pause exceeds the TTL, every turn is a full-price cache miss. The workaround: refresh the cache just before expiry, but that only works for 3 pings. And disabling unused MCP servers and plugins shrinks the cold-turn penalty. For anyone running Antigravity or similar implicit-cache agents, this is the difference between a 10% discount and a 100% bill.
