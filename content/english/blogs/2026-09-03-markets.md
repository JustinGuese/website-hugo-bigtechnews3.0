The story that matters today is not any single product launch - it is that AI agents are now visibly re-sorting enterprise software spend. Meta told its people to move off Google Chat and onto Slack, and the stated reason was agent suitability. Atlassian told its customers that from December 3, agents get metered like any other infrastructure. And Anthropic signed another tranche of compute deals that will all, eventually, be someone's GPU order. The rest of the day - a Defender false-positive investigation, a Chrome release-cycle change, Okta's third week of agent-identity features - is noise by comparison.

> When a customer as large as Meta says it is switching chat platforms *because of AI agents*, the collaboration suite has quietly become an AI distribution channel - and the incumbents that treated chat as a solved product are the ones being re-priced.

<h2 id="collaboration-suites-are-being-re-priced-as-agent-surfaces">Collaboration suites are being re-priced as agent surfaces</h2>

Meta migrating internal communications from Google Chat to Slack, citing AI agent suitability, is the kind of customer win you almost never see announced. Companies this size do not casually rip out a Google Workspace component; doing it *for agent reasons* says the evaluation criterion for workplace software has changed. The direct beneficiary is Salesforce, which owns Slack; the direct loser is Alphabet, though in revenue terms the loss is rounding error against Workspace's scale.

The second-order read is worse for Alphabet than the first: if Meta - a sophisticated AI buyer - concluded Slack is the better substrate for agents, other large enterprises running the same evaluation will reach the same conclusion. Slack is becoming the desktop where agents are rendered, and everything that plugs into it gains distribution. That is exactly what Adobe is betting with the Adobe for Slack MCP app: Firefly, Photoshop, Premiere, Acrobat, and 70+ tools now accessible inside Slack Business+ and Enterprise+. Adobe is not selling more seats; it is putting its software where the agents already are. We have flagged Adobe's Slack integrations before, and this is a continuation, not a new fact - but the direction is consistent and compounding.

<h2 id="atlassian-formalizes-what-we-have-been-saying-for-weeks">Atlassian formalizes what we have been saying for weeks</h2>

We have been tracking Atlassian's move toward metering AI like infrastructure for three weeks now. Today's news makes it concrete: effective December 3, 2026, new usage-based meters for Rovo credits, automation steps, and outcome-based AI agent resolutions. The outcome-based meter is the interesting one - charging for *resolutions* rather than seats or tokens is the pricing model everyone in software says they want and almost no one has shipped. Atlassian's installed base of developer and IT teams gives it the usage density to make this real revenue rather than a press release. This is a building theme, not a stale one.

<h2 id="the-compute-ledger-keeps-growing">The compute ledger keeps growing</h2>

Anthropic hired former OpenAI Stargate exec Shamez Hemani and is in talks with Meta to lease compute, on top of signed commitments of $35B with Lambda, $45B with Nscale, and $19B with TeraWulf. None of these counterparties trade on our list, but the flow-through is unambiguous: roughly $99B of committed AI infrastructure spend eventually clears through GPU vendors and the memory attached to them. The Meta lease talks are the more interesting wrinkle - Meta as a compute landlord to a rival model lab confirms that its capex is being monetized, not just consumed. We have run positive on the silicon complex all week and nothing today argues for a change.

<h2 id="alphabet-defends-the-model-layer-with-price">Alphabet defends the model layer with price</h2>

Losing Meta's chat business is trivial. What is not trivial is Gemini 3.8 Flash at $0.75/M input and $3.75/M output through year-end, doubling afterward. That is land-grab pricing aimed squarely at every rival's unit economics, and it pairs with the two-week Chrome stable release cycle from September 2026 - Alphabet is compressing iteration speed across both its distribution and its model layer. We stay positive; the Flash pricing is defensive but effective, and the enterprise safeguards news from Anthropic (built with AWS, Google Cloud, *and* Azure) keeps Google Cloud embedded in the enterprise AI stack regardless of which model wins.

<h2 id="the-rest-is-thin">The rest is thin</h2>

Microsoft's Defender Safe Links false-flagging Google search URLs is an operational embarrassment, not a thesis change - though Microsoft's absence from today's agent-compute and agent-distribution news flow, while Salesforce, Atlassian, and Adobe all show up, is the kind of silence that adds up.
