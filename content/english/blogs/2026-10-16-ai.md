A research paper from Goodfire argues that [neural networks are already clean](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/dxaIP7-edFVW8hJwrQ1TlQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMTYtYWl8aW50ZXJwcmV0YWJpbGl0eS1yZXNlYXJjaC1hcmd1ZXMtc3VwZXJwb3NpdGlvbi1pcy1vdmVyYmxvd258fGh0dHBzOi8vYWxyZWFkeS1jbGVhbi5nb29kZmlyZS5jb20vP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ), challenging the baseline assumption that polysemanticity is primarily caused by superposition. Historically, researchers assumed that models packed unrelated concepts into individual neurons because the parameter space lacked sufficient dimensions, requiring complex dictionary learning to untangle internal states.

Goodfire's analysis shows that apparent arbitrary packing is largely recursive generalization. If networks organize concepts naturally into structured recursive abstractions rather than messy overlays, engineers can analyze internal representations directly without complex post-hoc decomposition layers. The finding simplifies the outlook for mechanistic interpretability, suggesting safety audits can inspect model activations without building massive projection dictionaries first.

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/BA8u7sxNaTrd9ScI7AIn8A/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMTYtYWl8cG9saWN5LWdyYWRpZW50cy1hY3QtYXMtcGFyYW1ldGVyLWNvdmFyaWFuY2VzfHxodHRwczovL3JpY2hhcmRsaS54eXovcG9zdC9ncmFkaWVudC1pcy1jb3ZhcmlhbmNlLz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: richardli.xyz ↗</a></p>

A mathematical deep dive into [expected policy gradients](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/BA8u7sxNaTrd9ScI7AIn8A/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMTYtYWl8cG9saWN5LWdyYWRpZW50cy1hY3QtYXMtcGFyYW1ldGVyLWNvdmFyaWFuY2VzfHxodHRwczovL3JpY2hhcmRsaS54eXovcG9zdC9ncmFkaWVudC1pcy1jb3ZhcmlhbmNlLz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) demonstrates why reinforcement learning, supervised fine-tuning, and on-policy distillation alter only 5% to 30% of a model's parameters during post-training. The paper formalizes that the expected policy gradient acts as a covariance, while the diagonal Fisher information matrix operates as the variance of logit sensitivities.

Because the score maintains a zero mean under the sampling distribution, parameter updates are strictly capped by the Fisher matrix regardless of how large the scalar reward is. If a parameter shift moves all candidate tokens equally, the update evaluates to zero. This framework gives ML engineers a way to predict parameter sparsity and calculate weight updates mathematically without running expensive on-policy rollouts.

Post-training setups for multi-step agentic workflows are running into hardware and distribution limits, detailed in an analysis of [multi-turn reinforcement learning](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/7fvudUAU5U1xejU-WRydoA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMTYtYWl8bG9uZy1ob3Jpem9uLWFnZW50LXJsLWZhY2VzLXRyYWplY3RvcnktZHJpZnR8fGh0dHBzOi8vaWFuYmFyYmVyLmJsb2cvMjAyNi8xMC8xNS9tby10dXJucy1tby1wcm9ibGVtcy8_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t). While single-turn RL relies on simple prompt-completion scoring, agent rollouts run for hours - such as Alibaba's 4-hour sessions for its 2.4T model - involving arbitrary subagents, external bash commands, and dynamic context compaction.

These extended trajectories degrade GPU utilization and trigger policy drift when execution sandboxes preempt or delay rollouts. Infrastructure teams are responding with two patterns: running persistent sandbox proxies that shuffle KV caches across GPU clusters dynamically, or embedding the orchestration harness inside the container sandbox as implemented in DeepSeek v4.1.
