JPMorgan's institutional ledger is no longer confined to permissioned subnets or Layer 2 rollups. The bank's Kinexys unit has deployed its USD deposit token, JPM Coin (JPMD), directly onto [Ethereum mainnet](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/bK8L7Ylzo29sK_7-Dcur9A/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMjAtY3J5cHRvfHx8aHR0cHM6Ly9saW5rcy50bGRybmV3c2xldHRlci5jb20vcDJHODBLP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ). 

This is not a wrapped synthetic or an unbacked private stablecoin. JPMD represents commercial bank deposits held at JPMorgan Chase, now directly transferable peer-to-peer across public EVM rails. For systems engineers building institutional settlement pipelines, it eliminates the custom API integrations traditionally required to move balances inside JPMorgan's perimeter.

The move comes alongside a clear macro shift in digital cash: token velocity is decoupling from minting volume. Data shows stablecoins are actively circulating through consumer payment stacks rather than idling in treasuries, while the underlying consensus layers work aggressively to drive transaction finality down to single-digit seconds.

JPMorgan originally deployed JPMD on Coinbase’s Base rollup in November 2025. Bringing the token to [Ethereum Layer 1](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/CHCgKix-zzvH_8PGF-c7IA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMjAtY3J5cHRvfGpwbW9yZ2FuLWRlcGxveXMtanBtLWNvaW4tdG8tZXRoZXJldW0tbWFpbm5ldHx8aHR0cHM6Ly9saW5rcy50bGRybmV3c2xldHRlci5jb20vcDJHODBLP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) enables institutional clients to settle transactions on the base consensus layer where high-value DeFi protocols and tokenized funds live. Kinexys reports that its infrastructure handles over $4 trillion in cumulative transaction volume, averaging $7 billion per day. 

For backend teams, JPMD is functionally distinct from traditional asset-backed stablecoins like USDC or USDT. It is a digital deposit liability issued directly by a Globally Systemically Important Bank (G-SIB). It enables 24/7 programmable gross settlement without running overnight clearing processes through Fedwire or CHIPS. 

The mainnet launch gives institutions a native cash leg to interact with institutional smart contracts. That includes JPMorgan’s own tokenized money-market funds, the first of which launched on Ethereum in December, followed by a second fund in May. Instead of redeeming funds to off-chain bank accounts via batch files, asset managers can execute atomic swaps directly against commercial bank money.

Digital dollar transaction volumes are growing rapidly even as aggregate circulating supply sits idle. According to data from Allium, stablecoin payment volume grew [45% year-over-year to $482 billion](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/6DpgaAgPsjfaMkM4Bw4Ulg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMjAtY3J5cHRvfHN0YWJsZWNvaW4tcGF5bWVudHMtc3VyZ2UtNDUtd2hpbGUtdG9rZW4taXNzdWFuY2Utc3RhbGxzfHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5jb2luZGVzay5jb20vYnVzaW5lc3MvMjAyNi8xMC8xOC9zdGFibGVjb2luLXN1cHBseS1zdGFsbHMtYnV0LXBheW1lbnRzLWp1bXAtNDUtYXMtdmlzYS1tYXN0ZXJjYXJkLWFuZC1zdHJpcGUtY29ubmVjdC1jcnlwdG8tdG8tY2FzaD91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) across more than 1 billion transactions through September 2026. Over the exact same period, total circulating stablecoin supply expanded by only 0.4%, moving from roughly $303 billion to $305 billion.

The turnover velocity is being driven by direct integrations into merchant acquirers and card networks. Stablecoin-linked payment card spending hit $3.9 billion - a tenfold increase over the prior year - with transactions under $50 driving 84% of transaction count growth. 

Financial infrastructure providers are rolling this out at the orchestration layer:
* **Mastercard** deployed its Global Money Platform with BVNK, combining fiat accounts, stablecoin balances, and card rails for processors like Nuvei and Marqeta.
* **Stripe** is expanding stablecoin debit card issuance from 30 countries to over 100 via its partnership with Plasma.
* **Visa** went live with Inyo Global as the inaugural client for Visa Direct’s USDC prefunding pipeline, settling cross-border flows without pre-allocating local currency reserves in destination accounts.

Even with this spike, stablecoin card volume remains around 0.03% of Visa’s $4 trillion quarterly payment run rate. The technical pipe works, but consumer adoption is in its infancy.

Gnosis has officially activated Ethereum's [Fast Confirmation Rule (FCR)](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/w7ePPiVCHLL3QuzFPi6XVQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMjAtY3J5cHRvfGdub3Npcy1zaGlwcy1mYXN0LWNvbmZpcm1hdGlvbi1ydWxlcy1hcy1ldGhlcmV1bS10YXJnZXRzLXN1Yi1taW51dGUtZmluYWxpdHl8fGh0dHBzOi8vbGlua3MudGxkcm5ld3NsZXR0ZXIuY29tL1k3M0JiSz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) on production nodes. The update slashes bridge deposit confirmation delays from roughly 14 minutes down to 12 to 24 seconds. Consensus client developers have implemented FCR upstream behind opt-in flags, clearing the way for rollups and L2 bridges to safely ingest L1 state without waiting two full epochs for deterministic Casper finality.

The formal verification of the FCR spec was completed in Lean by AI agent tools in four to five days - a process that historically took several months. The machine-checked proof caught an edge-case fork-choice vulnerability in the specification itself prior to deployment. 

The update arrives alongside broader pipeline work on base-layer Ethereum latency and security primitives. At an infrastructure workshop in Cambridge, Barnabé Monnot presented a roadmap targeting single-slot finality down to tens of seconds within the I* fork following the Hegotá upgrade. 

Engineers running validator infrastructure should also track the newly launched `heze-devnet-2`, which is now running Quick Slots (EIP-8198) alongside Fork-Choice Inclusion Lists (FOCIL) to constrain proposer builder separation abuse. The Glamsterdam testnet upgrade lands on the Hoodi testnet on October 26.

Operating simplified trading frontends on top of high-throughput execution engines carries steep integration drag. A forensic analysis tracking [400,000 retail wallets](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/w7Fx2VRbktuaXV0GT-aFFA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMjAtY3J5cHRvfHRoZS0yODBtLWNvc3Qtb2YtcmV0YWlsLW9uYm9hcmRpbmctb24taHlwZXJsaXF1aWR8fGh0dHBzOi8vbGlua3MudGxkcm5ld3NsZXR0ZXIuY29tLzRNdXNZRz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) on the Hyperliquid perpetual exchange across six consumer wallet frontends - Phantom, MetaMask, Rabby, Trust Wallet, Invo, and fomo - revealed $280 million in total user losses. 

Of that total, $126 million was lost after November 10, 2025. Builder fees levied by the UI wrappers accounted for more than half of those recent losses. Frontends added a surcharge of 6.5 to 14.5 basis points per $100 ordered on top of Hyperliquid’s base protocol fee. Because typical consumer accounts had initial deposits of just $30 to $60, high-frequency position cycling compounded the builder fee drag rapidly. 

On the Invo frontend, users deposited $12.4 million in aggregate capital and turned it over into $5.8 billion in total volume. On BTC positions specifically, the cumulative builder fees exceeded the net directional gains of every winning trade combined. 

The retention metrics reflect this churn. Across all six client applications, 50% of monthly active addresses were first-time users, and only one in seven remained active after 90 days. For infrastructure developers, the takeaway is clear: embedding perpetual contract wrappers into consumer crypto wallets acts as an account-draining funnel unless order flow sizes are high enough to survive basis-point margin stacking.

Revolut has secured a Distributed Ledger Technology provider licence from the [Gibraltar Financial Services Commission](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/47_MbwYcfJOXQjdY-5aGMQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMjAtY3J5cHRvfHJldm9sdXQtdGFyZ2V0cy1pbnRlcm5hdGlvbmFsLWRpZ2l0YWwtYXNzZXQtb3BlcmF0aW9ucy12aWEtZ2licmFsdGFyfHxodHRwczovL2xpbmtzLnRsZHJuZXdzbGV0dGVyLmNvbS9hbUttR2o_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t). The regulatory approval was granted to a dedicated operating subsidiary, Revolut Global Digital Assets Limited. 

The neobank, which claims 80 million retail users and 16 million crypto-enabled accounts, operates its European digital asset trading desks under local EEA entities aligned with the EU's Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) framework, alongside a UK operational entity. Revolut did not disclose which jurisdictions the new Gibraltar arm will service. The expansion follows the company obtaining conditional approval from the OCC for a US commercial banking charter last month and the ongoing regional rollout of its EURR euro-pegged stablecoin across Denmark, Poland, and Portugal.
