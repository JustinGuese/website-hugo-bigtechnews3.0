After years of budget uncertainty and program reviews, NASA has re-established launch windows for its flagship missions to Venus. [Ars Technica reports](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/yN7Qqm5EBRZD1eVEi7oElw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMTUtdGVjaHxuYXNhLWxvY2tzLWluLWVhcmx5LTIwMzBzLWxhdW5jaC1kYXRlcy1mb3ItdmVudXN8fGh0dHBzOi8vYXJzdGVjaG5pY2EuY29tL3NwYWNlLzIwMjYvMTAvbmFzYXMtZW1iYXR0bGVkLXZlbnVzLW1pc3Npb25zLWFyZS1iYWNrLW9uLXRyYWNrLWFmdGVyLWNhbmNlbGxhdGlvbi10aHJlYXQvP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) that DAVINCI (Deep Atmosphere Venus Investigation of Noble gases, Chemistry, and Imaging) is now set to fly in December 2030, while VERITAS (Venus Emissivity, Radio science, InSAR, Topography, And Spectroscopy) is scheduled for November 2032. 

DAVINCI will drop a parachute-assisted probe through the Venusian clouds, sending back the first direct optical surface imaging since the Soviet Venera landings in 1982. VERITAS will map the planet’s surface topography from orbit using synthetic aperture radar. They will operate alongside the European Space Agency’s Envision orbiter (targeted for 2032) and Rocket Lab’s commercial cloud probe, which could launch as soon as 2027. The simultaneous missions mean deep-space communications and high-temperature planetary sensor hardware will have long-term operational targets to build against.

Data from [Ramp's latest AI Index](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/mVoOYfSlUP-SbOnnJ_QKcQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMTUtdGVjaHxlbnRlcnByaXNlLXNwZW5kLWhpdHMtdGhlLXRvcC1vZi10aGUtcy1jdXJ2ZXx8aHR0cHM6Ly9lY29ubGFiLnN1YnN0YWNrLmNvbS9wL2FpLWluZGV4LW9jdC0yMDI2P3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) indicates that enterprise adoption has hit a plateau at 55.9%, up just 0.62 percentage points. <h2 id="timescale-open-sources-pgvectorsearch-with-prism-indexing"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/DE_osKwl6VnyVkaZn2x0DQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMTUtdGVjaHx0aW1lc2NhbGUtb3Blbi1zb3VyY2VzLXBndmVjdG9yc2VhcmNoLXdpdGgtcHJpc20taW5kZXhpbmd8fGh0dHBzOi8vZ2l0aHViLmNvbS90aW1lc2NhbGUvcGdfdmVjdG9yc2VhcmNoP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Timescale open-sources pgvectorsearch with PRISM indexing</a></h2>

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/DE_osKwl6VnyVkaZn2x0DQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMTUtdGVjaHx0aW1lc2NhbGUtb3Blbi1zb3VyY2VzLXBndmVjdG9yc2VhcmNoLXdpdGgtcHJpc20taW5kZXhpbmd8fGh0dHBzOi8vZ2l0aHViLmNvbS90aW1lc2NhbGUvcGdfdmVjdG9yc2VhcmNoP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: github.com ↗</a></p>

Timescale has launched [pg_vectorsearch](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/DE_osKwl6VnyVkaZn2x0DQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMTUtdGVjaHx0aW1lc2NhbGUtb3Blbi1zb3VyY2VzLXBndmVjdG9yc2VhcmNoLXdpdGgtcHJpc20taW5kZXhpbmd8fGh0dHBzOi8vZ2l0aHViLmNvbS90aW1lc2NhbGUvcGdfdmVjdG9yc2VhcmNoP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ), an alpha PostgreSQL extension tailored for approximate nearest-neighbor (ANN) lookups over 100 million or more vectors. The project implements a new PRISM index, combining IVF-style hierarchical routing, RaBitQ quantization with strict mathematical error bounds, and SIMD optimizations spanning AVX2, AVX-512, and ARM NEON. 

Timescale claims the extension delivers faster query throughput and reduced index build times compared to `pgvector`’s standard HNSW and IVFFlat implementations, all while maintaining competitive recall and supporting dynamic index splits. It remains an alpha release, but it points to a future where high-scale vector workloads can stay inside traditional relational architectures instead of requiring dedicated vector databases.
