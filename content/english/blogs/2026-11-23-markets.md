The day belonged to Anthropic's close: $93.5B from the IPO plus $26B of Class N shares placed directly with AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, NVIDIA, and TSMC. Strip the headline and read the cap table - the company's largest suppliers are now its largest shareholders, which means the compute demand that justifies their datacenter buildouts is partly self-referential. We've been negative NVIDIA on exactly this circularity for a week; today it stopped being an inference and became a filed fact. The second-order name here isn't the obvious accelerator vendor: it's whoever sells the power. Anthropic's own disclosures - 15 GW contracted, 1.7 GW of gas generators for $3.4B, ~87% gas and 11% coal projected for 2028 - say the binding constraint has moved from silicon to megawatts, and that the "renewable-powered AI" narrative is not what the contracts say.

> When your customers' equity appears on your own balance sheet, revenue quality and valuation quality stop being the same number.

<h2 id="agents-start-spending-money">Agents start spending money</h2>

The most underpriced datapoint of the day is Stripe's Link Agent Wallet: daily spend up nearly 80x since early September, doubling in the last month, with travel and lodging over 40% and over half of consumers making repeat purchases. This is the first volume evidence that agent commerce is transactions, not demos. The obvious read is Stripe; the second-order read is travel - an agent-booked lodging mix that large means distribution economics in travel get repriced before anyone updates models. Anthropic's linked billing beta (platforms like Asana and Linear passing Claude API costs to end customers) completes the loop: AI spend decouples from platform gross margins, which is quietly good for the platforms and quietly bad for anyone whose pricing assumed AI costs were absorbed upstream.

<h2 id="monitoring-breaks-quietly">Monitoring breaks, quietly</h2>

AWS's claim that 95% of enterprises run agent workflows in production - across application, reasoning, model, and infrastructure layers - is a requirements document for the observability industry. Datadog has already started splitting the agent bill; today's news says the four-layer problem is now mainstream, not a positioning exercise. The non-obvious beneficiary is whoever owns the identity and secrets layer: GitHub's air-gapped AI secret detection and eight-hour OAuth tokens, plus the OpenRouter canary study showing prompt-level data leakage across the ecosystem, all point at a security gap the endpoint vendors haven't priced.

<h2 id="exchange-plumbing-and-the-rest">Exchange plumbing, and the rest</h2>

Regulated-native liquidity is now a competitive fact, not a threat. Elsewhere: Google pushed agent infrastructure into open source (Agent Substrate to CNCF, sub-500ms resume) and GA'd sidecar-less ambient mesh on GKE - consistent with our positive Alphabet stance on being the neutral substrate. Tesla's FSD v15 rollout is the same self-graded safety-multiplier story as last week; stale. OpenAI's perplexity disclosure and distillation-defense numbers are interesting safety bookkeeping but move nothing today. A thin day for the semis on the surface - but the supplier placement in the Anthropic close is the semiconductor story, and it cuts against the names holding the equity.
