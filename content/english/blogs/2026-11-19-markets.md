The day's most useful number was a null result. Agent-driven purchases remain negligible across the consumer platforms that were supposed to be disrupted first: Uber sees four agent rides per quarter, DoorDash a few hundred against 10M daily deliveries, and Airbnb calls them "very non-material." This is the first time the platforms themselves have published the figure, and it deflates the agentic-commerce revenue narrative that has run for weeks. The infrastructure bets - tokenized payments, agent detection, compliance tooling - are not invalidated by this, but their timing assumptions just moved out. The second-order read: the card networks and identity/compliance vendors benefit from agent traffic *detection* before anyone benefits from agent traffic *volume*. Plaid's Agent Detection launch is exactly that trade - selling the audit before the transaction.

> The most honest datapoint of the agentic-commerce cycle came from the companies themselves: four agent rides a quarter is not a distribution channel, it's a demo.

<h2 id="anthropic-prices-the-arms-race">Anthropic prices the arms race</h2>
The [Anthropic IPO](#) priced at $83/share, raising $81.3B at a ~$1.5T fully diluted cap - the largest IPO ever, with Google, Microsoft, Nvidia and AMD investing $26B collectively. The more interesting number is the S-1 disclosure of $447.5B in compute commitments through 2030, roughly $100B/year, with Epoch estimating Anthropic's compute spend growing 4x/year against OpenAI's 3x. The second-order effect lands on the suppliers: at this intensity, the binding constraint is accelerators and memory, which is why the Intel story below matters more than it looks.

<h2 id="intel-finally-gets-a-foundry-customer-that-counts">Intel finally gets a foundry customer that counts</h2>
Intel and Microsoft expanded their partnership: Microsoft is the first user of Intel Foundry's EMIB packaging for Azure silicon, alongside multi-year Xeon (Diamond Rapids) supply and up to 2.3x Azure M-series v4 VM performance via CXL. Foundry wins have been announced before; this one differs because EMIB packaging is a real production commitment from a hyperscaler with alternatives, and it pairs with a Xeon order that supports the CPU business independently. For Microsoft, this is supply-chain diversification against TSMC concentration - groundwork, not earnings. We keep our negative MSFT stance: the Windows agent control plane (Entra Agent ID, Execution Containers, Intune management) and the AI Transparency Initiative are positioning for a governance market that doesn't yet have a price.

<h2 id="googles-model-day-one-win-one-self-inflicted-wound">Google&#x27;s model day: one win, one self-inflicted wound</h2>
Google released Gemini 4 Neon broadly at $3/$15 per M tokens with a 1M context, scoring 65.4% on CursorBench versus Opus 5.5's 58.1% at 32% lower cost per task - a direct shot at Anthropic on its home turf, and supportive of our positive GOOGL stance. But the same day surfaced two execution problems: Gemini 4 Carbon took 22m21s versus Opus 5.5's 9m33s on a real coding task, and a disassembly of Antigravity found a 15-second prompt-cache eviction cliff that makes interactive coding a worst-case cost workload. Separately, Edgee's analysis of mid-conversation routing as a ski-rental problem (switching discards warm cache) means the switching cost Google just created works in both directions - it locks in Neon users but punishes Carbon.
