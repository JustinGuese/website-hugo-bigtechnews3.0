<h2 id="the-s-1-is-the-document-anthropics-revenue-is-substantially-a-cloud-channel-story">The S-1 is the document: Anthropic&#x27;s revenue is substantially a cloud-channel story</h2>

Anthropic filed to list as "ANT" at a potential $100bn raise against a ~$2trn valuation, on $19.7bn of FY2026 revenue with Q2 at $11.6bn, up 1,368% year over year. We covered the draft prospectus and the debt backstop recently; today is the filing itself with hard numbers, so this narrative is building, not stale.

The disclosure that matters is not the valuation - it is that 47% of 2025 revenue flows through Amazon and Google cloud partners, with the two largest customers at 12% of 2024 revenue each. The obvious name in this story is Anthropic, which you cannot own until the listing. The less obvious beneficiaries are the channels: the S-1 hands AMZN and GOOGL an audited, third-party-verified demand anchor for their AI consumption narratives. Cloud capex stops being a leap of faith when a private lab's prospectus quantifies who resells the compute. The concentration cuts both ways - Anthropic depends on two channels - but for the clouds it is booked revenue.

The conspicuous absence is Oracle. ORCL spent the week courting AI workloads - Vera Rubin NVL72 Superclusters, the Acceleron RDMA fabric, first-party Snowflake - and now lands Google TPU v8 on OCI from 2027. That is a silicon-arbitrage strategy: rent everyone's constrained compute to everyone. It is a real business, and we move positive on it, but the S-1 shows Oracle has not yet cracked the model-lab consumption channel that Amazon and Google own. SNOW's first-party AI Data Cloud on OCI is a distribution win inside that same strategy; it does not answer the consumption-growth question, so it stays neutral for us.

<h2 id="agentic-software-delivery-goes-ga-and-the-token-bill-arrives">Agentic software delivery goes GA, and the token bill arrives</h2>

GitHub shipped the Copilot app and made Copilot Fusion generally available: multi-model orchestration with three-tier automated routing, claiming ~50% token-cost reduction and 40% faster task completion, plus self-improving meta-automations. Microsoft exposed the same multi-model harness through Foundry with MCP support. Slack made the Code API generally available (two items in today's feed, one story), opening dedicated multi-user code channels to any agent vendor.

The demand side is no longer hypothetical. Ramp's Inspect agent generates 74% of merged pull requests at 3.4× PRs per person. Spawn burns $3.1M per month on Anthropic tokens to push ~77k production changes. Pair that with Anthropic shipping enterprise reporting, in-app surveys and pooled group budgets, and the picture is coherent: token spend is becoming an audited ROI line item, and the labs are building the finops layer themselves.

The second-order read sits above the models. When the flagship coding platform's headline feature is *routing between models to cut token cost 50%*, the neutral gateway layer - policy, caching, routing, observability outside any single vendor - becomes infrastructure. That is the category NET sells through AI Gateway, and it is why we stay positive: every enterprise adopting routed multi-model inference needs someone neutral holding the routing table. And note the elasticity: Gemini 4 Carbon just cut prices while Spawn's bill grows. Volume is outrunning price, which is the correct bear-case rebuttal for inference margins.

<h2 id="googles-pincer-cut-inference-prices-rent-the-silicon-to-rivals-clouds">Google&#x27;s pincer: cut inference prices, rent the silicon to rivals&#x27; clouds</h2>

Gemini 4 Carbon prices at $2/$10 per million tokens with a 95% discount on cached inputs - a structure that specifically favors agentic workloads, which are read-heavy - and leads security benchmarks at 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro. The Connected Apps Platform extends Gemini distribution through MCP servers into third-party apps.

The bolder move is TPU v8 on Oracle Cloud from 2027: 12.6 PFLOPS FP4 per chip, >97% goodput. Google is externalizing TPU distribution into a rival's cloud. For NVDA, that seeds a credible second silicon channel at hyperscaler scale, and it is why our negative stance holds - though the Vera Rubin NVL72 expansion on OCI is the partial offset that keeps conviction low.

<h2 id="microsoft-the-print-that-broke-our-bear-case">Microsoft: the print that broke our bear case</h2>

We reverse the MSFT negative call today. The thesis rested on capex squeezing free cash flow; the quarter printed $18.6bn of FCF, roughly $10bn above estimates, with Azure at 48% and Q2 guided to 49 - 50% against 47.6% consensus. What changed is the numbers, not the narrative - if Q2 confirms acceleration with FCF intact, we go further positive. Separately, Entra retires SMS and voice MFA in February 2027 with passkeys defaulting in September: the passkey transition is now enforced by the largest identity provider, which validates the category while raising the default-installed competitive bar for standalone vendors like Okta.

<h2 id="crypto-rails-settlement-market-microstructure-and-an-agent-with-a-wallet">Crypto rails: settlement, market microstructure, and an agent with a wallet</h2>

Ripple partnered with HOOD to run RLUSD through institutional settlement, treasury automation and financing via Ripple Prime and Bitstamp; Fynd's "last-mover" AMM went live on Robinhood, Base, Arbitrum and Ethereum with a 2.7× LP-fee claim we treat as unverified. Slack open-sourced the Centaur multi-agent framework and the Reth 2.0 high-throughput Ethereum client. The thread tying it together is Instinct's demo: an agent converted stablecoins via Stripe, moved USD to a Lead Bank prepaid account, and paid with a Visa virtual card - $700, end to end. Agents need programmable money, and stablecoin rails are becoming that substrate, which is the quiet case for HOOD's infrastructure drift.
