The [Kava Lend exploit](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/q0T0f3hD0ogtJ40tDNXzhw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTYtY3J5cHRvfHRoZS0zOG0ta2F2YS1sZW5kLWhhY2stdGhhdC1zZW50LWthdmEtZG93bi00MHx8aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlYmxvY2suY28vbmV3cy9kZWZpLzIwMjYtMTEtMTQta2F2YS1sZW5kLWxvc2VzLXJvdWdobHktMy04LW1pbGxpb24tdG8taGFjay1zZW5kaW5nLWthdmEtdG9rZW4tZG93bi1uZWFybHktNDAtNDQwMzUwP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) is a textbook case of DeFi's long-tail risk. An attacker drained about 55.2M KAVA (~$3.76M at pre-attack prices) by exploiting a dormant BUSD market. The trick: shrink the market's net supply to a tiny nonzero figure so deposit values inflated roughly 243,000-fold, then borrow KAVA against the inflated collateral 127 times. About 49.2M KAVA was bridged to BNB Chain through LayerZero and sold for about $2.1M.

The attacker had been preparing since November 8. Kava says the issue is "not an infinite mint" and is contained to Kava Lend, has paused the Axelar bridge, and promises a full incident report. KAVA was down about 39% at publication, and exchanges paused deposits and withdrawals. For anyone running DeFi protocols: dormant markets are attack surface, and net supply manipulation remains a viable vector even without a minting bug.

[OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/xBbkU8olrb9ZaXqJn1Pg9w/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTYtY3J5cHRvfGNoYXRncHQtd2FsbGV0LWFnZW50cy1wYXktd2l0aC15b3VyLWNhcmQtbm90LXRoZWlyc3x8aHR0cHM6Ly94LmNvbS9hbGZvbmdqL3N0YXR1cy8yMTIxNjgyOTUyMTU3MzE1NTMwP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) on Friday, and it's a genuinely different architecture for agentic payments. The "dot" agent checks out on merchant sites using the user's own credit card. At checkout, the card network generates a one-time substitute card number capped at the approved amount, so the agent never holds the real card details, and the user keeps points and dispute rights.

This contrasts with wallets like Stripe's Link that charge your card in a second transaction, giving issuers better transaction visibility and merchants lower interchange fees. Under the hood it uses a vault, enrollment in the networks' agent programs (Visa Intelligent Commerce, Mastercard Agent Pay, and Amex ACE), and two sets of headers: Web Bot Auth signatures and user signals in the Forwarded header, with OpenAI working with fraud providers like Forter, Riskified, Sardine, and Signifyd. Users approve each purchase with Face ID, and it's limited to US ChatGPT Pro users 18 or older with Amex, Mastercard, or Visa credit cards only. Drawbacks: sites still block agents, per-purchase approval adds friction, and checkout is slow and token-heavy.
