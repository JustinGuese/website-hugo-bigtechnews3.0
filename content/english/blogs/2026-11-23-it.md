<h2 id="circuit-boards-are-the-new-bottleneck">Circuit boards are the new bottleneck</h2>

The problem is not just volume, it is complexity. AI servers need more layers, denser routing, and higher-end materials than a typical industrial controller. Each board soaks up disproportionately more manufacturing capacity, and fab lines cannot switch on a dime. The Global Electronics Association says the strain is now pushing non-AI orders to the back of the queue, with automotive and medical buyers feeling it first.

For engineers, the practical takeaway is to lock in board supply early and design for alternative sources. The crunch is not a rumor of a shortage, it is a forecast of one that runs to mid-2028. If your product has a PCB in it and is not an AI box, start qualifying a second supplier now.

The cost-control framework is a three-step approach: identify use cases, distinguish between general productivity tasks and centrally managed agents, and differentiate custom solutions. Then estimate consumption based on factors like employee count and process frequency, with budgets and limits set to cap spending.

Von Rüden admits predicting expenses remains difficult. That is the honest part. For engineers running agentic AI, this is a useful governance pattern: tier your agents, cap their budgets, and do not pretend you can forecast token burn precisely. The framework is SAP-specific, but the shape of it - central control, usage limits, and a clear distinction between commodity and custom agents - transfers to any platform.

Forrester analysts argue that [traditional cloud monitoring is insufficient for AI workloads](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/k2efuxih_LzfVvze-lWh9Q/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjMtaXR8eW91ci1tb25pdG9yaW5nLWlzLWJsaW5kLXRvLWFpLWZhaWx1cmVzfHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5jZG90cmVuZHMuY29tL3N0b3J5LzUxODQveW91ci1jbG91ZC1vcGVyYXRpb25zLWFyZW50LXJlYWR5LWFpP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ). An AI service can be technically healthy while producing wrong outputs, drifting, or violating policies. They distinguish between AI-enhanced observability (using AI for operations) and observability for AI (monitoring model drift, output quality, prompts, token consumption, and business outcomes).

The key insight is that AI failures are gradual degradation, not clear outages. Your pager will not fire when a model's output quality silently slips. Recovery now means restoring the model to a trustworthy state, not just restarting the pod. If you operate anything with a model in the path, add output-quality and drift metrics to your dashboards, and define what "trustworthy" means for your use case before you need it.

Google announced the [general availability of ambient networking for GKE](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/TJjmZLa8acpz0pLpvJUBQg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjMtaXR8YW1iaWVudC1uZXR3b3JraW5nLWZvci1na2UtZ29lcy1nYXx8aHR0cHM6Ly9jbG91ZC5nb29nbGUuY29tL2Jsb2cvcHJvZHVjdHMvbmV0d29ya2luZy9hbWJpZW50LW5ldHdvcmtpbmctZm9yLWdrZS1zaWRlY2FybGVzcy1zZXJ2aWNlLW1lc2g_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t), a sidecar-less service mesh architecture. It decouples mesh functionality from application pods, shifting networking responsibilities to shared per-node components like an L4 Envoy proxy, an xDS agent, and a control plane using Traffic Director and Certificate Authority Service.

For anyone who has run Istio with sidecars, the appeal is immediate: no more doubling your pod count for proxies, no more managing sidecar versions, no more memory overhead on every replica. The trade-off is that L4-only means you lose some L7 features, and the per-node model changes how you think about blast radius. But for production meshes on GKE, this is a real operational win. If you skipped service meshes because of the overhead, this is worth a second look.
