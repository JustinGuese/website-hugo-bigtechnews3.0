Morgan Stanley is pushing its tokenized equities launch on its alternative trading system (ATS) back to Q1 2027. Amy Oldenburg, the bank's head of digital asset strategy, previously targeted late 2026, but the operational stack simply is not ready. Institutional clients cannot trade assets that custodians cannot yet hold under federal oversight. 

The delay highlights a growing split in real-world asset (RWA) infrastructure. While permissionless platforms continue deploying synthetic and wrapper stocks on rollups, institutional desks are waiting on two specific milestones: the Depository Trust & Clearing Corporation's (DTCC) tokenization service rollout later this month, and the GENIUS Act taking effect in January 2027.

Despite the schedule slip, Oldenburg maintained that the broader tokenized RWA segment will hit between $3 trillion and $10 trillion in aggregate value by 2030. Behind the scenes, Morgan Stanley is engineering its own abstracted wallet systems, operating an internal Digital Asset Lab, and holding conditional approval from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) to stand up a dedicated digital trust.

Automated market makers continue to bleed capital through loss-versus-rebalancing (LVR), where latency between off-chain order books and on-chain liquidity pools lets searchers pull arbitrage value out of passive pools. A [new design paper](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/kt9264nrHv4NpXRHUMnDXw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMTMtY3J5cHRvfGJyby1hbW0tcHJvcG9zZXMtb3JhY2xlLXJlY29uY2lsaWF0aW9uLXRvLXN0b3AtbHZyLWxvc3Nlc3x8aHR0cHM6Ly9ldGhyZXNlYXIuY2gvdC9icm8tYW1tLWEtYm9uZGVkLXJlY29uY2lsaWF0aW9uLW9yYWNsZS1hbW0tYXMtYS1sb3ctcmlzay1kZWZpLXByaW1pdGl2ZS8yNjEzOD91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) introduced the Bonded Reconciliation Oracle AMM (BRO-AMM) to cut off that structural drain.

The protocol restructures swap finality. Instead of settling pool swaps immediately against a static invariant curve ($x \cdot y = k$), BRO-AMM enforces a post-trade reconciliation window. Swappers post collateral bonds alongside their transactions, which settle only after a finality-verified oracle checks the off-chain external market price. 

Simulations over six months of ETH/USDC trading show BRO-AMM reduced arbitrage losses by 68% to 79% on Ethereum, pulling liquidity providers back into net profitability. On Arbitrum, where block times are shorter, LVR fell by 49% to 55%. The trade-off is familiar: the system sacrifices atomic, single-block execution and depends entirely on the uptime, latency, and manipulation resistance of its oracle network.

The mechanics relied on market depth imbalances. The attackers pushed $20 million spot and perp clips into Hyperliquid order books, moving ETH index prices by 10 to 20 basis points. They simultaneously opened nine-figure paper long positions on Paper Trade that referenced the manipulated feed. 

Because the synthetic protocol settled paper orders against the real-time mark price without factoring in liquidity depth or market impact, the attackers captured manufactured price movements at zero execution cost. The vectors demonstrate that synthetic trading engines cannot safely pull marks from isolated, lower-liquidity order books without execution delay buffers or synthetic spread penalties.
