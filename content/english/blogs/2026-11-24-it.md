[PostgreSQL 19's release slipped from September to November](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/_Zaxg2n8OwMHzXWQVltusw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjQtaXR8cG9zdGdyZXMtMTktc2hpcHBlZC1sYXRlLWJlY2F1c2UtYWktZm91bmQtdG9vLW1hbnktYnVnc3x8aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuaW5mb3dvcmxkLmNvbS9hcnRpY2xlLzQyNTE1MzIvaG93LWFpLWltcGFjdGVkLXRoZS1yZWxlYXNlLW9mLXBvc3RncmVzLTE5Lmh0bWw_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t), and AI-assisted development is why. Models like Fable 5, Opus 5, and GPT-6 influenced contributions and testing, and they surfaced enough defects that the team reverted six key features - including SQL/PGQ property graph query support and online enabling/disabling of data checksums. The team decided shipping with known problems was worse than shipping late.

The headline new feature is REPACK (CONCURRENTLY), which reclaims space from dead tuples without the intrusive VACUUM operation. The broader lesson: AI accelerates bug discovery, but human validation and architectural review are still the bottleneck. Expect more database releases to slip as AI-generated code raises the bar on what counts as "tested."
