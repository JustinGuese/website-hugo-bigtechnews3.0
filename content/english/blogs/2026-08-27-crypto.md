## Ethlabs' four-track plan to make Ethereum fast

Ethlabs laid out a "Fast Ethereum" roadmap in a [thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/2092574823939461247.html) that treats speed as the protocol's economic lever, since every second of block time is idle capital and wasted settlement cost. Four workstreams are live.

"Fast blocks" targets the hardcoded 12-second slot, with a cut to 10 seconds proposed for the upcoming Hegotá fork. Hegotá is framed as possibly the last realistic window to start block-time reductions for years, so the timing matters. "Fast finality" is an EF/Consensys proposal to compress the current ~15-minute finality to a couple of minutes in the following fork. "Fast Confirmation Rule" is the closest to production: tens-of-seconds confirmation with formally provable security, already adopted by one major bridge. "Fast interoperability" leans on real-time zk proving to speed L2-to-L1 asset movement. The background material in the thread also revisits EIP-1559, the August 2021 fee-market change that routes usage-driven value out of supply and keeps ETH as the only asset able to pay the basefee, plus a new "RIG Open Problems" set for collaborative research modeled on Flashbots' FRP.

## Bitwise ships non-custodial model portfolios on tokenized equities

Bitwise launched Automated Token Portfolios (ATPs) on top of Coinbase's tokenized stocks, NVDAc, METAc, AAPLc and GOOGLc, all deployed on Base two days prior. The structure is fully non-custodial: Bitwise defines the target allocation, users hold the tokenized equities in their own wallets, and Glider's automated rebalancing protocol keeps weights aligned without taking custody. It's TradFi model-portfolio plumbing rebuilt for self-custody, with one important asterisk, as access is gated to non-US persons under Regulation S, so the addressable market is everywhere except the US. Bitwise's own survey says 32% of financial advisors allocated to crypto in 2025 (up from 22%) and 56% personally own some, while the firm's 2026 calls put new all-time highs on BTC, ETH and SOL and project Bitcoin to be less volatile than Nvidia this year. ## Tokenized deposits under, stablecoins on top

The "stablecoins versus tokenized deposits" framing is the wrong fight, per a [Uniswap-adjacent thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/2092561198310940716.html) on Unichain and tokenized bond issuance. A tokenized deposit is a direct bank liability, best used inside a controlled institutional perimeter for treasury and payments. A stablecoin is a separate liability built to move across open networks. The architecture that actually makes sense has tokenized demand deposits as the operational liquidity layer underneath a stablecoin's reserves, with the issuer holding a deposit claim on the bank, and minting and burning synchronized atomically and 24/7 against reserve receipts, rather than the manual orchestration most issuers do today. The same thread covers Unichain, an OP Stack L2 explicitly designed to fight 12-second Ethereum block latency, uncontrolled MEV, and cross-L2 liquidity fragmentation.

## EIP-8130 over EIP-8141 for native account abstraction

The argument in this [piece](https://x.com/pedrouid/status/2091861469218632158) is that EIP-8130, a standardized product surface, beats EIP-8141, a general-purpose frame-transaction machine, for native AA on EVM rollups. The case rests on a decade of "responsibility displacement" from EIP-86 meta-transactions through ERC-4337, EIP-7702 and now Frame Transactions, with each iteration stacking more infrastructure. EIP-8130 encodes the three primitives users actually need: transaction batching, gas sponsorship, and key rotation, without EIP-8141's seven new opcodes, frame modes, and layered execution model. Deterministic contract addresses let accounts move across chains without bridges, and wallets get a canonical interface for secp256k1, P-256, and WebAuthn/passkey authenticators. The timeline matters: Glamsterdam slipped to Q4 2026, EIP-8141 isn't credibly activatable before late Q2 2027, and L2s don't want to wait. Base is targeting EIP-8130 in its Cobalt upgrade for a roughly 2x per-transaction cost cut, with Optimism partnering to extend native AA across the OP Stack.

## x402 facilitators are eating the micropayments

x402 crossed 1-1.5 million transactions per day for the first time, with volume up about 2x versus June and July, per a [thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/2092240591442808913.html). The catch: average transaction size has fallen to $0.023-$0.031, and the popular third-party facilitators (Coinbase CDP on Base, PayAI on Solana) charge a flat $0.001 per settlement. That fee now eats 32-43% of the average transaction value. Batching, live since May, could slash that cost by 90-99% by settling thousands of transactions at once, but adoption is near zero. The thread doesn't pin down whether the blocker is tooling, counterparty risk, or accounting complexity. For anyone building agent-driven payment flows, that's the number to stare at.

## Airwallex finally adds stablecoins

Airwallex runs cross-border payments across 120+ countries with local currency pools and same-day settlement, and for years it didn't need stablecoins. Its existing rails covered dollar collection, FX, and supplier/employee payouts with compliant infrastructure, and card processing fees already made sub-dollar transactions uneconomical. The thing that changed is AI agents, since agent-driven API calls, compute purchases and data lookups at scale are exactly the sub-dollar use case stablecoins were made for, and US and European regulatory clarity made the path legible. To build the settlement layer, Airwallex incubated Metal, a settlement infrastructure product for regulated institutions, and is adding direct stablecoin support where local rules allow. [Thread here](https://x.com/dankimxyz/status/2092285033214292285). The positioning is explicit: agent payments are the near-term demand driver, slower clearing corridors are secondary.

## Card spending splits, DAC8 pushback, Japan's 24/7 plan, and a Zcash ETF

A 30-day snapshot of crypto card spending shows Wirex One up 59% and Plasma One up 42%, while Bitget Wallet fell 49% and SafePal dropped 62%, per the [spending trends thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/2092547308780925162.html). Wirex separately told users its cards aren't subject to DAC8 reporting (effective January 2026) because Wirex isn't custodian of user funds, a [worth-reading response](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/2092519596242747774.html) for any EU-based issuer thinking about the same posture.

Japan's FSA, Ministry of Finance and Bank of Japan are forming a study group with financial institutions to design 24/7 blockchain settlement for government bonds and stocks, targeting a development plan by early 2027. The [thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/2092455014954565838.html) also recaps Bybit opening registration to Chinese users, the "Dilation Effect" research flagging uncleared BUSD approvals on Binance and a large Multichain address holding roughly $500M USDT, $290M USDC and $480M KCS at KuCoin, plus a May 2023 note on Hong Kong's new crypto regime taking effect June 1.

Grayscale's ZCSH launched as the first Zcash ETF, giving traditional brokerage accounts direct exposure to ZEC: proof-of-work, 21M cap, halving schedule, with optional shielded transactions on top. The [announcement thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/2092268305285607797.html) is short, but the milestone isn't, since this is the first privacy-coin ETF in a US-listed wrapper, and the regulatory path it blazed will matter for every shielded-asset issuer behind it.
