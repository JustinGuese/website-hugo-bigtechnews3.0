Binance has replenished 90% of the $6.3 billion drained from its hot wallets on October 29, backing 100% of its top-ten assets including BTC, ETH, USDT, BNB, and SOL. The exchange covered the shortfall using its Secure Asset Fund for Users (SAFU), regulatory-cleared tranches of its Futures insurance fund, and corporate treasury reserves, with a Zero-Knowledge Proof of Reserves snapshot scheduled for November 3.

The post-incident forensics reveal an architectural failure familiar to backend infrastructure teams: the attackers never needed the raw private keys. Instead, they compromised a DevOps laptop, accessed internal transaction-dispatching microservices, and tricked an automated signing pipeline into approving arbitrary state changes.

Here is the technical reality of the exploit, how security operations at major perp DEXs compare, and the rest of the day’s infrastructure shifts across the ecosystem.

According to [incident disclosures and post-mortem analysis](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/jUrUES96ygaHkb5soWzt9Q/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDItY3J5cHRvfHNpZ25pbmctcG9saWNpZXMtZmFpbGVkLW9uLWVpcC03NzAyLWRlbGVnYXRpb25zfHxodHRwczovL2xpbmtzLnRsZHJuZXdzbGV0dGVyLmNvbS9LQk9ZaXQ_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t), the attackers hit Binance's internal withdrawal systems by compromising a DevOps engineer's machine. Once inside the internal network, the attacker submitted an authorization request containing an [EIP-7702 delegation](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/jUrUES96ygaHkb5soWzt9Q/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDItY3J5cHRvfHNpZ25pbmctcG9saWNpZXMtZmFpbGVkLW9uLWVpcC03NzAyLWRlbGVnYXRpb25zfHxodHRwczovL2xpbmtzLnRsZHJuZXdzbGV0dGVyLmNvbS9LQk9ZaXQ_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t). EIP-7702 allows standard externally owned accounts (EOAs) to temporarily execute smart contract logic by pointing their code field to an external contract address for the duration of a transaction or until overwritten. 

Because Binance’s automated signing orchestrator relied on a blocklist of known-bad payload patterns rather than a strict allowlist of deterministic function signatures, the novel EIP-7702 authorization payload was approved and signed. Control of the hot-wallet address was effectively delegated to an attacker-controlled contract. A month earlier, Bitget lost $350 million via the exact same signing architectural flaw. 

The security post-mortem points out that perp DEXs share similar centralized systemic risks. Hyperliquid, for example, holds $5.6 billion of its $6.5 billion USDC balance in a Coinbase-controlled treasury address moved by unsigned system transactions; a single compromise of those validator signing keys gives complete access to the underlying collateral. By contrast, zero-knowledge exchange architectures like Lighter enforce a 14-day forced-exit escape hatch where server compromise alone cannot drain user balances, although Lighter’s 4-of-7 multisig bypassed upgrade timelocks five times in October alone.

Despite the operational failure, [Binance confirmed](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/4WO85pbdf0XbYuIikFXJTg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDItY3J5cHRvfHNpZ25pbmctcG9saWNpZXMtZmFpbGVkLW9uLWVpcC03NzAyLWRlbGVnYXRpb25zfHxodHRwczovL2xpbmtzLnRsZHJuZXdzbGV0dGVyLmNvbS9HTFFjM0E_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t) that all primary assets are fully backed and users will absorb no financial shortfall. Less liquid, non-top-10 tokens make up the remaining 10% deficit, which Binance is buying back through OTC and open-market execution.

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/IKTRI78qshUVGlYf9lmmkQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDItY3J5cHRvfHN0YXRlbGVzcy1jb21wdXRlLWwxNS1lcGhlbWVyYWwtcm9sbHVwcy1vbi1ldGhlcmV1bXx8aHR0cHM6Ly9ldGhyZXNlYXIuY2gvdC9lcGhlbWVyYWwtcm9sbHVwLXRoZS1uZXctbDEtNS1vZi1ldGhlcmV1bS8yNjE4Nz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: ethresear.ch ↗</a></p>

Ethereum researchers proposed a new architectural pattern dubbed ["L1.5" or Ephemeral Rollups](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/IKTRI78qshUVGlYf9lmmkQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDItY3J5cHRvfHN0YXRlbGVzcy1jb21wdXRlLWwxNS1lcGhlbWVyYWwtcm9sbHVwcy1vbi1ldGhlcmV1bXx8aHR0cHM6Ly9ldGhyZXNlYXIuY2gvdC9lcGhlbWVyYWwtcm9sbHVwLXRoZS1uZXctbDEtNS1vZi1ldGhlcmV1bS8yNjE4Nz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20), designed to eliminate the long-term state burdens carried by standard Layer 2 sequencers. In normal rollup architectures, the L2 holds both user state and application state, requiring complex, stateful fraud proofs or validity assertions alongside exit bridges to allow withdrawals back to the base layer.

Under the L1.5 paradigm, the compute layer is completely stateless:
1. The user's wallet stores and maintains their private state.
2. Ethereum L1 holds the shared state, commitments, nullifiers, and verification contracts.
3. Untrusted, dynamic provers execute transactions as stateless functions over the user’s inputs and current L1 state.
4. Proofs settle directly to L1, updating storage without intermediate rollup operators maintaining user balances.

This construction relies on three core Ethereum improvement proposals. [EIP-8141](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/IKTRI78qshUVGlYf9lmmkQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDItY3J5cHRvfHN0YXRlbGVzcy1jb21wdXRlLWwxNS1lcGhlbWVyYWwtcm9sbHVwcy1vbi1ldGhlcmV1bXx8aHR0cHM6Ly9ldGhyZXNlYXIuY2gvdC9lcGhlbWVyYWwtcm9sbHVwLXRoZS1uZXctbDEtNS1vZi1ldGhlcmV1bS8yNjE4Nz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) introduces frame transactions, allowing contracts to self-pay execution gas only after verifying an execution proof. EIP-8250 introduces keyed nonces to repurpose zero-knowledge nullifiers against double-spending attacks without running an EVM account counter. EIP-8288 standardizes proof aggregation, condensing an entire block's worth of stateless executions into a single recursive SNARK verified once by an L1 node. The design directly serves use cases like private RPC payment ticketing, anonymous balance proofs, and MEV-shielded transaction pools.

An institutional supply-side audit by [RWA.xyz](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/P9ot35U-OtTKFYqXELddIA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDItY3J5cHRvfG1vcnBoby1saXF1aWRpdHktYW5hbHlzaXMtcmV2ZWFscy1hLWN1cmF0b3ItYm90dGxlbmVja3x8aHR0cHM6Ly9saW5rcy50bGRybmV3c2xldHRlci5jb20vVXpmTFVNP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) analyzing $6.1 billion deposited into Morpho's variable-rate stablecoin vaults concluded that stablecoin supply, not borrower leverage demand, remains the primary growth bottleneck. Curators - principally Steakhouse, Sentora, Gauntlet, SparkDAO, and RockawayX - control over 80% ($5.0 billion) of deployed capital allocation.

Of the $2.6 billion in explicitly attributed institutional liquidity, $1.1 billion stems from retail-facing exchange "earn" integrations, and another $1.1 billion comes from Sky-authorized (formerly MakerDAO) protocol balance sheets. Protocol allocations are effectively capped by internal governance-mandated credit lines, while synthetic dollar reserves like Ethena are restricted by off-chain delta-neutral collateral requirements.
