The Commerce Department is shutting down a workaround that let foreign rivals rent advanced chips from data centers in Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Brazil, bypassing export restrictions. Companies headquartered in high-risk countries must now apply for licenses with strict know-your-customer controls when transferring high volumes of chips, and licensed firms must submit monthly reports detailing all parties involved. The rule takes effect 90 days after posting to the Federal Register.

This follows allegations that a person linked to "Alice Huang" used shell companies to access [240,000 banned chips](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/dkaHxCdq-cs0POowU1dUbA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTctdGVjaHx1cy1jbG9zZXMtdGhlLXJlbW90ZS1hY2Nlc3MtbG9vcGhvbGUtb24tYWktY2hpcHN8fGh0dHBzOi8vYXJzdGVjaG5pY2EuY29tL3RlY2gtcG9saWN5LzIwMjYvMTEvdXMtbW92ZXMtdG8tY3V0LWNoaW5hcy1yZW1vdGUtYWNjZXNzLXRvLWFpLWNoaXBzLz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20). Trump downplayed the spying claims but expects no Chinese countermeasures. Experts warn this could accelerate China's shift to domestic chips, potentially harming US companies in the world's largest chip market. For anyone provisioning GPUs, this means the already complicated global supply chain just got another layer of paperwork and legal exposure.

Cornell researchers built [EchoMotion](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/Boscw4gxo_A6uxEsLkXbeA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTctdGVjaHxlY2hvbW90aW9uLXRyYWNrcy15b3VyLXdob2xlLWJvZHktd2l0aC1qdXN0LWEtc21hcnR3YXRjaHx8aHR0cHM6Ly9uZXdzLmNvcm5lbGwuZWR1L3N0b3JpZXMvMjAyNi8xMS9yZXNlYXJjaGVycy10cmFjay13aG9sZS1ib2R5LW1vdmVtZW50LWp1c3Qtc21hcnR3YXRjaD91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20), a system that tracks full-body pose using only a smartwatch's speaker and microphone via sonar-like acoustic sensing. It runs a lightweight AI model on off-the-shelf watches, preserving privacy by avoiding cameras. The paper was presented at UbiComp/IWSC on October 13.

This matters because it's a scalable, battery-efficient alternative to multi-device or camera-based motion tracking. The team is developing it for fitness tracking and exploring combining it with first-person vision on smart glasses to track objects a person handles. For wearables and VR engineers, this is a promising direction: no cameras, no extra hardware, just audio.

Proofs require no external review, unlike test bug reports which are human-reviewable. The post notes AI's role in scaling verification - Claude has written a billion lines of Rocq - and its economic value, but contends that "formally verified" systems can still have bugs.

The distinction matters for engineers assessing correctness claims: verifiable testing produces artifacts a human can inspect, while formal proofs are self-referential. That doesn't make them useless - formal verification produces well-defined, reviewable objects that are fairly reliable at finding bugs - but it means "formally verified" isn't a guarantee.
