<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/VdoKqio2u1TPBa8gNTaYrA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjMtdGVjaHx0aGUtMi1taWxsaW9uLWNhbmFyaWVzLWFpLXN1cHBseS1jaGFpbi1sZWFrcy1zZWNyZXRzLWxpa2UtYS1zaWV2ZXx8aHR0cHM6Ly9hcnN0ZWNobmljYS5jb20vc2VjdXJpdHkvMjAyNi8xMS9hLWxlYWt5LWFpLXN1cHBseS1jaGFpbi1pcy1leHBvc2luZy1zZWNyZXQtdXNlci1kYXRhLWF0LWFuLWFsYXJtaW5nLXJhdGUvP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: arstechnica.com ↗</a></p>

Researchers planted [2 million digital canaries](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/VdoKqio2u1TPBa8gNTaYrA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjMtdGVjaHx0aGUtMi1taWxsaW9uLWNhbmFyaWVzLWFpLXN1cHBseS1jaGFpbi1sZWFrcy1zZWNyZXRzLWxpa2UtYS1zaWV2ZXx8aHR0cHM6Ly9hcnN0ZWNobmljYS5jb20vc2VjdXJpdHkvMjAyNi8xMS9hLWxlYWt5LWFpLXN1cHBseS1jaGFpbi1pcy1leHBvc2luZy1zZWNyZXQtdXNlci1kYXRhLWF0LWFuLWFsYXJtaW5nLXJhdGUvP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) - unique secrets like AWS keys and URLs - in prompts sent through AI routers like OpenRouter and Vercel. The result: the ecosystem leaked highly sensitive data at an alarming rate.

OpenRouter has since notified customers that some prompts may have been read, citing potential exposure involving prompts sent to MiniMax, a China-based model provider. The researcher, HD Moore, controlled the infrastructure that received any requests using the credentials, allowing him to identify each link in the chain responsible for the leak.

This is the story that should scare every engineer who sends sensitive data through AI routers. The supply chain is leaky, and it's not just theoretical - real secrets were exposed. If you're putting proprietary code, customer data, or credentials into prompts, assume they're readable by intermediaries you don't control.

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/P5UJ9P6IkdVW_7IW6sOycA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjMtdGVjaHx3aHktY2hpbmEtd29uLWFpLXZpZGVvfHxodHRwczovL21hbmFzc2Fsb2kuY29tLzIwMjYvMTEvMjEvd2h5LWNoaW5hLXdvbi1haS12aWRlby5odG1sP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: manassaloi.com ↗</a></p>

[Manas Saloi argues](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/P5UJ9P6IkdVW_7IW6sOycA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjMtdGVjaHx3aHktY2hpbmEtd29uLWFpLXZpZGVvfHxodHRwczovL21hbmFzc2Fsb2kuY29tLzIwMjYvMTEvMjEvd2h5LWNoaW5hLXdvbi1haS12aWRlby5odG1sP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) that China has won the AI video race: over 95% of new micro dramas in China are now made with AI, and top models on Higgsfield are Chinese. His hypothesis: China approached video like manufacturing - shipping rough but good-enough products, selling volume, and using the feedback loop to improve faster.

The insight for engineers: video is different from code. A half-right clip still gets watched and provides data, unlike code which needs to be verified. That feedback loop is why Chinese models like Seedance and Kling are more widely used for actual production than US models like Veo, despite the US having better text models.

<h2 id="tufazip-compressing-a-billion-bytes-of-wikipedia"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/Np34anEKjks_n54OZTys7A/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjMtdGVjaHx0dWZhemlwLWNvbXByZXNzaW5nLWEtYmlsbGlvbi1ieXRlcy1vZi13aWtpcGVkaWF8fGh0dHBzOi8vdHVmYWxhYnMuYWkvcmVzZWFyY2gvdHVmYXppcC8_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">tufazip: Compressing a Billion Bytes of Wikipedia</a></h2>

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/Np34anEKjks_n54OZTys7A/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjMtdGVjaHx0dWZhemlwLWNvbXByZXNzaW5nLWEtYmlsbGlvbi1ieXRlcy1vZi13aWtpcGVkaWF8fGh0dHBzOi8vdHVmYWxhYnMuYWkvcmVzZWFyY2gvdHVmYXppcC8_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: tufalabs.ai ↗</a></p>

A researcher took #2 on the [Large Text Compression Benchmark](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/Np34anEKjks_n54OZTys7A/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjMtdGVjaHx0dWZhemlwLWNvbXByZXNzaW5nLWEtYmlsbGlvbi1ieXRlcy1vZi13aWtpcGVkaWF8fGh0dHBzOi8vdHVmYWxhYnMuYWkvcmVzZWFyY2gvdHVmYXppcC8_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t) with tufazip, compressing one billion bytes of Wikipedia into 105,388,353 bytes - the best result using a neural network as the sole prediction model. The system uses a 12-layer, 163M-parameter Transformer, initialized from a 34M-parameter offline model quantized to 4.33 MB.

The clever part: two offline-trained layers are copied into twelve independent layers that continue learning during compression. The decoder repeats the learning process, so the full model's learned weights never need to be transferred. This is a state-of-the-art demonstration of online learning for compression.
