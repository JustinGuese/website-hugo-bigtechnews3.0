The robots are not just writing your code anymore. They're deploying it. Fly.io reports that at least 55% of deployments to its platform now come from AI agents, and it has shipped new infrastructure to make that work without a human in the loop. If you run a platform, a SaaS product, or anything with an API, the question is no longer whether agents will show up at your door. It's whether you're ready when they do.

That's the through-line of today's newsletter: agents are moving from writing code to operating systems, buying things, and managing infrastructure. The companies that build for agent-led workflows are pulling ahead. The ones that don't are about to find out what it's like to be the bottleneck.

[Fly.io reports](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/-8Hdb3Ew1KLjYnhgoo9XyQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjYtZm91bmRlcnN8Zmx5aW8tb3BlbnMtdGhlLWRvb3ItZm9yLWFnZW50LWRyaXZlbi1kZXBsb3ltZW50c3x8aHR0cHM6Ly9mbHkuaW8vYmxvZy9ob2xkaW5nLXRoZS1kb29yLWZvci1hZ2VudHMvP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) that at least 55% of deployments to its platform are now performed by AI agents. That's not a pilot program or a beta feature. It's the majority of production traffic on a major cloud platform.

To support this shift, Fly.io now supports [auth.md](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/-8Hdb3Ew1KLjYnhgoo9XyQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjYtZm91bmRlcnN8Zmx5aW8tb3BlbnMtdGhlLWRvb3ItZm9yLWFnZW50LWRyaXZlbi1kZXBsb3ltZW50c3x8aHR0cHM6Ly9mbHkuaW8vYmxvZy9ob2xkaW5nLXRoZS1kb29yLWZvci1hZ2VudHMvP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ), an open spec for agent signup. Agents can register for accounts, obtain credentials valid for 24 hours with self-renewal, and manage apps, Machines, volumes, secrets, deploys, and Sprites. The credentials explicitly exclude billing or organization membership changes, and agents can request fixed credit purchases that a human approves in Stripe's Link app after reading the agent's stated reason.

The engineering takeaway: this is a real auth flow designed for non-human actors, not a hack. If you're building a platform, the question is whether your API keys and onboarding flow can handle an agent that shows up, wants credentials, and starts deploying without ever talking to a human. Fly.io just made that a competitive differentiator.

[The fifth wave of shopping has begun](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/cx8YDvE02YxccF1PTQX5Zg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjYtZm91bmRlcnN8eW91ci1uZXh0LWN1c3RvbWVyLXdpbGwtc2VuZC1hbi1hZ2VudHx8aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cubGF0ZWNoZWNrb3V0LmFnZW5jeS9ibG9nL3lvdXItbmV4dC1jdXN0b21lci13aWxsLXNlbmQtYW4tYWdlbnQ_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t), and this one removes the customer from the purchase process. The four previous waves took out a person or step each: self-service in 1916, the card in 1950, the internet in 1994, and the phone in 2007. The next wave takes out the customer entirely.

The evidence is already here. Stripe has integrated with Anthropic's Claude Duet agent, and Shopify, Walmart, Meta, and Google are all making moves in the same direction. Agents can now find products, sign in, and pay with single-use card numbers. That means your e-commerce checkout needs to handle a customer that doesn't have a browser session, doesn't type, and doesn't click "I agree to the terms."

For engineers, this changes the shape of authentication and payment flows. Single-use card numbers, agent-friendly identity, and APIs that don't assume a human on the other end are no longer nice-to-haves. They're the difference between being the platform agents can buy from and being the one they skip.

The median growth for companies under $10M ARR is now 900%, compared to the traditional T2D3 model of tripling. At $100M+ ARR, the median growth is 115%, close to the old bar of 100%. But the gap is widest at seed and Series A stages.

The reason: top growth funds are benchmarking against the fastest-growing AI-forward companies, not typical startups. The takeaway is brutal but clear: if you're growing 2x and calling it good, you're not in the conversation. The bar moved, and it moved because AI companies are growing at a pace that would have been absurd five years ago.

Google's AI Overviews cite YouTube in 44-70% of answers and Reddit in 35-63%. ChatGPT cited them 0% of the time. Perplexity cites about ten sources per answer; ChatGPT cites about four.

For anyone doing SEO, this is the data that matters. The pages that show up for your buyers' comparison questions depend entirely on which engine they're using. Brands named in at least half the answers had their own sites cited in a median 12% of answers. That means your website is often not the source that gets cited, even when your product is the answer.

The engineering implication: you can't optimize for one engine. The citation patterns are so different that a strategy that works for Google's AI Overviews does nothing for ChatGPT and vice versa. Check which comparison pages show up for your buyers' questions on each engine, because they're not the same.

[The fall fundraising window is now closed](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/L9XAR3-7q_Gje0pzpTsqjg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjYtZm91bmRlcnN8dGhlLWZhbGwtZnVuZHJhaXNpbmctd2luZG93LWlzLWNsb3NlZHx8aHR0cHM6Ly9jaHJpc25ldW1hbm4uY29tL2FyY2hpdmVzL3RoZS1mYWxsLWZ1bmRyYWlzaW5nLXdpbmRvdy1pcy1jbG9zZWQ_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t), as the fourth Wednesday of November marks the day when Silicon Valley VCs begin winding down for the year. The period between US Thanksgiving and Christmas is "fundraising purgatory," where momentum is lost and rounds often die.

The practical advice: if you hold a signed term sheet, push to close before year-end. If you have soft-circled angels and no lead, close those commitments now so you don't lose them. If you have no real investor traction, pause, improve the business, and restart in January. Casual intro calls in December can still build relationships for January.
