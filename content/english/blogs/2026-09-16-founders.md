<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/tIeoqglEwkU96ZFJjZZ7rQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMTYtZm91bmRlcnN8dGhlLWJhcmJlbGwtaXMtY29taW5nLWZvci15b3VyLXNvZnR3YXJlfHxodHRwczovL3guY29tL212ZXJuYWwvc3RhdHVzLzIwOTk4ODUxMzIzNzk1MDA1NjI_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: x.com ↗</a></p>

The internet killed the distribution monopoly, and the fallout is a market that splits into two extremes. [A new essay argues](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/tIeoqglEwkU96ZFJjZZ7rQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMTYtZm91bmRlcnN8dGhlLWJhcmJlbGwtaXMtY29taW5nLWZvci15b3VyLXNvZnR3YXJlfHxodHRwczovL3guY29tL212ZXJuYWwvc3RhdHVzLzIwOTk4ODUxMzIzNzk1MDA1NjI_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t) that the old model - where a few gatekeepers controlled the pipes - is dead, replaced by a "barbell" shape. On one end sit global giants like The New York Times, which grew from 1.1M to 13.4M subscribers. On the other end are niche solopreneurs like Lenny's Newsletter, who own a tiny, loyal audience outright. In the middle? Stranded.

The consequence for founders is brutal. If you're building a mid-sized point solution, you're competing against both a giant with infinite reinvestment capital and a solo operator with zero overhead. The essay's core claim: a software moat comes from replication cost, switching costs, and network effects - not from being first or having early momentum. If your product can be copied in a quarter, it was never defensible, no matter how fast you grew.

The plugin pulls meetings, deals closing soon, at-risk opportunities, and unread threads into a personalized daily brief. You can tell Claude to prep for a call and it will pull data from Salesforce, Slack, and email - then automatically add stakeholders it finds in threads as contacts if they're missing.

It ships with 38 skills covering the daily grind: account research, call prep, and pipeline analysis. The interesting part for engineers is what this replaces - this isn't a chat widget bolted onto your CRM. It's a workflow engine that writes back to Salesforce. For teams running lean SDR operations, this collapses the loop between research and action. If your sales team is still tab-switching between Slack and Salesforce, this is the kind of tool that makes the old workflow feel like a punishment.

[Linear's new Loops feature](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/lq6_kwDABbD1b6gifjq61A/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMTYtZm91bmRlcnN8bGluZWFyLWxvb3BzLXR1cm5zLXByb2R1Y3QtbWFuYWdlbWVudC1pbnRvLWEtcnVsZXMtZW5naW5lfHxodHRwczovL2xpbmVhci5hcHAvY2hhbmdlbG9nLzIwMjYtMDktMTQtbG9vcHMtZm9yLXByb2R1Y3QtbWFuYWdlbWVudD91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) automates the busywork that eats PM hours. A loop can watch for a target date shift, update the launch plan, and post a Slack message explaining what changed and why - no human in the loop. Triggers fire on changes to status, ownership, target dates, milestones, membership, labels, and new comments for projects; issues add priority, assignee, and cycle to the list.

For SREs and engineers, this is the missing glue between your issue tracker and your communication tools. The promise is that information never falls out of sync because the system does the syncing. The risk is a new class of alert fatigue if teams configure loops badly - a loop that posts every status change will drown out the one that matters. Start narrow, trigger on the events that actually need human attention, and let the routine stuff fire unattended.

[One founder's deep dive into messy cap tables](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/pxFYR01a7C7EJjb8XZtnQA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMTYtZm91bmRlcnN8dGhlLXNhZmUtY2FwLXRhYmxlLXByb2JsZW0taXMtYS10aW1lLWJvbWJ8fGh0dHBzOi8vd3d3LmRhbmhvY2suY28vcC9tb21lbnR1bS1pcy1ub3QtYS1tb2F0P3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) confirms what many suspect: the problem isn't the SAFE document, it's the accumulation. Issuing SAFEs at different caps and terms defers the pricing conversation, but creates hidden liabilities. When a priced round finally happens, there isn't enough room for everyone, and investors who took notes with a due date become creditors if no round closes in time.

The author's advice is pragmatic: treat every SAFE like a debt instrument with a ticking clock, because that's what it becomes. For founders who've raised a rolling SAFE, the math on dilution is unknowable until the priced round. The takeaway isn't to avoid SAFEs - it's to know what you're signing. If you can't model the outcome of every cap and discount in a spreadsheet, neither can your investors.
