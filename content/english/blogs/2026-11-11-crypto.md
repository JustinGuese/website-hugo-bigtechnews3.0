The New York Stock Exchange selected [Avalanche](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/_NKBs3sTTTkithb3tOPafw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTEtY3J5cHRvfG55c2UtcGlja3MtYXZhbGFuY2hlLWZvci0yNDctdG9rZW5pemVkLXN0b2NrLXNldHRsZW1lbnR8fGh0dHBzOi8vdGhyZWFkcmVhZGVyYXBwLmNvbS90aHJlYWQvMjExOTc3OTE5MzE3Mjk5MjAzMC5odG1sP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) as the exclusive settlement blockchain for a new 24/7 tokenized US equities trading platform. NYSE cited Avalanche's transaction speed, throughput, and compliance capabilities as key selection criteria. The platform targets a Q4 launch pending regulatory approval, and will offer instant settlement with stablecoin-based funding.

This is not a pilot or a proof of concept. This is the largest US stock exchange building its equity tokenization infrastructure on a public layer-1, not a permissioned consortium chain. For engineers, the interesting part is the settlement model: trades that currently take T+1 or T+2 to clear will settle instantly, 24/7, with stablecoins as the funding rail. The selection places AVAX at the core of NYSE's equity tokenization infrastructure, marking a direct integration between the US' largest stock exchange and a public layer-1 network for securities settlement.

The compliance angle matters too. Avalanche's subnet architecture lets NYSE run validators it controls while keeping the public chain's security, which is likely what made this palatable to regulators. If this launches on schedule, it becomes the reference architecture for every other exchange considering blockchain settlement.

The deal adds warehouse financing capabilities, allowing clients to borrow against pooled loans ahead of refinancing. The ABF market is projected to exceed $9 trillion by 2029 per KKR.

Q3 results released alongside the announcement showed revenue of $18.6 million (+13% YoY), average tokenized assets of $5 billion (+39%), and transaction volume of $4.3 billion (+20%), with $300 million in cash and no debt on the balance sheet. Warehouse financing is the technical piece worth understanding. It lets a client borrow against a pool of loans before those loans are refinanced or securitized. Tokenizing that process onchain means the collateral, the borrowing, and the repayment all live in the same system. For institutional clients, that collapses what is normally a multi-week process into something that can settle in hours.

A [payments industry analysis](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/nMVn598UuWiO-Rs0oowqyQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTEtY3J5cHRvfHlvdXItcGF5bWVudC1wcm9jZXNzb3ItaGFzLWEtcGF5bWVudC1wcm9jZXNzb3J8fGh0dHBzOi8veC5jb20vYWxheGljL3N0YXR1cy8yMTE5ODk4Mjk5MjIyNDQyMjQ5P3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) examines the merchant-of-record (MoR) model, highlighting that MoRs like Whop, Commas, and Dodo Payments do not explicitly name their underlying payment processors or acquiring banks in their terms. This creates a chain where merchants lack direct contractual relationships with entities that can freeze funds or shut down accounts.

The concrete numbers are stark. Terms allow holds of 180 days or longer. Whop reserves up to 100% for up to 180 days. Commas calls some risk decisions final. Other sellers' fraud or disputes can tighten rules for everyone on the platform. A decision made several layers up can freeze a merchant who has no direct relationship with the decision-maker.

The MoR model solves onboarding and compliance problems, but it shifts complexity and risk to an invisible layer. If you are a merchant, ask who processes payments, how risk is assessed, what the reserve terms are, who can release funds, and plan for a long hold. The analysis argues that engineers should understand the full payment stack's dependencies and failure points, because the entity that can freeze your funds is not the entity you signed up with.
