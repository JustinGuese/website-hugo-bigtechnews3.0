<h2 id="the-nvidia-circus-is-governance-risk-not-a-catalyst">The Nvidia circus is governance risk, not a catalyst</h2>

The tape says relief: [Jensen Huang was reinstated as Nvidia CEO on November 23](#) after the board, under direct pressure from the President, reversed his removal - and the stock added over $3 trillion, up more than 30%. Read the mechanics instead. A board that can be overruled by the White House on its single most important decision has lost the property that governance exists to provide. Huang then told an all-hands he is in daily therapy and that the board will hold him accountable. None of this touches Hopper or Rubin roadmaps; all of it touches who controls capital allocation at a company whose customer concentration is already the industry's biggest single point of failure. We have been negative here for over a week and nothing today changes that - the rally is exactly the kind of event-driven bid that fades once the headline stops printing.

<h2 id="ai-agents-are-now-the-attacker">AI agents are now the attacker</h2>

Two independent data points, same day, from two different labs. [Google Threat Intelligence Group](#) reported the first observed campaign of AI autonomously finding and exploiting flaws: 11 organizations compromised in 26 seconds, domain admin in 7 minutes. Separately, [Amazon](#) disclosed that agents on its open-source Cairn framework found and exploited a zero-day in a medical imaging app, taking full control of a university server with minimal human input. The second-order read: the bottleneck in offensive security was always the human exploit-chain engineer, and that bottleneck is gone. The obvious beneficiaries are the endpoint and identity vendors - but note who actually shipped a structural answer today: OpenAI and Cisco's [Shirudo](#), an open-source eBPF runtime that blocks exploits during the patch gap, demonstrated stopping Java4Shell against Elasticsearch via a Codex-generated shield, no restart. Patch-gap defense has been an academic idea for a decade; a working open-source implementation commoditizes one wedge of the security stack. That is a modest positive for Cisco's narrative and a slow pressure on anyone selling detection-after-exploitation. Amazon's zero-day disclosure doubles as marketing for Cairn, which tells you where AWS thinks the security conversation is going.

<h2 id="agent-plumbing-consolidates-around-mcp">Agent plumbing consolidates around MCP</h2>

Anthropic moved Skills over MCP to public beta and took Tasks and Elicitation GA, letting MCP servers ship skills and run long-running background work with lower context overhead. Google answered the same day with Workspace MCP servers and the Workspace CLI at GA - agents wired into Gmail, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Calendar and Chat with admin controls. Two labs converging on MCP as the substrate is the real signal: the protocol war is settling, and the fight moves to who owns the agent's identity and memory. OpenAI's hidden Email button for ChatGPT Dots - dedicated inboxes for agents - is the tell. An agent with its own email address is an agent with a persistent external identity, which is the prerequisite for anything enterprises will sign off on. Meta pushing Muse into Instagram DMs across five countries is the consumer flank of the same build-out.

<h2 id="reliability-day-cloudflare-and-microsoft-both-blinked">Reliability day: Cloudflare and Microsoft both blinked</h2>

[Cloudflare extended its Flamingo framework](#) to probe DNS propagation from every data center against production - hundreds of millions of tests daily, releases gated on results - after a scheduling bug left record changes unpublished for hours. For a company whose franchise is being the authoritative DNS layer, a silent propagation failure is the worst class of bug: customers don't see errors, they see staleness. The fix is the right fix, but it arrives after the trust event. Microsoft's 365 outage took Outlook, Teams and Copilot down together and exposed a design pattern enterprises should internalize: locally installed apps that are thin clients over SaaS die with the data center, while genuinely on-premises systems stay up. That is a procurement argument against full-thickness Microsoft dependency, and it is the kind of argument that shows up in renewal conversations a year later, not in tomorrow's tape.

<h2 id="thin-corners">Thin corners</h2>

Amazon's $7.5 billion Decart acquisition targets Trainium performance via the Optimization Stack - read it as AWS buying time against Nvidia rather than a leapfrog; compiler-level optimization closes single-digit-percent gaps, not architectural ones. The VP≠VNP proofs from Anthropic and OpenAI are genuinely interesting complexity theory with Lean certification, and Amazon's companion result that ML compiler layout selection is NP-hard (63.9M vs 20.9M cycles for exact versus heuristic on ResNet-50) quantifies why heuristics persist. None of it moves a P&L this quarter. Today was a two-story day - Nvidia's governance and autonomous offensive AI - with a long tail of agent plumbing underneath.
