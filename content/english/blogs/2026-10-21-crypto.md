<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/wzzQFaoOTYEEd2oUC9a7YQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMjEtY3J5cHRvfHZpdGFsaWtzLWVpcC04Mjg4LW1heGltYWxpc20taGFsdGluZy10aGUtZXZtLXByZWNvbXBpbGUtY3JlZXB8fGh0dHBzOi8vdGhyZWFkcmVhZGVyYXBwLmNvbS90aHJlYWQvMjExMjAzMjAwOTQxMzQzOTcwNC5odG1sP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: threadreaderapp.com ↗</a></p>

Ethereum core developers have spent years debating which cryptographic primitives deserve hardcoded state transition shortcuts. Vitalik Buterin published an argument for [EIP-8288 maximalism](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/wzzQFaoOTYEEd2oUC9a7YQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMjEtY3J5cHRvfHZpdGFsaWtzLWVpcC04Mjg4LW1heGltYWxpc20taGFsdGluZy10aGUtZXZtLXByZWNvbXBpbGUtY3JlZXB8fGh0dHBzOi8vdGhyZWFkcmVhZGVyYXBwLmNvbS90aHJlYWQvMjExMjAzMjAwOTQxMzQzOTcwNC5odG1sP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ), advocating that the protocol stop adding new EVM precompiles altogether. 

Precompiles were originally introduced to let gas-intensive cryptographic operations - like elliptic curve pairings and hash functions - run at native machine speeds rather than choking on EVM bytecode interpreter overhead. The core problem is data availability bottlenecks: routing raw inputs through calldata to reach precompiles caps protocol throughput at roughly 8 MB/s, even with PeerDAS operational. 

Under the EIP-8288 design pattern, developers execute arbitrary cryptography entirely client-side, produce a STARK proof over the execution trace, and submit only the proof through a standardized recursive aggregation mechanism. The base layer verifies the proof and logs state transitions without ever touching or storing the underlying parameters. If adopted broadly, client implementations could freeze the EVM's precompile attack surface permanently, decoupling layer-1 consensus upgrades from the developer community's evolving appetite for non-standard signature schemes.

A research team has published an architecture on [ethresear.ch porting Tornado Cash-style privacy pools](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/zqqkdf2tQySxrskq3l4glw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMjEtY3J5cHRvfHBvc3QtcXVhbnR1bS1wcml2YWN5LXBvb2xzLWJpbmFyeS1maWVsZHMtYW5kLXN1Yi0zMG1zLXByb3ZlcnN8fGh0dHBzOi8vZXRocmVzZWFyLmNoL3QvZmFzdGVyLWFnZ3JlZ2F0YWJsZS1hbmQtcXVhbnR1bS1zYWZlLXByaXZhY3ktcG9vbHMvMjYxNjM_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t) to Flock, a post-quantum, binary-field SNARK system. Existing private balance architectures depend heavily on Pedersen commitments and Groth16 zk-SNARKs, both of which rely on discrete-log assumptions that collapse under Shor's algorithm.

```
Traditional Pool (Groth16 / Pedersen)
  [Secret + Nullifier] -> Poseidon / Pedersen Hash -> Discrete-Log Proof (~800ms)
                                                             |
                                                       Quantum Breakable
Flock Architecture (Binary Fields)
  [Secret + Nullifier] -> SHA-256 Compression x34  -> Sumcheck Glue -> Proof (28.6ms)
                                                             |
                                                       Quantum-Resistant
```

The system replaces algebraic group operations entirely with standard SHA-256 for Merkle tree paths and nullifier derivations. Because running SHA-256 inside standard arithmetic circuits typically balloons constraint counts, the Flock implementation uses a single-compression circuit executed 34 times across a multiplexer, bound together by a sumcheck glue argument. 

The performance shift is drastic:
* **Prover Latency**: Depth-32 tree membership proofs drop from 810 milliseconds down to 28.6 milliseconds, producing a 266 KiB single proof.
* **Proof Aggregation**: An aggregation layer enables local user-side proving in ~19.5ms, folding *N* transactions into an aggregated ~344 KiB payload that verifies onchain in 46 to 64ms regardless of batch size.
* **Emergency Mitigation**: The proposal integrates a ZIP 2005-inspired post-quantum recovery fallback. If an adversary demonstrates a quantum discrete-log break, the smart contract freezes, allowing users to withdraw liquidity through an alternate verification pathway keyed directly from their original 256-bit entropy seeds.

AnchorWatch is capping monthly administrative billing at a fixed $10,000, insulating enterprise balance sheets from fee inflation as the dollar value of the underlying bitcoin fluctuates.

The custodian is offering two infrastructure setups:
* **Flagship Multi-Sig**: AnchorWatch holds quorum-qualifying keys while the client retains complementary key control, establishing a compliant primary-signoff workflow.
* **Distributed Quorum**: Key shards are partitioned across AnchorWatch, BitGo inside the US, and CoinCorner in the Isle of Man, mitigating single-jurisdiction regulatory exposure.

To backstop catastrophic risk, AnchorWatch secured policies through Lloyd's of London syndicates providing up to $370 million in dedicated insurance per individual client. Unlike pooled omnibus policies where claims compete against an aggregate ceiling, these policies are underwritten in the customer's legal name, specifically covering insider fraud, violent coercion, and physical extortion.
