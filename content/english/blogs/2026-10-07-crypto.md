FinCEN is walking back two of the most aggressive surveillance expansions ever proposed for on-chain finance. The Treasury bureau formally withdrew both its 2023 proposal to label all international crypto mixing a "primary money laundering concern" under Section 311 of the USA PATRIOT Act and its 2020 rule targeting self-hosted wallets.

The shift removes the immediate threat of reporting mandates that would have treated ordinary non-custodial software like suspicious offshore shell banks. FinCEN acknowledged industry pushback warning that sweeping mixing rules would crush legitimate user privacy, citing a July 2025 President's Working Group report noting legal use cases for transactional shielding.

For developers shipping custody systems, smart contracts, and consumer wallets, the regulatory retreat clarifies operating bounds - even as enterprise platforms consolidate stacks elsewhere.

> FinCEN withdrew its 2020 proposal requiring banks and money services businesses to verify identities and keep records on self-hosted wallet transactions above $3,000.

The [withdrawal from FinCEN](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/CEpbmCpUSkM8BXjm_S_RWQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMDctY3J5cHRvfGZpbmNlbi1kcm9wcy1taXhlci1kZXNpZ25hdGlvbnMtYW5kLXVuaG9zdGVkLXdhbGxldC1yZWNvcmRrZWVwaW5nfHxodHRwczovL3d3dy50aGVibG9jay5jby9uZXdzL3JlZ3VsYXRpb24vMjAyNi0xMC0wNS1maW5jZW4tZHJvcHMtY3J5cHRvLW1peGluZy1ydWxlLXNlbGYtaG9zdGVkLXdhbGxldC1wcm9wb3NhbC00MTc2OTA_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t) cancels two rulemakings that hung over infrastructure teams for years. The first was the October 2023 proposal under Section 311 that would have required domestic financial institutions to implement sweeping recordkeeping and reporting on any transaction touching an international mixing service. The second was the controversial December 2020 midnight rulemaking that aimed to force money service businesses (MSBs) to verify ownership and log counterparty identity for any transaction to or from a self-hosted wallet exceeding $3,000.

Because neither rule was ever finalized into an administrative order, production compliance obligations do not change: existing Bank Secrecy Act and Travel Rule thresholds remain right where they were. What changes is the architectural risk profile. Had the mixer designation passed, any protocol using zero-knowledge pools or batching relays risked being classified as a laundering pipeline, exposing integrating endpoints to severe liability. FinCEN stated it will continue monitoring illicit flows, but its explicit citation of legitimate privacy protections suggests enforcement will focus on actual threat actors rather than protocols by default.

Polymarket has launched [Protocol V2](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/JHcnaqhWSJU4wwEJC6u1-w/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMDctY3J5cHRvfHBvbHltYXJrZXQtcHJvdG9jb2wtdjItdW5pZmllcy1wb3NpdGlvbnMtb24tZXJjLTExNTV8fGh0dHBzOi8veC5jb20vMHhyYWphdGgvc3RhdHVzLzIxMDcxMjI1NDEyMTkyMDUzNDQ_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t), ditching its legacy architecture built on the 2019 Gnosis Conditional Tokens Framework (CTF). The original setup had become deeply fragmented, requiring bespoke contract layers, wrapping steps, and state tracking whenever new trading mechanics were added.

```
V1 Architecture:
[User] -> [CTF Wrapper] -> [Multiple Collateral Contracts] -> [Scattered Orderbooks]

V2 Architecture:
[User] -> [Universal Router] -> [ERC-1155 Position Token] -> [OracleAggregator]
                                \_ [Unified pUSD Collateral]   \_ (UMA / Chainlink)
```

The new core architecture consolidates the state footprint:
* A single ERC-1155 token handles all market positions, allowing token IDs to pack internal market data that can be parsed by an on-chain router.
* A single collateral contract (`pUSD`) backs settlements across every book.
* One exchange contract serves each market class, spanning binary, atomic negative-risk, incremental negative-risk, and combinatorial engines.
* Resolution routes through an `OracleAggregator` with swappable modules capable of handling UMA, Chainlink, or custom feeds.

Protocol V2 natively supports cross-chain bridging of collateral, positions, and resolutions. Audited by multiple security firms, the deployment is backed by a $5 million bug bounty.

The Zcash network activated its [NU7 upgrade on the public testnet](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/poCoB1sjfykaOeJ8yufKvw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMDctY3J5cHRvfHpjYXNoLW51Ny1icmluZ3MtMjUtc2Vjb25kLWJsb2NrLXRpbWVzLXRvLXRlc3RuZXR8fGh0dHBzOi8vd3d3LmNvaW5kZXNrLmNvbS90ZWNoLzIwMjYvMTAvMDUvemNhc2gtcy0yNS1zZWNvbmQtYmxvY2tzLWdvLWxpdmUtb24tcHVibGljLXRlc3RuZXQtYWhlYWQtb2Ytc2NoZWR1bGU_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t) at block 4,465,026, cutting target block times from 75 seconds down to 25 seconds. The shift drops standard three-confirmation finality latency from 225 seconds down to 75 seconds, materially reducing wait states for exchange deposits and cross-chain messaging.

To prevent the accelerated chain from altering the programmatic ZEC emission curve, NU7 reduces per-block mining rewards to exactly one-third of their prior value. The fee allocation model is also revamped: 60% of all transaction fees are diverted to a long-term mining reserve to support security spend post-halving, while 40% goes directly to the block producer. NU7 completely deprecates Sprout, Zcash’s original 2016 zero-knowledge pool. Node operators running Zakura or Zebra clients must migrate balances and confirm compatibility ahead of a scheduled November 5 mainnet rollout, with developer signoff targeted for October 20.
