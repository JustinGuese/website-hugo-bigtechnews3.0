[Simon Willison reports](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/AHynLNLI8P96ZJhxodoyfA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMDgtdGVjaHxhbnRocm9waWMtbGF1bmNoZXMtY2xhdWRlLWhhaWt1LTU1LXdpdGgtYS01eC1jb250ZXh0LXN1cmNoYXJnZXx8aHR0cHM6Ly9zaW1vbndpbGxpc29uLm5ldC8yMDI2L09jdC83L2NsYXVkZS1oYWlrdS01LTUvP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) that Anthropic has released Claude Haiku 5.5. The baseline pricing matches OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna: $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens. The catch sits at the 100,000-token boundary. The moment a context payload crosses 100k tokens, the unit rate jumps to $0.50 input and $2.50 output per million tokens.

The model enforces reasoning traces and defaults to medium effort, meaning developers cannot run pure zero-thought completion calls to shave milliseconds. It also ships with a fresh tokenizer that consumes roughly 1.25x more tokens than Haiku 4.5 for the same text payloads. If you feed large documents into Haiku 5.5, the combined token expansion and the 5x pricing penalty mean your invoice can jump by roughly six times what you budgeted on small test calls. 

For architectures relying on prompt caching, Anthropic cut Sonnet 5.5 cache read prices in half and broadened account-level API credit ceilings. If your production pipeline pumps large document context into an agent, Luna or cached Sonnet 5.5 remains more predictable; keep Haiku 5.5 strictly quarantined behind a 100k-token cutoff filter.

Research groups in Austria and China have independently built the world's [first operational nuclear clocks](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/9MjaGqO1HBIa9VX-0v9ASw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMDgtdGVjaHx0aG9yaXVtLTIyOS1udWNsZWFyLWNsb2Nrcy1nby1saXZlLWluLXZpZW5uYS1hbmQtYmVpamluZ3x8aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cubnl0aW1lcy5jb20vMjAyNi8xMC8wNy9zY2llbmNlL2ZpcnN0LW51Y2xlYXItY2xvY2tzLXRob3JpdW0tMjI5Lmh0bWw_dW5sb2NrZWRfYXJ0aWNsZV9jb2RlPTEuSEZFLjFpVXkuTm9IZE9BbWVBTUFMJnNtaWQ9dXJsLXNoYXJlJnV0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ), moving timekeeping precision from atomic electron clouds directly to the atomic nucleus. By tracking the ultraviolet transitions of thorium-229 nuclei, the new instruments hit drift tolerances of roughly one second every few million years.

Because atomic nuclei are packed thousands of times tighter than orbital electron shells, nuclear clocks are largely immune to stray magnetic fields, thermal fluctuations, and stray radio emissions that degrade traditional optical lattice systems. Outside of metrology and ultra-precise network synchronization, these instruments double as physical sensors: a passing dark matter candidate or variation in fundamental physical constants will register immediately as a frequency wobble in the thorium tick.
