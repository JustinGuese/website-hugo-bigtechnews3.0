<h2 id="googles-two-front-squeeze">Google&#x27;s two-front squeeze</h2>

The AI Overviews expansion - dynamically expanded answers that take over the whole SERP for some queries - is a direct traffic hit to publisher and SEO-dependent businesses, and an indirect reinforcement of Google's monetisation lead against search competitors: if the SERP is the answer, the only ads that matter are Google's. Paired with that, Vycari founder Allen Hutchison's account of running agentic work at "Corolla" economics - 150M tokens for roughly $40, mostly on Gemini Flash Lite with slices of Claude Haiku and GPT-5.6 Luna - is micro-scale but a real-world confirmation of the pattern we flagged in the Datadog cheaper-model story: routine agent workloads go to the cheapest capable model, and the cheapest capable model is increasingly Google's. The Chrome MV2 purge (uBlock Origin gone, Brave self-hosting four extensions to keep them alive) is a smaller flex of the same muscle - distribution control over the browser layer.

<h2 id="nvidia-underwrites-anthropics-scaling">Nvidia underwrites Anthropic&#x27;s scaling</h2>

The $35B cloud deal for Anthropic, backed by Nvidia capital, is material relative to either company's recent deal flow, and it is the second day running that the "circular money is load-bearing" thesis has looked less like a criticism and more like the mechanism. Nvidia is now tying its balance sheet directly to a customer's compute scaling - the chip vendor has stopped waiting for cloud counterparty capex cycles and started creating them. This is consistent with our view that the moat has moved up the stack; we keep the positive call. The Cursor-loses-OpenAI-models-in-November item is the smaller version of the same consolidation: OpenAI restricting third-party distribution to protect its own products shifts Cursor's mix toward Anthropic and Google, which validates the suppliers and tells you OpenAI values its direct product channel more than raw API volume.

<h2 id="amazons-ad-business-in-the-crosshairs">Amazon&#x27;s ad business in the crosshairs</h2>

The FTC suit alleging Amazon deceived advertisers and inflated ad prices targets its dominant search-ad business - the highest-margin line it has, and the piece of the P&L that has been carrying the retail multiple expansion. The named company is Amazon, but the second-order risk sits with ad-tech partners routed through its marketplace, who face both the discovery cost of the suit and the pricing pressure if the alleged inflation gets unwound. We move to negative; this is a new fact, not a rehash, and it attacks the part of the story that was actually working.

<h2 id="microsofts-bad-day-googles-timing">Microsoft&#x27;s bad day, Google&#x27;s timing</h2>

An Exchange Online outage causing email and auth failures is an operational event, but it is a recurring one, and recurrence is what converts an outage into a narrative. Google launching OneDrive data import for Workspace migrations the same day is either exquisite timing or a competitive tool that just found its pitch deck. We keep the negative on Microsoft: the reliability story keeps the Workspace-migration door open, and today Google walked through it with a feature in hand.
