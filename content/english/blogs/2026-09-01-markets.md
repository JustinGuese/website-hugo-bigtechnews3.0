Two genuinely new facts hit today, and the rest is continuation. The new facts: Tim Cook is done, effective immediately, with John Ternus - the hardware engineering SVP - taking over a $391bn-revenue company; and the FTC filed a fresh suit against Amazon alleging advertiser deception on placement costs plus monopoly leverage over sellers. Everything else - the MV2 purge, AI Overviews expansion, the Apple trade-secrets case - is the latest installment of narratives we have been tracking for weeks.

> The second-order winner in the ad-blocking purge is not a listed name at all - Brave is private - which means the market has no clean way to price user defection, only Google's own traffic data.

<h2 id="apple-the-succession-is-the-story">Apple: the succession is the story</h2>

Ternus inheriting the hardware roadmap is the cleanest possible continuity signal - our archive already had "Apple hands the keys to a hardware guy" queued up, so the direction of travel was visible. The risk is not the person, it's the timing: immediate-effect transitions at companies this size rarely go as smoothly as the memo suggests, and the foldable iPhone and robotics programs Ternus now owns are exactly the kind of long-cycle bets that get re-scoped under a new CEO. The trade-secrets suit against former engineer Chang Liu is the quieter item with the larger tail: Apple claims to have found evidence on his MacBook linking him to OpenAI work that used a downloaded Apple circuit schematic via an AI agent. If that holds up, the remediation burden lands on OpenAI - a private-company counterparty - but it also tells you Apple believes its silicon IP is being exfiltrated through agentic tooling, which will shape how restrictive Apple gets with on-device agent access going forward.

<h2 id="the-compute-layer-anthropics-35bn">The compute layer: Anthropic&#x27;s $35bn</h2>

The Anthropic deal is the day's clearest read-through for the book. Nvidia backing a $35bn multi-year commitment to secure Claude training and deployment capacity is the circular-financing structure we flagged on 8/28 ("Nvidia's circular money is load-bearing") getting another load-bearing wall added. Amazon and Google participate as Anthropic investors and likely hosting partners, which means the hyperscalers eat part of the benefit too. We keep our overwhelmingly-positive on NVDA; nothing in today's material dents the demand-lock thesis, and this adds to it.

<h2 id="amazon-a-new-filing-not-a-rehash">Amazon: a new filing, not a rehash</h2>

The FTC suit is new legal exposure, distinct from the antitrust coverage Amazon has carried for years. The specific allegation - deceiving advertisers about placement costs - is the more dangerous one, because advertising is Amazon's highest-margin line and advertiser trust is the asset. Structural remedies against the ad business would move the multiple far more than seller-side remedies, which the market has largely learned to shrug at. We shift AMZN from the positive we held on 8/28 to negative: the filing itself is the change, and it lands on an already-extended regulatory docket.

<h2 id="google-the-open-web-gets-narrower">Google: the open web gets narrower</h2>

MV2 removal is complete, uBlock Origin is gone from the Web Store, and Brave is self-hosting four popular MV2 extensions as its differentiation play. Meanwhile Google confirmed testing AI Overviews that expand to fill the entire results page for definitional queries. These are the same story from two directions: Google is compressing the space between a query and its own monetized answer, and shrinking the room for anyone else - publishers, extension developers, rival browsers. The second-order observation is that the clearest beneficiary of the ad-blocking purge, Brave, is private, so the market cannot express this trade directly. For GOOGL the near-term revenue logic actually favors the change - full-page AI Overviews are ad inventory Google controls end to end - which is why we hold our positive rather than join the CTR-erosion bears, though we acknowledge the long-term query-volume risk if users learn the results page has less on it.

<h2 id="microsoft-nothing-to-change">Microsoft: nothing to change</h2>

We held negative on MSFT yesterday on agent-cloud value drift, and today's Anthropic deal - compute committed outside Microsoft's stack, with Nvidia and the hyperscaler-investors capturing the economics - reinforces rather than reverses that view. Conviction stays modest; it's a continuation signal, not a new fact.

A busy day, and the two things that actually move numbers are the Apple handoff and the Amazon filing. The Anthropic deal matters, but it confirms a thesis we already held rather than starting a new one.
