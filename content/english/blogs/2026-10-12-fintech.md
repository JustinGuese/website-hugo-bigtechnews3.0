<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/DRAYyr8pLnh5ea60lJOIaw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMTItZmludGVjaHx0aGUtbmZsLXRha2VzLWV2ZW50LWNvbnRyYWN0cy10by10aGUtc3VwcmVtZS1jb3VydHx8aHR0cHM6Ly91bmNoYWluZWRjcnlwdG8uY29tL25mbC10ZWxscy1zdXByZW1lLWNvdXJ0LXByZWRpY3Rpb24tbWFya2V0LXNwb3J0cy1jb250cmFjdHMtYXJlLWdhbWJsaW5nLW5vdC1zd2Fwcy8_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: unchainedcrypto.com ↗</a></p>

The National Football League has formally entered the regulatory dogfight surrounding prediction markets. In an amicus brief filed with the US Supreme Court in [*Flaherty v. KalshiEX*](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/DRAYyr8pLnh5ea60lJOIaw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMTItZmludGVjaHx0aGUtbmZsLXRha2VzLWV2ZW50LWNvbnRyYWN0cy10by10aGUtc3VwcmVtZS1jb3VydHx8aHR0cHM6Ly91bmNoYWluZWRjcnlwdG8uY29tL25mbC10ZWxscy1zdXByZW1lLWNvdXJ0LXByZWRpY3Rpb24tbWFya2V0LXNwb3J0cy1jb250cmFjdHMtYXJlLWdhbWJsaW5nLW5vdC1zd2Fwcy8_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t), the league argued that binary event contracts settled on athletic performance are illegal gambling instruments, not financial swaps protected by the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) under the Dodd-Frank Act.

The NFL backed its filing by highlighting market volume: on a single football Sunday, $1.8 billion of the total $3.3 billion traded across public prediction venues was tied directly to NFL matchups. The league argued that classifying sports bets as CFTC-cleared swaps bypasses critical state-level age gates and consumer protections while creating insider trading vectors that federal derivatives rules were never drafted to police. The Supreme Court's ruling will determine whether developers building event-contract platforms can operate under a unified federal derivatives framework or face a fractured, state-by-state gaming licensing regime.

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/J8jIPgf_YkXrrmZfHt8hAQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMTItZmludGVjaHxyZWd1bGF0b3JzLWhpdC1hbWVyaWNhbi1leHByZXNzLXdpdGgtYS0zNTBtLWFtbC1wZW5hbHR5fHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5wYXltZW50c2RpdmUuY29tL25ld3MvYW1leC1kaW5nZWQtYnktZmVkLW9jYy1tdXN0LXBheS0zNTBtLWZvci1hbWwtZmFpbHVyZXMvODMyNTc1Lz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: paymentsdive.com ↗</a></p>

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) and the Federal Reserve hit American Express National Bank with a [$350 million regulatory penalty](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/J8jIPgf_YkXrrmZfHt8hAQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMTItZmludGVjaHxyZWd1bGF0b3JzLWhpdC1hbWVyaWNhbi1leHByZXNzLXdpdGgtYS0zNTBtLWFtbC1wZW5hbHR5fHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5wYXltZW50c2RpdmUuY29tL25ld3MvYW1leC1kaW5nZWQtYnktZmVkLW9jYy1tdXN0LXBheS0zNTBtLWZvci1hbWwtZmFpbHVyZXMvODMyNTc1Lz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) over systemic failures inside its Anti-Money Laundering (AML) monitoring systems. The OCC found pervasive blind spots in Amex’s suspicious activity tracking that allowed an estimated $13 billion in illicit transaction flow to circulate unflagged across a ten-year span.

Alongside the financial fine, the Federal Reserve served Amex an enterprise-wide cease-and-desist order mandating an immediate rebuild of its transaction monitoring, customer due diligence, and risk governance platforms. Amex stated the penalty will not impact its 2026 or 2027 corporate earnings targets. For financial systems architects, the consent order shows the regulatory risk of technical debt inside compliance stacks: batch rules engines and delayed transaction auditing inevitably break down when scaled across high-volume card portfolios.
