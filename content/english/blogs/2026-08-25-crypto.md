## Circle: $140 target, Arc mainnet in three weeks

Circle had a 24-hour news cycle that would normally take a quarter. [Bernstein reiterated Outperform and a $140 target](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-08-24-bernstein-says-circles-growth-cycle-can-continue-without-the-clarity-act-sees-59-upside-412573) (59% upside from Friday's $87.98 close), arguing the company's growth is decoupled from the Clarity Act. The trigger: USDC supply added $1.7B in a single week after roughly six months of sideways drift. That is not a marketing metric, it is settlement demand. Circle also holds ~80% of DEX trading and finance volume, and its Agent Stack, launched in May, now hosts 900+ paid services with 99% of x402 agent-payment volume settling in USDC. If you are pricing stablecoin issuers, the framework analysts are using matters: per the [unrolled thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/2091836616130052522.html), Wall Street genuinely cannot agree because Circle's revenue model is yield on turnover plus stablecoin velocity, not interchange or net interest margin, so it does not slot into standard interbank comps.

Then the infrastructure play: [Arc goes live September 16](https://thedefiant.io/converge/blockchains/circle-targets-sept-16-for-arc-public-mainnet-launch). Arc is a permissioned proof-of-authority chain, meaning block production stays restricted to a validator allowlist even as the network opens to public users and developers. Founding validators are Circle plus 11 institutions: BlackRock, DTCC, Mastercard, Standard Chartered, and Visa among them, with documentation pointing to ~20 validators at launch. The testnet logged 671.5M transactions across ~3M wallets, 100+ partners are already live on private mainnet, and Uniswap is committed to a day-one deployment. Fees settle in USDC, and it stays EVM-compatible. Read that as Circle turning USDC into the native gas token of a chain whose validator set is a who’s-who of TradFi payments and custody. That is a serious bet on a closed validator model surviving in a world that spent five years making fun of closed validator models.

## The maximalist eulogy

Nic Carter's [Second and Final Eulogy for Bitcoin Maximalism](https://x.com/nic_carter/status/2091197072145777042) is the best piece of crypto writing this week and worth the eight minutes. The structural argument: maximalism mirrors 19th-century Millerism, a movement that survived repeated failed predictions by retreating into unfalsifiable "not yet" framing. Bitcoin trades at all-time highs with broad institutional custody and government recognition, but it circulates as a regulated store of value, not the sovereign payment layer maximalists predicted, and Lightning never hit mass adoption. The ideology's contradiction is that bitcoin's success vindicates a store-of-value thesis maximalists explicitly rejected, while the institutional custody they condemned is the same thing driving the price. The conclusion is that the ideological death is now irreversible as the original cohort ages out. It is a long read and a fair one, even if you disagree.

## The stablecoin cross-border pitch is shakier than the deck says

[Jonah Burger's piece](https://x.com/jonah_b/status/2090497443787952455) is the antidote to the "stablecoins fix remittances" slide. The cross-currency problem (dollars in, pesos out) is already solved by fintechs: Wise and peers hold prefunded local currency pools, net offsetting flows in-country, and cut costs from the ~15% that SWIFT correspondent banking charges to under 1% with near-instant settlement. Stablecoins' real edge is narrower: dollar-to-dollar, unbanked recipients without fintech access, 24/7 settlement, and crypto-native workflows. If you are building a stablecoin payments startup, the wedge is not "beat SWIFT," it is "beat Wise for the cases Wise cannot serve." Different pitch, different TAM.

## Operation Asterix: 885,000 phone numbers and a Binance shortlist

The recovered logs matched 5,576 accounts to Binance users, queued for attack, with fake emails impersonating Crypto.com in the mix. If you run treasury or ops at an exchange, expect another wave of social engineering attempts this week, and expect your users to get texts pointing at lookalike wallet domains.
