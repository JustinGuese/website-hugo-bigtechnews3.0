The gap between Binance's loss figure and the DOJ's recovery target is worth noting: the DOJ's number only covers the USDC portion, not the full haul.

An [Ethereum research post](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/obMdm24NzVPaKf4ridUr1Q/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjQtY3J5cHRvfHdoYXRzLWEtYmxvY2stYW5kLWhvdy1iaWctY2FuLWl0LWdldHx8aHR0cHM6Ly9ldGhyZXNlYXIuY2gvdC93aGF0cy1hLWJsb2NrLWFuZC1ob3ctYmlnLWNhbi1pdC1nZXQvMjYyODE_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t) analyzes block size limits under "Glamsterdam" rules with a 60M gas limit reference point. EIP-7934 caps the RLP-encoded EL block at 8 MiB, while the CL caps gossip messages at 10 MiB, and gas pricing does not keep blocks under either limit.

The biggest block is not calldata-driven but zero-byte calldata plus thousands of skipped EIP-7702 authorizations, yielding about 1.8 MiB at 60M gas. The 8 MiB EL limit is hit at 266M gas, and with EIP-8131, the 10 MiB CL limit breaks first at 339M gas. This creates a structural conflict with FOCIL (EIP-7805): a transaction satisfying gas constraints may breach the 8 MiB EL byte cap when appended, leaving builders unable to honor attesters' inclusion expectations despite valid gas usage. The proposed re-encoding patch fails in four edge cases, so the author argues for pricing every payload byte in gas via EIP-8131 and EIP-8279 plus deposit-request metering, with a capped gas limit. This bounds worst-case envelope size and gives the CL a deterministic maximum: max_envelope_size = C + ceil(1.25 x gas_limit / 64). For block producers and validator operators, these limits directly affect block production, gossip propagation, and the viability of FOCIL.

[XMR1, Wagyu's wrapped Monero token on Hyperliquid](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/WmrBlSWH-nFY9xJdkLoKAQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjQtY3J5cHRvfHhtcjEtY29sbGFwc2VzLTk5LWFmdGVyLWRlcGxveWVyLWtleS1zdG9sZW58fGh0dHBzOi8vZGVjcnlwdC5jby8zODM3MzIvd3JhcHBlZC1tb25lcm8tdG9rZW4tY29sbGFwc2VzLTk5LWFmdGVyLTEzLW1pbGxpb24taGFjay1ibGFtZWQtb24td2ViLWhvc3Q_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t), fell from about $500 to $1 in 30 minutes after an attacker extracted 5.2 million tokens from the deployer wallet and sold 364,000 of them to 388 buyers for $13.5 million, converting the proceeds to about 7,350 ETH.

Wagyu founder PerpetualCow attributed the breach to Icelandic host 1984.hosting, claiming the deployer private key persisted on a deleted server whose logs the host retained. 1984 confirmed a broad cyberattack on its infrastructure without naming Wagyu or disclosing whether customer data was taken. The attacker seeded the exploit wallet via Tornado Cash minutes before the drain, and the same deployer account still controls ZEC1, a wrapped Zcash token carrying identical centralization risk. Bitquery says it cannot verify how much Monero backs remaining tokens, and native Monero was not affected. This is Wagyu's second reported breach in two months and follows the service's appearance in ZachXBT's tracing of a $106 million theft routed through the platform.
