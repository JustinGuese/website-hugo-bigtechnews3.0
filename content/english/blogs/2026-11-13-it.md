Your email was down again. Microsoft's November 12 outage took out Exchange Online, Teams features, and the Intune admin console, with the company blaming failed server health checks and an overwhelmed server process. Recovery meant rerouting traffic and restarting servers, which is a fancy way of saying the fleet got tired and needed a nap. It's the kind of day that makes you wonder why you migrated to the cloud in the first place, and then you remember: because you didn't want to rack servers.

The bigger story is that Google just shipped a model that makes decisions instead of generating text. The Gemini Decisions API is a closed-set scorer, not a chatbot. And if you run a Mac fleet, Claris wants to turn it into an AI cluster.

Google's new [Gemini Decisions API](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/R8EAn-d16Gf0xEKNED7WKg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTMtaXR8Z2VtaW5pLWRlY2lzaW9ucy1hcGktdGhlLTE1MC1tcy1hbnN3ZXItdG8tYWdlbnQtcm91dGluZ3x8aHR0cHM6Ly9ibG9nLmdvb2dsZS9pbm5vdmF0aW9uLWFuZC1haS90ZWNobm9sb2d5L2RldmVsb3BlcnMtdG9vbHMvZ2VtaW5pLWRlY2lzaW9ucy1hcGkvP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) is powered by Gemini Decision 1 (`gemini-decision-1`), a model built for fast, closed-set decisions: tool selection, policy checks, model routing, yes/no, multiple choice, rubric scoring. It takes multimodal input (text, images, audio, video, PDFs) up to 256K tokens and answers up to 1,000 independent questions in a single forward pass. End-to-end latency is 150-250 ms, and the model returns a confidence score from 0 to 1 for each answer.

The pricing is the headline: $0.10 per 1M input tokens, with zero output tokens billed. Google claims that's nearly 5x cheaper than Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite for decision workloads. For engineers building agent orchestrators, this is the difference between a routing layer that costs real money per call and one that costs fractions of a cent. The calibrated confidence scores also mean you can set thresholds: route to a human or a bigger model only when the score drops below 0.8. That's the kind of knob SREs like to turn.
