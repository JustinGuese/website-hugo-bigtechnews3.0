The day's real event is a financing structure, not a product. Amazon is negotiating to sell and lease back roughly $8bn of Nvidia Grace Blackwell hardware - thousands of chips - while the ChatGPT operator circles a $30bn round anchored by a major asset manager and UAE sovereign money. Put side by side, these say the same thing: AI compute demand has outrun the balance sheets that were supposed to carry it, and structured finance is arriving to fill the gap. Everything else on the tape - a signed Qualcomm license, ServiceNow's credit-card service desk, an Apple foldable milestone - is real but smaller.

<h2 id="the-buildout-learns-structured-finance">The buildout learns structured finance</h2>

Sale-and-leaseback is the interesting instrument. Amazon keeps the GPUs and the AWS capacity; a private-credit buyer carries the asset and earns the spread. Three second-order reads:

- **For Nvidia**, this is demand confirmation - thousands of Grace Blackwell parts moving into one of the three biggest clouds. But it is *levered* demand. Leased fleets can be handed back faster than owned ones, and that option is precisely what the lessor is being paid for. If AI revenue disappoints next year, the return queue forms at the leasing desk first.
- **For Amazon**, it preserves capex headroom at a moment when every hyperscaler is defending free cash flow. The unspoken question is why the largest capex budgets on earth need help financing
