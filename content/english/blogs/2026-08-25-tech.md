Tesla has a date. On September 3, the company is holding an invite-only event in Austin to launch the [Cybercab](https://electrek.co/2026/08/24/tesla-cybercab-exclusive-access-robotaxi-sept-3/?utm_source=tldrnewsletter) — a two-seat, no-steering-wheel vehicle added to Tesla's existing Robotaxi app. Invites go to the sweepstakes winners and the riders who logged the most Robotaxi trips. ## Solid-state transformers become a data center "killer app"

The AI buildout has a less glamorous bottleneck than GPUs: the box that sits between the substation and the server rack. [Solid-state transformers](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/08/energy-hungry-ai-data-centers-spur-new-power-transformer-technology/?utm_source=tldrnewsletter) (SSTs) use high-frequency semiconductor switching — typically silicon carbide — to do voltage conversion that iron-core transformers have handled with hum and heat for a century. They are smaller, lighter, modular, and crucially can convert AC from a local distribution network straight to the DC that data centers actually need, eliminating a stage of rectification. The economics are weirdly favorable: anything built like a semiconductor fab scales like one, so what was a custom utility device starts to look like a SKU. For anyone sizing a campus, SSTs reduce floor area, simplify cooling, and let you upgrade the conversion stage without rewiring medium-voltage gear.

## Humanoids are shipping in volume

The World Humanoid Games in Beijing produced both a viral [Honor Lightning bot beating Usain Bolt's 100m record](https://itcanthink.substack.com/p/approaching-robotics-hardware-takeoff?utm_source=tldrnewsletter) and a steady stream of fires and crashes. The interesting number is off-stage: Unitree sold over 5,500 humanoid robots in 2025, mostly the G1, and Agibot reportedly ships more. The pattern is the same one Chinese hardware hit in drones and EVs — aggressive iteration, vertical integration, and a willingness to break hardware in public. For engineers, the question is no longer "can a humanoid walk" but "what can a production-volume humanoid do reliably in a structured environment." The demo-to-deployable gap is closing, and the supplier base is real.

## Uber's "software factory" for agentic coding

Uber dropped a [concrete reference architecture for running agentic coding org-wide](https://newsletter.port.io/p/how-uber-built-a-software-factory?utm_source=tldrnewsletter). The numbers do the talking: more than 70% of pull requests are now written by AI agents, and code shipped per engineer has doubled in a year. The platform underneath has six pieces: a context graph with 40M entries across 150 node/edge types (replacing 20–30 source systems), an MCP gateway, an LLM gateway, compute, governance, and workflow orchestration. The lesson Uber is explicit about: platform infrastructure has to come before agent workflows, or the agents step on each other. For anyone staring at a "let's just give everyone Cursor" mandate, this is the most useful blueprint published so far.

## The context window is the state machine

A companion [essay on context engineering](https://www.vedanshh.com/blog/context-engineering?utm_source=tldrnewsletter) reframes what an "agent harness" actually is. Every component either injects information into the model's context, gates what is allowed in, or validates what comes out. That is the whole job. The trap is treating harnesses as additive — bolt on more retrievers, more tools, more memory — without thinking about what to evict. The context window is finite, so engineering the window is engineering the state machine. Practical takeaway: budget your window the way you'd budget heap, and design for eviction, not just insertion.

## Your executable is a SQLite database

A prototype called [SELF (Structured Executable & Linkable Format)](https://fzakaria.com/2026/08/23/your-executable-is-a-sqlite-database?utm_source=tldrnewsletter) replaces ELF with a SQLite database as the actual binary you chmod +x and run. The post maps ELF internals — strtab, .gnu.hash, section headers, .gnu.version_r — onto database primitives: string interning, indexes, schema tables, foreign keys, b-tree pages. There is a working `sqlelf` tool for SQL-based ELF exploration on GitHub. If this proves viable, executables become queryable, mutable, and inspectable in ways that would make postmortems and security analysis much cheaper. It is a hackathon-grade prototype today, but the direction is interesting.

## The public really hates data centers

75% of Americans [oppose local data center development](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/the-american-people-really-hate-data?utm_source=tldrnewsletter) — polling worse than coal plants. The author argues the opposition is mostly a proxy for distrust of AI, big tech, and big money, layered on top of plain NIMBYism, and is not moved by tax revenue or job numbers. For anyone planning a campus, this is the binding constraint now: even useful infrastructure like transmission lines is getting swept up. Permitting timelines and site selection are where buildouts die.

## Starlink wants to be a carrier

On SpaceX's first earnings call, Starlink outlined a [terrestrial cellular strategy](https://potsandpansbyccg.com/2026/08/24/starlinks-interesting-approach-to-cellular/?utm_source=tldrnewsletter): deploy cell sites with satellite backhaul, ride on next-gen V3 sats, and feed hundreds of thousands of sites from the constellation. The spectrum position is thin — 65 MHz acquired from EchoStar, with an 800 MHz FCC auction mandated but not yet held. The US wireless market is already a bloodbath, which is why the analyst's read is that developing markets like Nigeria are the more realistic beachhead. The whole plan collapses without the auction and without V3 capacity, and SpaceX has filed to grow the constellation from ~15,000 approved sats to 100,000.

## "The harness is the company"

A short, sharp [framing of where SaaS goes next](https://blog.sshh.io/p/the-harness-is-the-company?utm_source=tldrnewsletter): every software business becomes the infrastructure, interfaces, context, and state around a stateless LLM. The trajectory runs from no harness → individuals operating harnesses → individuals orchestrating harnesses → harnesses orchestrating individuals. In the end state, the org chart exists to put humans where their judgment matters, and the company's moat is domain knowledge plus review loops. If you are building a SaaS product today, the question is what part of your stack survives as the harness.
