[Levent Alpöge](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/EvwA78laN711sPwFEYCcVA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjUtdGVjaHx0aGUtcmllbWFubi1oeXBvdGhlc2lzLTE3LW1pbGxpb24tbGluZXMtYW5kLXR3by1pbmRlcGVuZGVudC12ZXJpZmljYXRpb25zfHxodHRwczovL3d3dy53c2ouY29tL3RlY2gvYWkvcmllbWFubi1oeXBvdGhlc2lzLWFudGhyb3BpYy1tYXRoLWxldmVudC1hbHBvZ2UtNzUzNTFiY2I_c3Q9bzFxR1R0JnJlZmxpbms9ZGVza3RvcHdlYnNoYXJlX3Blcm1hbGluayZ1dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) graduated Harvard with the highest GPA in his class, took America's top young researcher prize, and earned his Princeton Ph.D. before joining Anthropic. His claim: the Riemann Hypothesis, the 160-year-old conjecture about the distribution of prime numbers that underpins huge swaths of number theory and cryptography, is true. The proof runs 17 million lines. Two independent groups have already verified it.

Whether you're a cryptographer or just someone who runs TLS, the Riemann Hypothesis matters more than its pure-math reputation suggests. Its truth tightens bounds on prime distribution that appear throughout algorithmic number theory. The verification process - not the proof itself - is the radical part: formally certified, machine-checkable, and beyond what any human referee could demand of a journal paper.

The [wider picture](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/wyyC3qi8rZxjTF22qXP1LQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjUtdGVjaHx0aGUtcmllbWFubi1oeXBvdGhlc2lzLTE3LW1pbGxpb24tbGluZXMtYW5kLXR3by1pbmRlcGVuZGVudC12ZXJpZmljYXRpb25zfHxodHRwczovL3Njb3R0YWFyb25zb24uYmxvZy8_cD0xMDM2MiZ1dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) is that this isn't an isolated event. Anthropic and OpenAI both published proofs of Valiant's VP≠VNP conjecture, the algebraic analogue of P≠NP. Anthropic's requires circuits of size exp(Ω(log^{4/3} n)) for the permanent; OpenAI's needs exp(Ω(log^{5/4} n)), both over fields of characteristic other than 2. The proofs use compression followed by diagonalization, dodging the natural proofs barrier, and are formally certified in Lean.

The consequence for working engineers: the "AI can't do original math" argument is dead. These aren't pattern-matched homework problems. They're frontier results with machine-checkable certificates. Expect formal verification tooling to move from niche to expected practice in the next few years.

Storage devices fail in ways that are messier than "the disk died": torn writes after power loss, partial sequences, I/O errors, and latency spikes that shake out concurrency bugs. Critically, ext4, XFS, and ZFS all behave differently under the same failure, so fault injection that doesn't account for filesystem behavior will miss real bugs.

For anyone running data-critical systems: your chaos testing is only as good as its fault model. If you're not testing torn writes and latency anomalies against the specific filesystem you run in production, you're testing a fairy tale.

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/srMZc5h5lQuPbramoGrRbg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjUtdGVjaHwyMDM1LWNhbWUtZWFybHktbGludXgtZ3VpLWFwcHMtaW4tdGhlLWJyb3dzZXJ8fGh0dHBzOi8vZnpha2FyaWEuY29tLzIwMjYvMTEvMjMvMjAzNS1jYW1lLWVhcmx5P3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: fzakaria.com ↗</a></p>

[trynix.dev now boots GUI programs in the browser](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/srMZc5h5lQuPbramoGrRbg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjUtdGVjaHwyMDM1LWNhbWUtZWFybHktbGludXgtZ3VpLWFwcHMtaW4tdGhlLWJyb3dzZXJ8fGh0dHBzOi8vZnpha2FyaWEuY29tLzIwMjYvMTEvMjMvMjAzNS1jYW1lLWVhcmx5P3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) using a new unkernel engine that compiles Linux to WebAssembly. The kernel runs at wasm speed while user programs are emulated, delivering 2x to 32x faster performance than the previous QEMU-based approach. Over 300,000 Nix packages can now run in the browser.

This is a real step toward "the browser is the OS" - full Linux systems, GUI and all, without a cloud VM behind them. For anyone shipping web-based dev tools or demos, this changes what's possible.
