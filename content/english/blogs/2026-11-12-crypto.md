Novig lost its federal preemption fight, and the ruling is a blueprint for how states can shut down sports prediction markets without waiting on the CFTC. Judge Colleen McMahon denied the preliminary injunction the exchange sought against New York, finding its sports event contracts don't depend on events with a financial or economic consequence, so they aren't swaps under the Commodity Exchange Act. Her words: they "bear more than a passing resemblance to sports betting."

That lets New York demand Novig get a gambling license or stop listing sports contracts. Ludlow Exchange, Novig's operator, got CFTC contract market designation in June, launched in New York in August, and sued Attorney General Letitia James on August 5 on federal preemption grounds. The ruling tracks a similar denial for Kalshi earlier this year, and lawyer Daniel Wallach notes all 10 Second Circuit federal rulings in sports prediction-market cases have favored the states. State regulators may now move to enforcement in state court, where the federal preemption argument doesn't apply at all.

<h2 id="visa-adds-monad-settlement-network-hits-20b-run-rate">Visa Adds Monad, Settlement Network Hits $20B Run Rate</h2>

Visa added Monad as a stablecoin settlement option, bringing its blockchain settlement network to 10 chains: Ethereum, Solana, Base, Polygon, Avalanche, Stellar, Canton, Tempo, Circle's Arc, and now Monad. The program hit a $20 billion annualized settlement run rate as of September, up 15x year over year, with 225 stablecoin-linked card programs live globally.

The network's edge over traditional rails is operational: it settles on weekends and holidays, while Visa's conventional card rails run on a five-business-day banking schedule. Monad is building an institutional privacy feature, expected in the coming months, that keeps transaction details off the public ledger while remaining auditable by regulators and sharing liquidity with the public chain. For engineers, that's a confidentiality-vs-transparency tradeoff that most public blockchains haven't solved; Monad is betting it can.

<h2 id="the-sec-just-legalized-amms-for-79-trillion-in-stocks">The SEC Just Legalized AMMs for $79 Trillion in Stocks</h2>

The SEC's September 17 Innovation Exemption created a new venue category, the Tokenized Securities Venue (TSV), that lets AMMs on public blockchains trade tokenized NMS stocks without registering as exchanges or ATSs. Compliant liquidity providers aren't considered dealers. The exemption runs five years, and Uniswap has published an analysis of how to build one.

Uniswap's numbers are already substantial: over $20 billion in stock tokens have traded on the protocol, and in August, ten tokenized stocks began trading against SPY on Robinhood Chain. That grew to over 40 pairs with $900 million in volume across 130,000 wallets in three months. The TSV path requires KYC/KYL identity gating, per-pool disclosures, and price oracles tied to real-world exchanges - all implementable via Uniswap v4 hooks without touching the core AMM.

What this changes for operators: AMM settlement is atomic and final, versus the T+1 cycle in traditional clearinghouses. The target market is the $79 trillion US equities market. The exemption is the first clear regulatory path to building onchain markets for it, and it sidesteps the exchange-registration question that killed earlier tokenized-equity efforts.

<h2 id="hyperliquids-liquidity-moat-is-eroding">Hyperliquid&#x27;s Liquidity Moat Is Eroding</h2>

An analysis of orderbook data from November 2025 to November 2026 across 38 comparable markets shows Hyperliquid's long-tail dollar depth fell 82%, versus 61% on Binance and 78% on OKX. After controlling for token price declines, HL's depth relative to Binance dropped 57.5% at 5 bps. A $10,000 buy costs 5.21 bps on HL versus 2.95 bps on Binance before fees.

The causes are structural, not cyclical. Wintermute has effectively monopolized read priority fees, and L1 congestion at 25-40k TPS has disadvantaged smaller market makers, pushing them to widen spreads and reduce depth against adverse selection risk. Deep long-tail liquidity was Hyperliquid's founding differentiation from centralized venues; if that's gone, the exchange competes on the same terms as everyone else, and its fee schedule and validator set become the remaining differentiators.

<h2 id="defi-moats-survive-ai-commoditization">DeFi Moats Survive AI Commoditization</h2>

Multicoin Capital's Spencer Applebaum argues AI has collapsed the cost of launching a DeFi product from six-month, $3M builds to weekend projects - and that this strengthens incumbents rather than eroding them. A model can write a lending protocol in an afternoon, but it can't manifest $10 billion in stablecoin deposits or sign up 28 million customers.

The updated value-capture framework adds AI agents as the next class of sophisticated users, and they'll gravitate toward protocols with the deepest liquidity and most reliable execution at scale, not the nicest UI. The moats are "unforkable state": liquidity depth, customer trust, and proprietary risk underwriting. For new entrants, this means competing on code is a losing game; the defensible assets are balance sheets and data, not smart contracts.

<h2 id="stock-tokens-are-where-stablecoins-were-in-2023">Stock Tokens Are Where Stablecoins Were in 2023</h2>

The DTCC launched its Digital Tokenization Service (DTS), which can turn a share into a token - the biggest change in market infrastructure since paper certificates went away in the 1970s. It's currently mostly unused: the tokenized stock market is about 0.004% of stocks and ETFs, with $4.6 billion in tokenized stocks, nearly all wrapped tokens backed by shares at one broker, Alpaca.

Four models are competing: wrapped (xStocks, no shareholder rights), pooled (OKXICE, where 56 of 62 targeted companies opted out), mirrored (DTCC, with 34 securities on Canton and Besu and one Canton transaction so far), and native (Superstate and Figure, which need SEC rule changes). Apple, Nvidia, and Microsoft refused to list on OKXICE. The comparison to stablecoins in 2023 suggests the market is about 18 months from takeoff. Headwinds: a Warren-led Senate Banking Committee and faster settlement eating into netting benefits. Banks that sit out risk buying tokenization later from Robinhood or BlackRock.

<h2 id="building-networks-for-agents">Building Networks for Agents</h2>

The intents model from UniswapX - users sign outcome-specified orders and providers compete to fill them - is a template for agent-to-agent commerce. Instead of building networks for humans, founders can build machine-to-machine networks where suppliers' agents compete to fulfill requests within pre-authorized limits. A group dinner becomes tractable: each attendee's agent coordinates preferences, and restaurants' agents offer deals based on capacity.

Four design problems are concrete: agent discovery of relevant suppliers, verifying supplier performance, pricing fuzzy multidimensional preferences, and preventing fake demand or supply. Crypto and stablecoins are the natural settlement layer for these transactions. This is a founder opportunity for infrastructure, not an app.
