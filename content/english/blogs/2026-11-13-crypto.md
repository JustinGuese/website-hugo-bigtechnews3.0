The SEC filed an [amicus brief](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/I4QqZa3sp8DNfMbOz0HbYw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTMtY3J5cHRvfHNlYy1iYWNrcy1jZnRjLWluLWVxdWl0eS1wZXJwcy1qdXJpc2RpY3Rpb24tZmlnaHR8fGh0dHBzOi8vd3d3LnRoZWJsb2NrLmNvL25ld3MvcmVndWxhdGlvbi8yMDI2LTExLTExLXNlYy1lbnRlcnMtZmlnaHQtd2hvLXJlZ3VsYXRlcy1wZXJwZXR1YWwtZGVyaXZhdGl2ZXMtc3RvY2tzLWluLXVzLTQzODQyNz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) supporting the CFTC after the Capital Markets Alliance sued to overturn the CFTC's approval of equity perpetual derivatives. The Alliance, which includes Cboe and Citadel Securities, claims the approval violated the Administrative Procedure Act and that these products belong under SEC jurisdiction, not the CFTC's.

The SEC rejected the picture of a "rogue" CFTC operating without oversight. Its brief argues that CFTC rule changes are subject to SEC review and that the two agencies "collaborate often." The subtext: the Alliance is trying to drag a product into a slower, more familiar approval process, and the SEC isn't having it.

For engineers building on either venue's rails, this matters because the outcome determines which regulatory regime equity perps fall under - different reporting, different margin requirements, different compliance hooks. A CFTC win keeps the product in the derivatives sandbox. An SEC win could push it into securities law territory with a whole different set of constraints.

If you run infrastructure that touches USDT on Tron, expect faster freezes and more active monitoring. The task force's stated goal is to act before funds move, which means fewer "too late" recovery attempts and more preemptive action.

Circle's engineers have been busy on two fronts. First, they contributed to [ERC-6900](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/EUwg0EH5tzV0s54EESityg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTMtY3J5cHRvfGNpcmNsZXMtYWdlbnRpYy13b3Jrcy1hbmQtdGhlLWVyYy02OTAwLXB1c2h8fGh0dHBzOi8vdGhyZWFkcmVhZGVyYXBwLmNvbS90aHJlYWQvMjEyMDI0NTEwODA3OTgxMjgzNS5odG1sP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) as co-authors alongside Alchemy and the Ethereum Foundation. The standard introduces plugins as modular building blocks for smart wallets, extending ERC-4337's account abstraction. Second, they launched Faucet, a permissionless tool at faucet.circle.com that provides free testnet USDC and EURC across all supported blockchain testing networks, with no sign-up required.

ERC-6900 is the more significant piece. It lets developers build wallets where features are added as plugins rather than hardcoded - think automated payments, gas sponsorship, or custom authorization logic swapped in without redeploying the wallet contract. For teams building on account abstraction, this standardizes how plugins are written and verified.

The Faucet is simpler but genuinely useful: free testnet stablecoins on demand, no sign-up, no KYC. It removes a friction point in testing fund flows in apps and smart contracts.

An [ethresear.ch opinion post](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/mK_WZT7M8qAAo7ff9dVI1A/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTMtY3J5cHRvfGVsYXN0aWMtZXRoZXJldW0tYWRhcHRpdmUtY2FwYWNpdHktaW5zdGVhZC1vZi1maXhlZC1saW1pdHN8fGh0dHBzOi8vZXRocmVzZWFyLmNoL3QvZWxhc3RpYy1ldGhlcmV1bS8yNjIzNj91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) argues Ethereum should replace fixed slot times, gas limits, and pipeline depth with measurement-driven elastic parameters. The author draws an analogy to TCP congestion control, which scales the same protocol across four orders of magnitude of link speed.

The data is compelling. Xatu telemetry from 233 instrumented nodes over 100,548 mainnet blocks (October 27 - November 9) shows median propagation reaches 95% of nodes in 0.38 seconds. But late-head rates range from 1.7% on company nodes to 11.0% on individual contributor nodes. Block size matters too: 250 - 260 KiB blocks take 0.61 seconds to propagate versus 0.34 seconds for 50 - 60 KiB blocks.

The author maps Ethereum's mempool to TCP's send buffer and orphaned blocks to packet loss, identifying slot time (EIP-7782, EIP-8198), gas-limit votes, and parallel pipelining via Rapidblocks as adaptive knobs. This is an opinion piece, not a proposal, but it frames the scaling debate in terms engineers understand: fixed margins are either idle or insufficient, and adaptive systems handle both better.

Nomura is exploring a sale of [Laser Digital](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/EoByG0ZqO247CZ20FPxhsQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTMtY3J5cHRvfG5vbXVyYS1leHBsb3Jlcy1zZWxsaW5nLWxhc2VyLWRpZ2l0YWx8fGh0dHBzOi8vdGhyZWFkcmVhZGVyYXBwLmNvbS90aHJlYWQvMjEyMDU5NDM2NDk2MzczODA1Ni5odG1sP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ), its institutional crypto subsidiary, with Marex Group among prospective buyers. Losses at Laser Digital from crypto market volatility pushed Nomura's European operations into a quarterly loss, prompting management to tighten risk controls.

This is the classic TradFi P&L problem: a crypto subsidiary's drawdowns roll up into the parent's segment results, and the parent decides the exposure isn't worth it. But Laser Digital's regulatory footprint - a Japanese crypto exchange license and conditional approval for a US national trust bank charter - makes it valuable to any acquirer who wants the licenses without building from scratch.
