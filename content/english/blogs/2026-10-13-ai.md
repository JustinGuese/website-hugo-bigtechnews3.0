NVIDIA researchers published work showing that [offline student rollouts often beat on-policy distillation](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/ez2A2yDiccaT9Ho_BYZjbg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMTMtYWl8bnZpZGlhLXJlcGxhY2VzLW9uLXBvbGljeS1kaXN0aWxsYXRpb24td2l0aC1zZW1pLW9wZHx8aHR0cHM6Ly9yZXNlYXJjaC5udmlkaWEuY29tL3B1YmxpY2F0aW9uLzIwMjYtMTJfd2hlbi1kby13ZS1uZWVkLXBvbGljeS1kaXN0aWxsYXRpb24tZGlzdGlsbGluZy1vZmZsaW5lLXN0dWRlbnQtcm9sbG91dHMtb2Z0ZW4_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t). Distillation is standard practice for compressing monster frontier models down to fast operational endpoints, but traditional On-Policy Distillation (OPD) requires expensive interactive sampling runs during student training.

NVIDIA tested its alternative, Semi-OPD - which samples rollouts offline from the initial student model - across 17 different teacher-student pairs ranging from 1.5B to 235B parameters. Semi-OPD outperformed OPD in 14 of the 17 evaluations, increasing task accuracy by up to 13.6% while accelerating training runs by up to 11.4x. The researchers noted a distinct architectural rule: traditional OPD only helps when the output distribution of the student and teacher already has high overlap. When transferring between divergent architectures or mismatched sizes, offline rollouts are both cheaper and significantly more accurate.
