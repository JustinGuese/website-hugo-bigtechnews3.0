Wells Fargo just became the biggest reported Bitcoin ETF holder among investment managers, and it wasn't close. The bank's 13F filing shows 49.58 million IBIT shares worth $2.35 billion as of September 30, a 103% share count increase from Q2's 24.4 million. That puts it ahead of Millennium Management, Jane Street, and Morgan Stanley. The catch: this aggregates client-managed positions across the bank's investment arms, so it's reported exposure, not necessarily the bank's own bet. Still, the number is the number, and it's a signal about where institutional money is actually flowing.

The snapshot predates Bitcoin's pullback from $74,000 to $63,000, during which IBIT recorded more than $1 billion in outflows. Current positioning is anyone's guess from public data alone. But the September 30 picture is unambiguous: a major US bank's clients are loading up on Bitcoin exposure at scale.

> Wells Fargo's $2.35 billion IBIT position represents indirect exposure to roughly 28,070 BTC, putting it ahead of Millennium Management, Jane Street, and Morgan Stanley.

<h2 id="trezors-shipping-partner-leaks-again-and-this-time-its-worse">Trezor&#x27;s Shipping Partner Leaks Again, and This Time It&#x27;s Worse</h2>

Trezor's cold wallets are fine. Your personal data is not. Shipping partner Skladon was breached on November 7, exposing names, phone numbers, addresses, emails, and order details for orders placed between August 9 and November 7. That's roughly 27,000 orders. No wallet backups, PINs, or passphrases were exposed, so funds are safe. But some customers have already reported scam calls impersonating Trezor support.

This is the second breach in months. In August, ShipMonk leaked data affecting about 13,700 customers. If you ordered a Trezor in the last three months, assume your shipping details are public and treat any "support" call as hostile. The hardware wallet is still the right answer for self-custody; the supply chain around it is the attack surface that keeps failing.

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/93VJ_uaB-wCUpZ5GhQU1Yw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTgtY3J5cHRvfGVpcC03OTk5cy1tdWx0aWRpbWVuc2lvbmFsLWZlZXMtbW9yZS1nYXMtbW9yZS1jb25nZXN0aW9ufHxodHRwczovL2V0aHJlc2Vhci5jaC90L21vcmUtbWV0ZXJzLW9yLW1vcmUtcHJpY2VzLW9uZS12cy1tdWx0aS1kaW1lbnNpb25hbC1mZWUtbWVjaGFuaXNtcy1vbi1ldGhlcmV1bS8yNjI0ND91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: ethresear.ch ↗</a></p>

Ethereum researchers published a detailed benchmark of [EIP-7999's multidimensional fee market](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/93VJ_uaB-wCUpZ5GhQU1Yw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTgtY3J5cHRvfGVpcC03OTk5cy1tdWx0aWRpbWVuc2lvbmFsLWZlZXMtbW9yZS1nYXMtbW9yZS1jb25nZXN0aW9ufHxodHRwczovL2V0aHJlc2Vhci5jaC90L21vcmUtbWV0ZXJzLW9yLW1vcmUtcHJpY2VzLW9uZS12cy1tdWx0aS1kaW1lbnNpb25hbC1mZWUtbWVjaGFuaXNtcy1vbi1ldGhlcmV1bS8yNjI0ND91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) against one-dimensional alternatives. The headline: a floor-adjusted model with a 4/5 target-to-limit ratio increases execution gas from 151.9M - 177.9M to 199.0M - 215.0M per block. That's real throughput gain. The cost is state growth, which drops to 56.7 - 68.7 GiB/year, and near-limit block congestion, which rises from ~4% to 13.2% - 20.6%.

The paper also flags a problem with the current EIP-1559 update rule: it constrains performance, keeping base fees at 18 - 27 wei versus a 2.4 - 7.2 wei equilibrium. The researchers replaced it with EIP-4844's fake-exponential update for comparison. The conclusion backs EIP-7999's design: separate resource pricing beats separate resource metering as the network scales. For anyone running infrastructure on Ethereum, this is the trade-off math that will define block space economics in the next upgrade cycle.

<h2 id="post-quantum-signatures-four-schemes-fit-one-doesnt"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/Q65r9q7JxylwIr2k9eKOPg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTgtY3J5cHRvfHBvc3QtcXVhbnR1bS1zaWduYXR1cmVzLWZvdXItc2NoZW1lcy1maXQtb25lLWRvZXNudHx8aHR0cHM6Ly9ldGhyZXNlYXIuY2gvdC9leHBsb3JpbmctcG9zdC1xdWFudHVtLXNpZ25hdHVyZS1hZ2dyZWdhdGlvbi1mb3ItZWlwLTgyODgvMjYyNjE_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Post-Quantum Signatures: Four Schemes Fit, One Doesn&#x27;t</a></h2>

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/Q65r9q7JxylwIr2k9eKOPg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTgtY3J5cHRvfHBvc3QtcXVhbnR1bS1zaWduYXR1cmVzLWZvdXItc2NoZW1lcy1maXQtb25lLWRvZXNudHx8aHR0cHM6Ly9ldGhyZXNlYXIuY2gvdC9leHBsb3JpbmctcG9zdC1xdWFudHVtLXNpZ25hdHVyZS1hZ2dyZWdhdGlvbi1mb3ItZWlwLTgyODgvMjYyNjE_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: ethresear.ch ↗</a></p>

The post-quantum migration is coming to Ethereum, and researchers are figuring out which signature schemes can actually work under [EIP-8288's recursive STARK aggregation](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/Q65r9q7JxylwIr2k9eKOPg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTgtY3J5cHRvfHBvc3QtcXVhbnR1bS1zaWduYXR1cmVzLWZvdXItc2NoZW1lcy1maXQtb25lLWRvZXNudHx8aHR0cHM6Ly9ldGhyZXNlYXIuY2gvdC9leHBsb3JpbmctcG9zdC1xdWFudHVtLXNpZ25hdHVyZS1hZ2dyZWdhdGlvbi1mb3ItZWlwLTgyODgvMjYyNjE_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t). They tested five candidates on Apple M4 Pro hardware: Falcon-512, ML-DSA-44, FORS, Raccoon-128, and Raccoon-G.

Four fit in one leanSTARK proof. FORS is the efficiency winner, fitting 100 signatures into a 298 KiB proof. Falcon-512 fits 32, ML-DSA-44 fits 6, and standard Raccoon-128 fits only 1. Raccoon-G is the outlier: it exceeds leanVM's commitment cap at ~839 million witness words versus a 268 million limit. It needs Merkle-tree-style recursive aggregation across five sub-proofs per signature, producing a 333 KiB root proof for 100 signatures plus 2.84 KiB of auxiliary data.

The practical takeaway: if you're building wallet infrastructure, FORS and Falcon-512 are the schemes to watch. Raccoon-G is technically viable but at a proof-size cost that will hurt on mainnet.

The pitch for tokenized equities usually leads with round-the-clock trading. The actual argument is access. [Tokenized equities](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/hIOVsjLhhBC682bOiZQKQg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTgtY3J5cHRvfHRoZS1yZWFsLWNhc2UtZm9yLXRva2VuaXplZC1lcXVpdGllcy1pcy1hY2Nlc3Mtbm90LTI0Ny10cmFkaW5nfHxodHRwczovL3RocmVhZHJlYWRlcmFwcC5jb20vdGhyZWFkLzIxMjIwNTgyNjE0MzIyNzA4NzUuaHRtbD91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) let users excluded from traditional brokerage systems hold US assets and generate yield onchain. That matters more than trading hours.

The thread makes a sharper point: major AI labs still route payments through credit card networks and legacy rails. If tokenized equities become the default investing layer inside AI-native financial applications, crypto stays at the center of global finance. If not, the payment layer cedes to the old system. The bet is that tokenized US equities become the killer app for international investors who already want US-denominated assets but can't get them easily.

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/IDxAm_tGeGHK9H1KTav5Tg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTgtY3J5cHRvfGJpdHdpc2VzLXNvbGFuYS1idWxsLWNhc2UtMTEwMC1ieS0yMDMxLWlmLWVxdWl0aWVzLXRva2VuaXplfHxodHRwczovL3d3dy50aGVibG9jay5jby9uZXdzL21hcmtldHMvMjAyNi0xMS0xNi1iaXR3aXNlLXNvbGFuYS1wcmljZS1wcmVkaWN0aW9uLTExMDAtMjAzMS00NDE0MDc_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: theblock.co ↗</a></p>

[Bitwise's onchain research team](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/IDxAm_tGeGHK9H1KTav5Tg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTgtY3J5cHRvfGJpdHdpc2VzLXNvbGFuYS1idWxsLWNhc2UtMTEwMC1ieS0yMDMxLWlmLWVxdWl0aWVzLXRva2VuaXplfHxodHRwczovL3d3dy50aGVibG9jay5jby9uZXdzL21hcmtldHMvMjAyNi0xMS0xNi1iaXR3aXNlLXNvbGFuYS1wcmljZS1wcmVkaWN0aW9uLTExMDAtMjAzMS00NDE0MDc_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t) put out a three-scenario SOL projection: bull case $1,100, base case $385, bear case $80. SOL was near $71 at publication.

The bull case hinges on 5% of global equities being tokenized by 2031, with Solana capturing 65% of the resulting trading volume. That implies ~$30.7 trillion in annual tokenized stock transactions and 3.6 billion daily transactions at 23x present throughput. The near-term catalysts are real: Securitize launched tokenized US stocks in October 2026, and DTCC plans a tokenized securities rollout on Solana in H2 2027.

The risks are equally concrete. The base case alone demands 2.5x Solana's maximum block compute. Ethereum and its L2s currently lead tokenized asset value. Tokenization pace, regulatory shifts, and infrastructure gaps could all derail the projection. Treat the $1,100 number as a scenario, not a forecast.

Revolut's stablecoin volume hit $3.2 billion in July, but [only 9% was customer activity](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/e0KzzhYZ0uuusyoHmXGv8A/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMTgtY3J5cHRvfHJldm9sdXRzLXN0YWJsZWNvaW4tdm9sdW1lLXdhcy1tb3N0bHktcmV2b2x1dHx8aHR0cHM6Ly90aHJlYWRyZWFkZXJhcHAuY29tL3RocmVhZC8yMTIyNDM5NTQ4MTQzOTExNDIzLmh0bWw_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t). After breaking down USDT and USDC transfers across its hot wallets on eight networks, two-thirds of the volume was Revolut shuffling funds between its own wallets ahead of the USDT delisting in the EEA.

Customers normally account for about 25-30% of volume. The July figure was a washout. Raw volume fell fourfold while customer flows only halved, and the decline began in May, before Revolut's delisting letter. The lesson: exchange volume metrics are garbage until you control for internal wallet movement.

<h2 id="is-crypto-vc-dead-the-math-says-its-changing">Is Crypto VC Dead? The Math Says It&#x27;s Changing</h2>

A venture capital LP dinner with $15 billion represented surfaced a structural problem for large crypto funds. A $2 billion vehicle targeting 3x needs $6 billion in returns. That was achievable in prior cycles via L1s and infrastructure. Now value has shifted to app-layer projects with distribution, and there's no clean path to venture-scale outcomes on crypto alone.

Consensus at the dinner: crypto-only funds have one or two more cycles before the asset class merges into broader frontier tech. Large VCs are expanding mandates into robotics, physical AI, and compute. Early talent is already moving. The near-term opportunities cited: AI-native companies adopting on-chain infrastructure, DeFi brokerages that haven't reached millions of users, and tokenized equities in emerging markets.

For engineers, this means funding and talent will reallocate toward AI and robotics. Crypto projects will need to justify their existence on real usage, not narrative.
