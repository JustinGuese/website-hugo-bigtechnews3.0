Prototyping custom hardware usually means waiting over a week for boards to clear assembly in southern China or paying massive domestic setup fees. CircuitHub launched [The Grid](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/hnxXeePGeFwavohHp0RjKg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMjktdGVjaHxjaXJjdWl0aHViLWxhdW5jaGVzLXRoZS1ncmlkLWZvci11cy1iYXNlZC1xdWljay10dXJuLXBjYnN8fGh0dHBzOi8vd3d3LmNpcmN1aXRodWIuY29tL3Bvc3QvdW52ZWlsaW5nLXRoZS1ncmlkP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ), a factory-scale automated PCB assembly plant in Massachusetts built specifically for high-mix, low-volume production runs.

The facility replaces traditional fixed-rail SMT lines with magnetically levitating movers directed by ML schedulers. Individual boards route dynamically through paste printing, component placement, reflow, and optical inspection without requiring manual tool reconfigurations between batches. The system delivered a tested 4-layer, 118-part board in 4 days for $630, compared to a 9-day median from quick-turn prototype shops in Shenzhen. That cuts the hardware iteration cycle in half for domestic engineering teams without requiring custom tooling charges.
