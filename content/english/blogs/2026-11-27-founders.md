The Jevons paradox - cheaper tokens should mean more total AI spend - is looking shaky. OpenRouter's paid usage data shows token volume up 44x year-to-date, but total spend only up 7.6x. The elasticity curve has collapsed from 5.4 in Q1 to 1.2 in Q4, and spend growth has been flat since July. Buyers are getting dramatically more output for roughly the same budget, and that changes how you price, build, and sell AI products.

The question is whether this is a permanent shift or just a lull. Similar dips in late 2024 and 2025 recovered when new models launched. But the magnitude here is different - two consecutive quarters of sub-2 elasticity suggests enterprises have hit a budget ceiling, not a capability ceiling.

Quarterly elasticity - the ratio of token growth to spend growth - fell from 5.4 in Q1 and 5.5 in Q2 to 1.6 in Q3 and 1.2 in Q4. Token growth is still strong at roughly 39% month-over-month, but spend growth has stalled since July.

The split is telling: enterprises are more cost-conscious than AI-native scale-ups. Enterprise buyers are optimizing for output per dollar, while startups are still throwing tokens at problems. If you're building an AI product, this means usage-based pricing is going to hit a wall sooner than you think. The buyers who drove the 44x token growth are now asking why their bill isn't going down as fast as model prices are.

Statista CEO Marc Berg told [The Next Web](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/BbRo8k2GW3g3YMUjD2Hixw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjctZm91bmRlcnN8c3RhdGlzdGEtdHJhZGVkLTI3LW1pbGxpb24tdXNlcnMtZm9yLWRhdGEtcHJvdGVjdGlvbnx8aHR0cHM6Ly90aGVuZXh0d2ViLmNvbS9uZXdzL21hcmMtYmVyZy1zdGF0aXN0YS1haS1jcmF3bGVycy1kYXRhLXRudy1wb2RjYXN0P3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) that the company's monthly search traffic collapsed from 35 million to about 8 million after moving data behind a registration wall. The trigger: chatbots started citing Statista data, and customers started asking why they should pay for a subscription when ChatGPT could answer their questions for $19/month.

The trade-off is stark. Statista now generates about 70% of its data internally, runs a consumer panel of 3+ million interviews, and maintains market models for 1,000+ industries. It sells licensed access through an API and MCP server that plugs into customers' AI tools. Some smaller customers left because chatbot answers were sufficient, but enterprise business is growing.

For anyone running a data business, this is the playbook question: do you optimize for search traffic that AI crawlers will hollow out, or do you wall off the data and sell access directly? Statista bet on the latter, and it cost them 77% of their organic reach.

For engineers, this kills the need to build a custom retrieval pipeline just to make your site queryable by agents. The domain itself becomes the search endpoint.

The [audit of enterprise network effects](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/KG9ZJ1s3Tj74VnUOZAlaEQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjctZm91bmRlcnN8bmV0d29yay1lZmZlY3RzLWluLXRoZS1haS1lcmEtd2hhdC1hY3R1YWxseS1ob2xkc3x8aHR0cHM6Ly9zZWxpbmFzc3RhY2suc3Vic3RhY2suY29tL3AvYXVkaXRpbmctZW50ZXJwcmlzZS1uZXR3b3JrLWVmZmVjdHM_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t) breaks down which moats survive AI. Counterparty network effects - where customers must share data, like Nominal for hardware test data or Carta for cap tables - are strengthened because proprietary data becomes more valuable to query. But trust and permissioning layers grow in importance.

The distinction matters: Equifax's proprietary credit data is harder to reproduce than ZoomInfo's publicly available contacts. AI makes thin marketplace features easier to build, so an app ecosystem like Salesforce AppExchange provides less protection than it used to. If your network effect depends on features competitors can recreate cheaply, AI just ate your moat.
