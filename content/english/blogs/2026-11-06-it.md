Google spent the week turning its data cloud into a federation layer rather than a destination. The centrepiece is a "borderless Lakehouse" on Apache Iceberg, cross-cloud interconnect and caching for AWS and Azure now generally available with zero egress fees, and catalog federation into Databricks Unity, Snowflake Horizon and AWS Glue.

That is a direct shot at the copy-everything-into-one-warehouse model that has defined the last decade of analytics. Google is saying the data can stay where it is, including in its competitors' catalogs, and you query it through Gemini-assisted tooling instead.

The rest of the day is a study in what AI actually costs. Microsoft is capping Copilot Chat without telling anyone the numbers, Apple is capping token budgets with numbers it also hasn't confirmed, and Binance lost $6.3 billion to an attack that started with a developer installing the wrong Homebrew formula.

<h2 id="google-wants-the-lakehouse-to-be-borderless">Google Wants the Lakehouse to Be Borderless</h2>

Iceberg is the open table format that lets different engines read the same files on object storage, and it has become the neutral ground in the warehouse wars. Google's pitch is that you no longer copy those tables into BigQuery to query them. Catalog federation means BigQuery can read metadata from Databricks Unity, Snowflake Horizon and AWS Glue without a migration project, and cross-cloud interconnect plus local caching means the bytes come over a managed path instead of a public-internet hop.

The caching layer is the part operators should care about. Zero egress fees are a pricing decision; local caching is a latency decision, and it changes the shape of a query plan when half your joins read from S3 in another region. Google also added Gemini natural-language querying and Knowledge Catalog context curation, aimed at agents that need to find the right table without a human writing the join.

What it replaces is the pipeline sprawl: CDC jobs, nightly extracts, and a serving copy per cloud. What it adds is a dependency on catalog APIs you don't control, and a governance problem that now spans systems owned by three other vendors. Federated access is not the same as federated permissions, and that gap is where the incidents will come from.

<h2 id="microsofts-copilot-chat-caps-are-secret-and-thats-the-point">Microsoft&#x27;s Copilot Chat Caps Are Secret, and That&#x27;s the Point</h2>

Microsoft is expanding usage limits for commercial Copilot Chat users who don't hold a paid Microsoft 365 Copilot licence. It won't disclose what the limits are, and it won't let administrators adjust them. The rollout started November 2 for smaller organisations and reaches tenants with more than 2,000 seats on November 9.

For anyone running a helpdesk, this is the worst possible shape for a quota. Users hit an invisible wall, admins can't see it coming, and there's no throttle to tune. Capacity planning becomes a matter of waiting for the tickets. Compare that with Apple, which at least leaked a number.

<h2 id="apple-cut-the-daily-ai-token-allowance-from-300-to-200">Apple Cut the Daily AI Token Allowance From $300 to $200</h2>

Apple is reportedly tightening employee AI budgets, dropping daily allowances from $300 to $200 and planning caps of $100 for engineers and $25 for other staff. The limits apply to third-party models including Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's ChatGPT, and some teams are being pushed toward cheaper or locally hosted models. Apple hasn't confirmed any of it.

If the numbers hold, the interesting part isn't the cut - it's the tiering. A $25/day ceiling for non-engineers is enough for chat and drafting; a $100 ceiling for engineers is not enough for agentic coding loops that burn tokens on test runs. That pushes exactly the workloads that benefit most from big models back toward smaller local ones, which is a capability decision disguised as a finance one.

The failure mode is the one every DR plan should be tested against: redundancy that shares a sensing or control path isn't redundancy. Two feeds and a generator farm are one system if a single misconfigured controller decides whether the generators start.
