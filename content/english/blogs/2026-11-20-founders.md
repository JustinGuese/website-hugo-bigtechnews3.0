Stripe finally put a number on the AI boom, and it's a big one: 103% aggregate revenue growth across nearly 20,000 AI-dependent businesses over the past year. But the number that matters more is the one underneath - recent annualized growth has cooled to 80%, down from 140% just a few months ago. The AI gold rush isn't over, but the easy money phase is.

The [Stripe AI Index](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/qYLP2AyfUY_X436F0907Ew/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjAtZm91bmRlcnN8fHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5zdHJpcGVlY29ub21pY3MuY29tL3AvaW50cm9kdWNpbmctdGhlLXN0cmlwZS1haS1pbmRleD91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) is a weekly measure of same-business payment activity, modeled on the company's existing SaaS Index. It's the first time founders have had a hard, recurring benchmark for what AI companies actually earn, not what they raise. And the median tells a different story than the aggregate: median growth was 41%, meaning the big players are pulling the average up hard. US companies grew 104%, versus 83% in the UK and 67% in the EU. If you're benchmarking your AI business against "the market," pick your geography carefully - the spread is wider than most people assume.

The operational details matter more than the metaphor. For engineers, the lesson is that in an exploding category, standard growth tactics that failed at older software companies now work simply because the tide lifts everything. The instability traces to the pages AI reads: only 42% of cited pages were the same day-to-day, so swapping pages swaps answers.

For anyone doing SEO or brand monitoring, this is the warning: don't rely on a single AI recommendation check. The volatility is structural, not a bug in one model. If you're tracking your brand's AI presence, you need a baseline across multiple days and engines, not a single screenshot.
