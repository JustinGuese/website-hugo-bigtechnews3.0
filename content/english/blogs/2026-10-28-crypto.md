<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/MSnXfnnfjkPQJ_qnOJIXTQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMjgtY3J5cHRvfHphcmstaW50cm9kdWNlcy16ay1wcm9vZi1wYXltZW50LXByaXZhY3ktZm9yLXNwYXJrfHxodHRwczovL2xpbmtzLnRsZHJuZXdzbGV0dGVyLmNvbS81T3VnZlI_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: links.tldrnewsletter.com ↗</a></p>

Bitcoin payments layer Spark - operated by firms like Lightspark, Flashnet, and Breez - suffers from the same fundamental privacy trade-off as Ark, Bark, and Wavelength: state leaf allocations and spend transactions are fully visible to central node operators. A new design dubbed [Zark](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/MSnXfnnfjkPQJ_qnOJIXTQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMjgtY3J5cHRvfHphcmstaW50cm9kdWNlcy16ay1wcm9vZi1wYXltZW50LXByaXZhY3ktZm9yLXNwYXJrfHxodHRwczovL2xpbmtzLnRsZHJuZXdzbGV0dGVyLmNvbS81T3VnZlI_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t) fixes this by wrapping offchain payments in zero-knowledge proofs without altering the core Spark consensus layer.

Zark introduces a 2-of-2 multisig spend condition pairing a Zark-managed keyshare with the Spark operator. User-to-user transfers are structured as zero-knowledge proofs that validate inputs and values, and the Zark protocol immediately deletes past keyshares to permanently break transaction graph linkages. The primary architectural drawback is arbitrary denomination splits through Spark Service Providers (SSPs): because each leaf swap requires an independent proof cycle, unoptimized payment settlement can drag up to 10 minutes. Production use will depend on aggregate SNARK proving implementations to compress multiple batched leaf swaps into a single verification round.

Hardware wallet manufacturer Keystone [disclosed a severe supply chain security incident](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/9n_a_eQzX2ON4-L8BzcJKw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMjgtY3J5cHRvfGtleXN0b25lLWRlc3Ryb3lzLWludmVudG9yeS1hZnRlci1mYWN0b3J5LWtleS1leHRyYWN0aW9ufHxodHRwczovL2xpbmtzLnRsZHJuZXdzbGV0dGVyLmNvbS94bWdYTHY_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t) stemming from a privileged compromise of an internal third-party monitoring integration. Attackers pivoted through the compromised service to pull customer records alongside the factory-level WebAuth device authentication private key.

The WebAuth key is physically burned into secure hardware modules during assembly to allow Keystone's verification service to validate device provenance. Because this key cannot be rotated over the air on units that have already cleared manufacturing, attackers holding the private key could construct maliciously modified hardware clones that pass Keystone's official authenticity checks. Keystone has halted order fulfillment, suspended manufacturing runs, and initiated the physical destruction of all 18,835 unprogrammed devices in its direct warehouse inventory until new factory keys are generated. The leak also swept up 25,964 customer order records due to a failure in an automated Shopify retention-erasure tool. Cryptographic private keys and seeds generated on user devices were not exposed.
