Snowflake wants its AI agents to keep working after you log off. Its CoCo and CoWork Bots are designed to run ongoing tasks in the background, act through tools teams already use, and pull people in when a decision or sign-off is needed.

That’s the useful promise - and the operational question. An agent that acts while nobody’s watching needs clear permissions, reliable context, and a way to stop it before a bad assumption becomes a completed task. Snowflake’s announcements put those control problems alongside the pitch.

More than 40 supermarkets across seven countries were affected by suspected cyberattacks that remotely raised refrigeration temperatures and spoiled food, according to [ITPro’s report](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/PlKe15tpNtcBvKVmRr4S2A/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDQtaXR8cmVmcmlnZXJhdGlvbi1zeXN0ZW1zLWJlY2FtZS1hLXJlbW90ZS1hdHRhY2stc3VyZmFjZXx8aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuaXRwcm8uY29tL3NlY3VyaXR5L2N5YmVyLWF0dGFja3MvYS1mcmVlemVyLXNob3VsZC1uZXZlci1hY2NlcHQtYW4taW5zdHJ1Y3Rpb24tdG8taGVhdC10by01MC1jLW5vLW1hdHRlci13aG8tc2VuZHMtaXQtbW9yZS1ldXJvcGVhbi1zdXBlcm1hcmtldHMtaGl0LWJ5LXN1c3BlY3RlZC1jeWJlci1hdHRhY2s_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t). Reports involved supermarket chains in Belgium, Luxembourg, Sweden, and Ireland, among others. The cause is still described as suspected; the incidents were reportedly detected after refrigeration equipment was tampered with remotely.

This is an operational technology problem, not just an IT account problem. Connected refrigeration controls can have direct physical consequences, while equipment may stay in service long after software support ends. A command path that lets a remote system alter temperatures needs authentication, network boundaries, and limits on what values it will accept.

The reported scale - hundreds of thousands of euros’ worth of food spoiled - makes the cost of leaving connected equipment exposed tangible. Operators need to know which devices are internet-accessible, who can issue commands, and whether controls can reject dangerous instructions regardless of where they came from.

For teams evaluating AI, the important distinction is between activity and outcomes. Tool usage, hours worked, and app switching are signals, not a productivity score. Measuring whether a workflow got faster or produced better results requires looking at the work itself.

For engineering and IT leaders, this is a reminder to map workflows before treating a model’s capability as a staffing plan. Rehiring costs can follow from removing the people who understood the edge cases.

A separate Business Ontology feature is planned for preview. Sandbox execution and agent-abuse detection are still in private preview, according to the newsletter. That distinction matters for teams assessing what they can deploy now versus what remains in testing. The architecture is aimed at central oversight, but it doesn’t remove the need to define who owns each agent and what it’s allowed to do.

“Sovereign” infrastructure is meant to give customers more control over where workloads and data run, and which providers operate them. Buyers will still need to assess the resulting system’s workload support, availability, and operating model.

<h2 id="teessides-data-center-plans-come-with-a-power-timeline"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/jCFA0obpJusJmBmtxOvscw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDQtaXR8dGVlc3NpZGVzLWRhdGEtY2VudGVyLXBsYW5zLWNvbWUtd2l0aC1hLXBvd2VyLXRpbWVsaW5lfHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5pdHByby5jb20vaW5mcmFzdHJ1Y3R1cmUvZGF0YS1jZW50cmVzL25hdGlvbmFsLWdyaWQtcGxhbnMtbWFzc2l2ZS1uZXctc3Vic3RhdGlvbi10by1zdXBwb3J0LXRlZXNzaWRlLWRhdGEtY2VudGVyLXBsYW5zP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Teesside’s data center plans come with a power timeline</a></h2>

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/jCFA0obpJusJmBmtxOvscw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDQtaXR8dGVlc3NpZGVzLWRhdGEtY2VudGVyLXBsYW5zLWNvbWUtd2l0aC1hLXBvd2VyLXRpbWVsaW5lfHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5pdHByby5jb20vaW5mcmFzdHJ1Y3R1cmUvZGF0YS1jZW50cmVzL25hdGlvbmFsLWdyaWQtcGxhbnMtbWFzc2l2ZS1uZXctc3Vic3RhdGlvbi10by1zdXBwb3J0LXRlZXNzaWRlLWRhdGEtY2VudGVyLXBsYW5zP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: itpro.com ↗</a></p>

National Grid plans a new substation at Dorman Point in Teesside to support data centers, battery storage, and other power-intensive industry, according to [ITPro](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/jCFA0obpJusJmBmtxOvscw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDQtaXR8dGVlc3NpZGVzLWRhdGEtY2VudGVyLXBsYW5zLWNvbWUtd2l0aC1hLXBvd2VyLXRpbWVsaW5lfHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5pdHByby5jb20vaW5mcmFzdHJ1Y3R1cmUvZGF0YS1jZW50cmVzL25hdGlvbmFsLWdyaWQtcGxhbnMtbWFzc2l2ZS1uZXctc3Vic3RhdGlvbi10by1zdXBwb3J0LXRlZXNzaWRlLWRhdGEtY2VudGVyLXBsYW5zP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ). The company is buying 73.3 acres, with the site located beneath 275kV and 400kV transmission lines.

The substation is expected to start with 1,440 MVA of capacity and come online by the end of 2029. That schedule puts a physical limit on how quickly new power-hungry projects can move from proposal to operation. Data center plans depend on grid infrastructure that takes years to build, not just on securing land and servers.

<h2 id="cios-are-deploying-ai-faster-than-they-can-operationalize-it"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/88135Ctee0g-41fS1z2Img/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDQtaXR8Y2lvcy1hcmUtZGVwbG95aW5nLWFpLWZhc3Rlci10aGFuLXRoZXktY2FuLW9wZXJhdGlvbmFsaXplLWl0fHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5hY2NlbnR1cmUuY29tL2VuL2luc2lnaHRzL2FpLWRhdGEvY2lvLXB1bHNlLXN1cnZleT91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">CIOs are deploying AI faster than they can operationalize it</a></h2>

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/88135Ctee0g-41fS1z2Img/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDQtaXR8Y2lvcy1hcmUtZGVwbG95aW5nLWFpLWZhc3Rlci10aGFuLXRoZXktY2FuLW9wZXJhdGlvbmFsaXplLWl0fHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5hY2NlbnR1cmUuY29tL2VuL2luc2lnaHRzL2FpLWRhdGEvY2lvLXB1bHNlLXN1cnZleT91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: accenture.com ↗</a></p>

Accenture’s survey of 300 technology leaders across 19 industries and 16 countries found that 64% are scaling enterprise AI deployment, but only 7% are optimizing it at scale, according to its [CIO Pulse report](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/88135Ctee0g-41fS1z2Img/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDQtaXR8Y2lvcy1hcmUtZGVwbG95aW5nLWFpLWZhc3Rlci10aGFuLXRoZXktY2FuLW9wZXJhdGlvbmFsaXplLWl0fHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5hY2NlbnR1cmUuY29tL2VuL2luc2lnaHRzL2FpLWRhdGEvY2lvLXB1bHNlLXN1cnZleT91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20). User adoption and training are the most frequently cited barriers. Nearly half still require manual approvals for agents in IT operations.

The gap is between putting AI into workflows and getting measurable value from it. Manual approval can be a sensible safety control, but it also limits how far an agent can operate without human intervention. The survey suggests organizations are still working out where to automate, what to measure, and which controls need to stay in the loop.

<h2 id="uk-businesses-have-less-than-100-days-to-move-off-copper"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/jcTWxqVxw3qE3RsrpkXk-g/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDQtaXR8dWstYnVzaW5lc3Nlcy1oYXZlLWxlc3MtdGhhbi0xMDAtZGF5cy10by1tb3ZlLW9mZi1jb3BwZXJ8fGh0dHBzOi8vd3d3Lml0cHJvLmNvbS9pbmZyYXN0cnVjdHVyZS9uZXR3b3JraW5nL3RpbWUtaXMtcnVubmluZy1vdXQtdG8tc3dpdGNoLXRvLWRpZ2l0YWwtcGhvbmUtbGluZXMtdWstYnVzaW5lc3Nlcy13YXJuZWQ_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">UK businesses have less than 100 days to move off copper</a></h2>

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/jcTWxqVxw3qE3RsrpkXk-g/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDQtaXR8dWstYnVzaW5lc3Nlcy1oYXZlLWxlc3MtdGhhbi0xMDAtZGF5cy10by1tb3ZlLW9mZi1jb3BwZXJ8fGh0dHBzOi8vd3d3Lml0cHJvLmNvbS9pbmZyYXN0cnVjdHVyZS9uZXR3b3JraW5nL3RpbWUtaXMtcnVubmluZy1vdXQtdG8tc3dpdGNoLXRvLWRpZ2l0YWwtcGhvbmUtbGluZXMtdWstYnVzaW5lc3Nlcy13YXJuZWQ_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: itpro.com ↗</a></p>

Lines may support payment terminals, alarms, or building systems, not just desk phones.

Any service still reliant on copper risks disruption after the deadline, and Openreach says there won’t be an extension. Businesses need to inventory what’s connected before swapping phone services; replacing a handset won’t migrate an alarm or a building system that also depends on the line.

Juniper Research expects global identity and access management spending to rise from $26.2 billion in 2026 to $40.5 billion by 2031, a 55% increase, [ITPro reports](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/xahbs5ya8QtBwKY4G-ji8w/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMDQtaXR8aWFtLXNwZW5kaW5nLWlzLWZvcmVjYXN0LXRvLWdyb3ctNTUtYnktMjAzMXx8aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuaXRwcm8uY29tL3NlY3VyaXR5L2lkZW50aXR5LWFuZC1hY2Nlc3MtbWFuYWdlbWVudC1pbnZlc3RtZW50LWlzLXNreXJvY2tldGluZy1oZXJlcy13aHk_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t). The forecast reflects more organizations managing AI agents and other nonhuman identities.

The shift is from authenticating a person once to continuously assessing access as context changes. Agents add a different identity-management problem: they need owners, scoped permissions, and a way to revoke access when a task or service ends. As more software acts on behalf of employees, IAM policies designed only around people will need to catch up.
