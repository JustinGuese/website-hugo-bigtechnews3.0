Surface Laptop 7 and Surface Pro 11 are the loudest victims, particularly freshly imaged machines that haven't pulled Store updates yet. Classic Outlook, Word, and Excel are unaffected. The root cause hasn't been disclosed, and this sits on top of a string of August update issues around printing, PDF export, and RGB peripherals. Admins on ARM fleets should patch the ASR package now and stop imaging new devices until the permanent fix lands.

Anyone who used to vet a single model once and call it done needs a new mental model, because the attack surface is now whatever the agent pulls mid-execution.

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/JzOKq_e9Vrhk5Uqxh-V0lQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMDQtaXR8dGhlLWh1bWFuLWluLXRoZS1sb29wLWlzLW9mdGVuLWh1bWFuLWFkamFjZW50LXRvLXRoZS1sb29wfHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5jaW8uY29tL2FydGljbGUvNDIxNTQ0Mi93aGVuLWFpcy1odW1hbi1pbi10aGUtbG9vcC1yZWFsbHktaXNudC5odG1sP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: cio.com ↗</a></p>

A growing pile of [governance write-ups](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/JzOKq_e9Vrhk5Uqxh-V0lQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMDQtaXR8dGhlLWh1bWFuLWluLXRoZS1sb29wLWlzLW9mdGVuLWh1bWFuLWFkamFjZW50LXRvLXRoZS1sb29wfHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5jaW8uY29tL2FydGljbGUvNDIxNTQ0Mi93aGVuLWFpcy1odW1hbi1pbi10aGUtbG9vcC1yZWFsbHktaXNudC5odG1sP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) makes the same point: many "human in the loop" setups give overseers the ability to see or flag a decision, but not to stop or override it. Cloudflare's Doug Shepherd calls the pattern performative governance, because it gets projects through review without doing the actual risk work. The fix is mechanical: overseers need the domain knowledge to act when the AI is not in the loop, and the authority to actually intervene. Treat it like any other operational control, with drills, not slideware.

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/DdxeX3wM4io8S--uWzg6nQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMDQtaXR8bmFzYXMtcGVvcGxlLWtub3dsZWRnZS1ncmFwaHx8aHR0cHM6Ly9tZW1ncmFwaC5jb20vYmxvZy9uYXNhLW1lbWdyYXBoLXBlb3BsZS1rbm93bGVkZ2UtZ3JhcGg_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: memgraph.com ↗</a></p>

NASA's People Analytics team built a [People Knowledge Graph](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/DdxeX3wM4io8S--uWzg6nQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMDQtaXR8bmFzYXMtcGVvcGxlLWtub3dsZWRnZS1ncmFwaHx8aHR0cHM6Ly9tZW1ncmFwaC5jb20vYmxvZy9uYXNhLW1lbWdyYXBoLXBlb3BsZS1rbm93bGVkZ2UtZ3JhcGg_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t) using Memgraph on Docker and EC2, an on-prem Ollama LLM server, S3, and GQLAlchemy. The graph connects employees, skills, projects, and career paths, and exposes queries like "who has worked on similar AI projects across centers" plus a GraphRAG chatbot for natural-language skill discovery. The interesting part is the stack: graph traversal plus LLM-based skill extraction, all on secure internal AWS. For anyone running expert-find or staffing analytics, the same shape works: keep the model inside your perimeter, let it enrich a graph, query with Cypher or NL.
