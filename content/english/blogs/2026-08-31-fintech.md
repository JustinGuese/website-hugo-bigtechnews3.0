Stripe and Advent International have [walked away from their $50B+ pursuit of PayPal](https://qz.com/stripe-advent-paypal-bid-dropped-082826?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com), leaving the payments company to soldier on with its turnaround. The consortium's $60.50-per-share offer valued PayPal above $53 billion; the board called it too low. PayPal fell as much as 16% in premarket trading. Block had already exited the group, and PayPal's market cap sits around $52.6 billion. A renewed approach isn't off the table, but for now CEO Enrique Lores keeps running the restructuring he planned, workforce cuts and all.

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://www.socure.com/news-and-press/strategic-growth-investment-fravity-acquisition?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: socure.com ↗</a></p>

[Socure raised a strategic growth investment](https://www.socure.com/news-and-press/strategic-growth-investment-fravity-acquisition?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com) at a $5.2 billion valuation led by Summit Partners and acquired Fravity, an agentic fraud and compliance automation shop. Fravity's tech will land inside Socure's [RiskOS](https://www.socure.com?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com) platform as **RiskOS_Agents**. Socure says it closed Q2 with $364 million in ARR, 63% year-over-year growth, and more than 3,000 customers. The pitch is the usual modern-stack one: proprietary data, first-party agents, and closed-loop decisioning to automate identity, fraud, and compliance operations that still run on swivel-chair review.

<h2 id="ninth-circuit-calls-sports-event-contracts-gambling-not-swaps"><a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/28/appeals-court-rules-against-prediction-markets-tees-up-scotus-fight.html?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Ninth Circuit calls sports-event contracts gambling, not swaps</a></h2>

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/28/appeals-court-rules-against-prediction-markets-tees-up-scotus-fight.html?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: cnbc.com ↗</a></p>

A three-judge panel of the [Ninth Circuit ruled against Kalshi, Crypto.com, and Robinhood](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/28/appeals-court-rules-against-prediction-markets-tees-up-scotus-fight.html?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com), denying injunctive relief against the Nevada Gaming Control Board and siding with the 44 states that treat sports-event contracts as illegal sports betting. The court held these products are not CFTC-regulated "swaps," undercutting the CFTC's claim of exclusive jurisdiction over event contracts. The decision directly conflicts with an April Third Circuit ruling that reached the opposite conclusion, which guarantees a circuit split and makes a high-court showdown likely. The CFTC has already sued nine states and looks ready to escalate.

For product and engineering teams building on these platforms, this means the regulatory floor under sports-event contracts in the western half of the country just got thinner overnight. Crypto.com and Robinhood's exposure is mostly product mix; Kalshi's is existential for its largest vertical.

<h2 id="ai-is-creating-new-fintech-problems-worth-solving"><a href="https://www.fintechbrainfood.com/p/ai-customer-problems?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">AI is creating new fintech problems worth solving</a></h2>

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://www.fintechbrainfood.com/p/ai-customer-problems?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: fintechbrainfood.com ↗</a></p>

A fintech industry analysis argues the biggest AI opportunities aren't old workflows done faster - they're [entirely new customer jobs](https://www.fintechbrainfood.com/p/ai-customer-problems?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com) created by AI itself: model spend management, model routing, agent identity, agent payments, and compute procurement. The framework sorts companies into AI adopters, beneficiaries, and natives. The concrete anchors: Ramp's recent $750M raise at a $44B valuation, with its Router tool claiming 40% savings on model costs, and Stripe's reported $7B-plus stack of payments, Metronome billing, and OpenRouter routing across 400+ models as the orchestration case study. The winners will own the orchestration layer, control a trusted point like identity or settlement, or become the easiest product for agents to call.

<h2 id="moats-when-intelligence-is-a-flood"><a href="https://x.com/nayakkayak/status/2092276375935963537?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Moats when intelligence is a flood</a></h2>

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://x.com/nayakkayak/status/2092276375935963537?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: x.com ↗</a></p>

A separate [opinion thread](https://x.com/nayakkayak/status/2092276375935963537?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com) argues that AI behaves like a utility with unbounded demand, so the traditional "moat" playbook - data, models, head start - is mostly useless against the flood of intelligence pouring out of foundation labs. Instead, value accrues to companies that build the "dams and waterways" that diffuse AI into real-world outcomes: orchestrating networks, accumulating workflow data, giving customers control over their AI deployments, climbing to higher-value abstractions, pricing against outcomes, and being the trusted layer models can't replace. The author notes GPT 5.5+ and Opus 4.5+ class models are already broadly available while institutional change still lags (electricity took ~40 years to show up in productivity numbers), which opens a short-lived arbitrage window for the companies that can sell the integration.

<h2 id="visa-expands-an-open-source-ai-cyber-defense-harness"><a href="https://www.finextra.com/pressarticle/110747/visa-expands-open-source-ai-cyber-defence-system?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Visa expands an open-source AI cyber defense harness</a></h2>

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://www.finextra.com/pressarticle/110747/visa-expands-open-source-ai-cyber-defence-system?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: finextra.com ↗</a></p>

[Visa announced enhancements](https://www.finextra.com/pressarticle/110747/visa-expands-open-source-ai-cyber-defence-system?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com) to its Visa Vulnerability Agentic Harness (VVAH), an open-source, model-agnostic AI cybersecurity framework originally developed via Anthropic's Project Glasswing. The new bits: closed-loop remediation that feeds structured feedback back to refine fixes without restarting the run, flexible model choice (Anthropic, OpenAI, or any model swapped via config rather than code changes), and real-time progress views. Visa also expanded its Consulting & Analytics Cybersecurity Advisory Practice around it. The stated goal is reducing Mean Time to Adapt (MTTA) from weeks to hours. For security teams, the model-agnostic hook is the meaningful one: no rewrites when swapping models in or out.

[RQD Clearing raised $74 million](https://fintech.global/2026/08/28/rqd-lands-74m-as-clearing-infrastructure-race-heats-up/?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com) in a minority growth round led by Bain Capital Tech Opportunities, with ABN AMRO Clearing Bank and Nyca Partners participating. The money funds expansion into North America, Asia, and the Middle East and pushes the digital assets and tokenization roadmap. The traction numbers are concrete: year-to-date 543M+ ledger transactions, 515M equity transactions covering 69.5B shares at roughly $2T notional - about 2.43% of the NMS equities market - and 64.8M options contracts at $120.7B premium and $3.93T notional, about 0.63% of the US options market. That's a real slice of post-trade flow on a stack that didn't exist a decade ago.

<h2 id="stripe-buys-clerky-to-own-the-startup-legal-stack"><a href="https://finovate.com/stripe-acquires-clerky-to-offer-legal-infrastructure-for-startups/?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Stripe buys Clerky to own the startup legal stack</a></h2>

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://finovate.com/stripe-acquires-clerky-to-offer-legal-infrastructure-for-startups/?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: finovate.com ↗</a></p>

[Stripe acquired Clerky](https://finovate.com/stripe-acquires-clerky-to-offer-legal-infrastructure-for-startups/?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com), the 2011-founded startup legal infrastructure provider handling incorporation, board actions, SAFEs, convertible notes, hiring docs, and equity issuances. Terms weren't disclosed. Clerky slots on top of Stripe Atlas (launched 2016 for incorporation, EIN, and 83(b) elections), adding deeper legal workflows and the attorney network Stripe lacked. The strategic logic is funnel placement: Stripe wants to meet founders at incorporation and fundraising - years before they need payments - and keep them through scale. Atlas becomes a wedge rather than a checkout button.

<h2 id="walmart-finally-flips-on-apple-pay-and-google-pay"><a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/21/walmart-to-finally-start-accepting-apple-pay-and-google-pay/?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Walmart finally flips on Apple Pay and Google Pay</a></h2>

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/21/walmart-to-finally-start-accepting-apple-pay-and-google-pay/?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: techcrunch.com ↗</a></p>

[Walmart will start accepting Apple Pay and Google Pay](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/21/walmart-to-finally-start-accepting-apple-pay-and-google-pay/?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com) on August 24 at select Walmart and Sam's Club locations, rolling out to every store by end of 2026 and all fuel stations by mid-2027. This reverses a years-long stance in which Walmart routed tap-to-pay through its own Walmart Pay and Scan-and-Go, after the CurrentC consortium flopped in 2016. The market context: Apple Pay is already accepted at roughly 85% of US retailers. For payments teams, this is the last big US holdout coming over the line and finally settling the question of whether a merchant of Walmart's scale can keep a third-party-tap-to-pay firewall up indefinitely.

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2026/08/27/291453.htm?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: carriermanagement.com ↗</a></p>

A Pew Research survey from June 2026 finds [US adults under 30 are now more concerned than excited about AI](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2026/08/27/291453.htm?utm_source=brief-tech-news.com) - a reversal from being AI's biggest enthusiasts since tracking began in 2021. Nearly three-quarters expect AI to reduce job availability, up from 61% two years ago. The cohort-specific concern lines up with the disappearance of the junior-tier roles that have historically been workforce entry points. Across all ages, over half of Americans now express AI concern, while people already established are growing more positive. For anyone hiring or planning headcount around AI tooling, the talent market just got more complicated at the entry level.
