Block propagation across heterogeneous physical links has long favored co-located datacenter builders over residential nodes. A new Ethereum research proposal, [Informed Diffusion](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/l_P0HzXfHCAhsyI6iDLqDw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMTQtY3J5cHRvfGluZm9ybWVkLWRpZmZ1c2lvbi1zbGFzaGluZy1ob21lLWJ1aWxkZXItYmxvY2stZGVsaXZlcnktdG8tMDYtc2Vjb25kc3x8aHR0cHM6Ly9ldGhyZXNlYXIuY2gvdC9pbmZvcm1lZC1kaWZmdXNpb24tbWFraW5nLWhvbWUtYnVpbGRlcnMtcHVibGlzaC1mYXN0LzI2MTUxP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ), extends EIP-8411 to cut residential block delivery from 6.3 seconds down to 0.63 seconds at the 99th percentile. This latency outperforms the standard 1.8-second propagation metric observed across commercial datacenter relays.

The architecture relies on four layers: data pipelining, chunking with push/pull announcements, erasure coding, and neighbor-aware chunk scheduling. Crucially, the protocol only introduces one wire-level modification: adding lightweight `HAVE` bitmaps to current libp2p GossipSub control envelopes. By allowing peers to advertise which erasure-coded shards they possess before attempting transfer, network bandwidth consumption drops from 4.4 redundant payload copies to 1.7 copies. Simulations verify that delivery stays under the strict 3.0-second attestation budget even when 50% of peers intentionally withhold packets on links throttled to 10 Mbps upstream.
