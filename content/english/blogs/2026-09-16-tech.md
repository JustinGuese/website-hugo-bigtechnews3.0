OpenAI is reportedly considering a pre-IPO funding round that would value the company at more than $1.2 trillion, according to [the Wall Street Journal](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/SbaCShaJVB18RA4nNCswOA/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMTYtdGVjaHx0aGUtMTItdHJpbGxpb24tcXVlc3Rpb258fGh0dHBzOi8vd3d3Lndzai5jb20vdGVjaC9haS9vcGVuYWktY29uc2lkZXJzLXByZS1pcG8tZnVuZGluZy1yb3VuZC1hdC1tb3JlLXRoYW4tMS0yLXRyaWxsaW9uLXZhbHVhdGlvbi01NDU1NTI5NT9zdD1MRjVXS24mcmVmbGluaz1kZXNrdG9wd2Vic2hhcmVfcGVybWFsaW5rJnV0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ). The round is being delayed, and the reason given is the same one that has dogged every frontier lab for the past year: AI safety. But the scale here is what matters. We are not talking about a Series C for a hot startup; we are talking about a private company raising money at a valuation that would put it in the top tier of global corporations, all before a single public market listing.

The user numbers explain the sticker shock. OpenAI says it has crossed one billion active users, with more than 200 million businesses using its models. For context, that is roughly one in eight people on the planet using a product from a company that is still technically private. The delay over safety is a signal that even the most aggressive players are feeling the pressure to be seen as responsible, but the fact that the round is still being considered at that valuation means the market is not exactly pricing in a regulatory crackdown.

SpaceX has set a target of September 22 for the first orbital attempt of its Starship rocket, its 14th mission overall. The [launch window](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/P4WbcXX_PieBL1wJkFPvzw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMTYtdGVjaHxzdGFyc2hpcHMtZmlyc3QtcmVhbC1zaG90LWF0LW9yYml0fHxodHRwczovL2Fyc3RlY2huaWNhLmNvbS9zcGFjZS8yMDI2LzA5L3NwYWNleC1zZXRzLWxhdW5jaC1kYXRlLWZvci1maXJzdC1zdGFyc2hpcC1vcmJpdGFsLWZsaWdodC8_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t) is 75 minutes long, opening at 12:15 UTC. The upper stage will carry 26 V3 Starlink satellites to a 275 km orbit and is expected to complete six orbits over roughly 10 hours before deorbiting.

For engineers, this is the milestone that separates the "big rocket" marketing from the "fully reusable super heavy lift" reality. The flight profile includes a boostback burn and a controlled re-entry for the Super Heavy booster, which, if it works, validates the specific failure-mode corrections from the previous test flights. If the upper stage survives re-entry and splashes down intact, the era of rapidly reusable orbital-class hardware is no longer theoretical.

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/AnKIgYvqyTUAuuXhEuGfAg/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMTYtdGVjaHx0aGUtZnJ1aXQtZmx5LXRoYXQtd29udC1xdWl0fHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5ueXRpbWVzLmNvbS8yMDI2LzA5LzE1L3RlY2hub2xvZ3kvZnJ1aXQtZmx5LWJyYWluLW1hcC1nb29nbGUuaHRtbD91bmxvY2tlZF9hcnRpY2xlX2NvZGU9MS5CbEUubnVCWC40OHJwbXdMRWVvMS0mYW1wO3NtaWQ9dXJsLXNoYXJlJmFtcDt1dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: nytimes.com ↗</a></p>

A complete map of a fruit fly's brain, all 166,000 neurons, is now being used to train AI models, and the results are equal parts impressive and absurd. According to [the New York Times](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/uMId5PyinVe9ak0s2LJ0aw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMTYtdGVjaHx0aGUtZnJ1aXQtZmx5LXRoYXQtd29udC1xdWl0fHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5ueXRpbWVzLmNvbS8yMDI2LzA5LzE1L3RlY2hub2xvZ3kvZnJ1aXQtZmx5LWJyYWluLW1hcC1nb29nbGUuaHRtbD91bmxvY2tlZF9hcnRpY2xlX2NvZGU9MS5CbEUubnVCWC40OHJwbXdMRWVvMS0mc21pZD11cmwtc2hhcmUmdXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t), researchers have used the connectome to train models that can complete tasks like parallel parking a car, playing Doom, and trading cryptocurrencies.

The punchline is that a fruit fly's brain, despite its size, is a remarkably efficient pattern-matching machine. If we can get that level of performance out of 166,000 neurons, it raises uncomfortable questions about how much of our own "intelligence" is just a very large lookup table. For anyone building on top of LLMs, this is a reminder that the current architecture is still a long way from anything resembling biological efficiency.

Agility Robotics has updated its Digit humanoid with a new trick: it will stop and squat to avoid harming human coworkers. The [new behavior](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/nxwODFG3nB-xuvyQoCIVCw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMTYtdGVjaHxhZ2lsaXR5cy1yb2JvdC1sZWFybnMtdG8tY3JvdWNofHxodHRwczovL2Fyc3RlY2huaWNhLmNvbS9haS8yMDI2LzA5L2FnaWxpdHlzLW5ldy1odW1hbm9pZC1yb2JvdC13aWxsLXN0b3Atc3F1YXQtdG8tYXZvaWQtaGFybWluZy1odW1hbi1jb3dvcmtlcnMvP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ) is a direct response to the 65,000+ hours Digit has already logged at sites including GXO, Schaeffler, Amazon, and Toyota Motor Manufacturing Canada.

The squat is a practical solution to a real problem: a 140-pound robot falling on you is a workplace injury. Rather than trying to make the robot faster or stronger, Agility is teaching it to lower its center of gravity and present a smaller target when a human is near. It is a small thing, but it is the kind of safety engineering that separates a demo from a production deployment in a warehouse.

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/PzpZEx7GiIVDSgyHrW6G0w/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMTYtdGVjaHxnb29nbGVzLWdlbWluaS1saXZlLWdvZXMtZnVsbC1icm93c2VyfHxodHRwczovL3NpbW9ud2lsbGlzb24ubmV0LzIwMjYvU2VwLzE1L2dlbWluaS1saXZlLz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: simonwillison.net ↗</a></p>

A third-party web UI is now demonstrating Google's Gemini Live models directly in the browser, using a WebSocket endpoint (`wss://generativelanguage.googleapis.com/ws/google.ai.generativelanguage.v1alpha.GenerativeService.BidiGenerateContent`) and the Web Audio API for capture and playback. As [Simon Willison notes](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/PzpZEx7GiIVDSgyHrW6G0w/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMTYtdGVjaHxnb29nbGVzLWdlbWluaS1saXZlLWdvZXMtZnVsbC1icm93c2VyfHxodHRwczovL3NpbW9ud2lsbGlzb24ubmV0LzIwMjYvU2VwLzE1L2dlbWluaS1saXZlLz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20), there is no server-side audio processing in the loop; the models handle real-time bidirectional streaming natively.

For developers, this is significant. It means you can build low-latency voice interfaces that run entirely in the browser, no dedicated audio pipeline required. The models can be interrupted mid-sentence, which is the kind of interaction pattern that feels natural to users but has been notoriously difficult to implement with traditional request/response APIs. The architecture is worth studying if you are planning to build anything with voice input in the next year.

[According to the New York Times](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/TqoGuWKuA0larL1o2cd5Uw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMTYtdGVjaHx6dWNrZXJiZXJnLWZpcmVzLWJhY2stb24tYWktc2FmZXR5fHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5ueXRpbWVzLmNvbS8yMDI2LzA5LzE1L3RlY2hub2xvZ3kvbWFyay16dWNrZXJiZXJnLWFudGhyb3BpYy1haS1zYWZldHkuaHRtbD91bmxvY2tlZF9hcnRpY2xlX2NvZGU9MS5CbEUubVpENy5oZHdGNThwb01pSHgmc21pZD11cmwtc2hhcmUmdXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t), he acknowledged the risks of AI but argued that companies are already incentivized to keep users safe and slow progress on their own. The subtext here is that "safety" is becoming a competitive moat. When a company like Anthropic talks about alignment, it is also signaling to regulators that it should be trusted with more compute. Zuckerberg's counter is that the market will sort it out, which is easy to say when you are not the one leading on safety research.

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/WX-GQqBulRw8dQ2FAzQ4ow/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMTYtdGVjaHxzZW5hdGUta2lsbHMtdGhlLWNyeXB0by1iaWxsfHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5ueXRpbWVzLmNvbS8yMDI2LzA5LzE1L3RlY2hub2xvZ3kvc2VuYXRlLWJsb2Nrcy1jcnlwdG8tYmlsbC5odG1sP3VubG9ja2VkX2FydGljbGVfY29kZT0xLkJsRS5EVzdnLmtfME5FdXE2LUp3RiZhbXA7c21pZD11cmwtc2hhcmUmYW1wO3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9YnJpZWYtdGVjaC1uZXdzLmNvbQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: nytimes.com ↗</a></p>

[Democrats wanted stronger language](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/9RNglngb9yapKMMD71gF3Q/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMTYtdGVjaHxzZW5hdGUta2lsbHMtdGhlLWNyeXB0by1iaWxsfHxodHRwczovL3d3dy5ueXRpbWVzLmNvbS8yMDI2LzA5LzE1L3RlY2hub2xvZ3kvc2VuYXRlLWJsb2Nrcy1jcnlwdG8tYmlsbC5odG1sP3VubG9ja2VkX2FydGljbGVfY29kZT0xLkJsRS5EVzdnLmtfME5FdXE2LUp3RiZzbWlkPXVybC1zaGFyZSZ1dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) to prevent public officials from using crypto to make money, and the bill died in the Senate. <p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/dHg3c3mREldY0o-WULR13w/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMTYtdGVjaHx0YWlsY2F0LW5ldGNhdC1idXQtb3Zlci10YWlsc2NhbGV8fGh0dHBzOi8vZ2l0aHViLmNvbS90YWlsc2NhbGUvdGFpbGNhdD91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: github.com ↗</a></p>

Tailscale has open-sourced [Tailcat](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/dHg3c3mREldY0o-WULR13w/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMTYtdGVjaHx0YWlsY2F0LW5ldGNhdC1idXQtb3Zlci10YWlsc2NhbGV8fGh0dHBzOi8vZ2l0aHViLmNvbS90YWlsc2NhbGUvdGFpbGNhdD91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20), a remix that provides netcat-like functionality over Tailscale's data plane without its control plane. The design is lightweight and userspace-only, which means you get secure, NAT-traversing peer-to-peer connections without the infrastructure dependencies of the full Tailscale stack.

If you have ever needed to pipe a file between two machines that are both behind NATs, this is the tool you did not know you wanted.

<p class="small mb-4"><a href="https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/Qj4VwBC_i00XUtwl-YPfsQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMTYtdGVjaHx0aGUtaW1hZ2UtcGlwZWxpbmUtdGhhdC1kZWNpZGVzLWV2ZXJ5dGhpbmd8fGh0dHBzOi8vdHJwZXZza2kuY29tL2Jsb2cvaW1hZ2UtbW9kZWwtZGVjaWRlLWV2ZXJ5dGhpbmc_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: trpevski.com ↗</a></p>

A [blog post from trpevski.com](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/Qj4VwBC_i00XUtwl-YPfsQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMDktMTYtdGVjaHx0aGUtaW1hZ2UtcGlwZWxpbmUtdGhhdC1kZWNpZGVzLWV2ZXJ5dGhpbmd8fGh0dHBzOi8vdHJwZXZza2kuY29tL2Jsb2cvaW1hZ2UtbW9kZWwtZGVjaWRlLWV2ZXJ5dGhpbmc_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t) walks through an image generation pipeline where the model does not just generate an image; it makes decisions.
