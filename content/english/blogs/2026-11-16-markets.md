<h2 id="nvidia-a-governance-trade-not-a-demand-call">Nvidia: a governance trade, not a demand call</h2>

We have been negative on Nvidia since late October on deceleration grounds. The resignation is a different negative - governance - and it lands while every hyperscaler is already shopping for reasons to diversify. The 13% drop prices the exit; what it does not price is the reinstatement fight, with 115,000+ signatures including 5,965 current employees, and the strategic drift of an interim CEO through peak capex season. A resignation does not cancel purchase orders, but it hands every hyperscaler diversification committee the cover it has been waiting for - which is why the more interesting exposure is in the beneficiaries: custom silicon at Broadcom, merchant GPUs at AMD, and Google's TPU stack, which open-sourced its own accelerant today (below). Two counter-signals to respect: Jane Street more than doubled its stake to 23.8M shares (~$5.44B) into the drop, though 13Fs omit shorts and options, and the demand trendline is unchanged. If the board reinstates quickly, the governance discount closes fast - that is the risk to the short. Meanwhile the product engine ran fine without the CEO: GB300's NVFP4 4-bit training at 6x BF16 throughput (with a quantization-error tradeoff) and a BirdDreams world model beating OmniDreams in human evals are both incremental but real.

<h2 id="microsofts-outage-is-a-sales-pitch-for-everyone-else">Microsoft&#x27;s outage is a sales pitch for everyone else</h2>

The obvious name is Microsoft; the unpriced action is the beneficiary list. AWS shipped cross-account Secrets Manager replication the same day the outage made single-vendor dependence the board-level topic. Google Cloud took its Organization Policy MCP server and natural-language constraint authoring GA across 100+ policies, deepening the agentic governance moat. Cloudflare's Private Origins went GA into hybrid architectures. And Anthropic's five-product enterprise suite - Meetings, Whiteboards, Sheets, Data, connectors - lands days before a Nov 19 - 20 IPO at up to $1.4T on revenue up 1,206% to $33.2B, with Claude Meetings aimed straight at Copilot's meeting features. A challenger with $33B of revenue does not dent a franchise Microsoft's size in a quarter, but it reprices the growth multiple, and it is buying its launch window during Microsoft's worst operational stretch. The counterpoint inside Microsoft's own day: GitHub retiring six models (Claude Fable 5, Haiku 4.5, Gemini 3.8 Flash, Gemini 4 Argon, Grok 4.6, Kimi K3) on December 14, 2026 consolidates Copilot's model menu and cuts rivals' distribution, and Copilot 1.2.0's GHES support extends reach on-premises.
