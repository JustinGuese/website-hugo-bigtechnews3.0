Qualcomm and Huawei have signed a comprehensive, multi-year cross-licensing agreement covering fundamental IP in 5G, networking, general compute, and AI. As part of the pact, Qualcomm is outright purchasing a tranche of Huawei's U.S. patents across compute, AI, and networking fields.

The move represents a major recalibration of telecom and silicon IP risk. By locking in terms under Fair, Reasonable, and Non-Discriminatory (FRAND) principles, both companies establish mutual clearance for cellular baseband and accelerator architectures despite severe geopolitical friction and export controls. For engineering teams building hardware stacks, it signals relative stability for core standard-essential patents (SEPs) across upcoming 5G-Advanced and modem roadmaps.

A worldwide audit of IPv6 allocations published by [IPinfo reveals a massive routing discrepancy](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/ZKQLOMgbaSukF1uEmmQqyQ/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMDYtaXR8Z2xvYmFsLWlwdjYtcm91dGluZy1hdWRpdC1leHBvc2VzLWEtMjAtYmxhY2staG9sZXx8aHR0cHM6Ly9jb21tdW5pdHkuaXBpbmZvLmlvL3QvcmVnaXN0ZXJlZC1idXQtbm90LXJvdXRlZC13aGF0LWEtZ2xvYmFsLWF1ZGl0LW9mLWlwdjYtc2F5cy1hYm91dC1kZXBsb3ltZW50Lzc0MzQ_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t). Comparing Regional Internet Registry (RIR) databases against live BGP tables and the IPv6 Hitlist project, researchers found that while 92.5% of allocated netblocks are advertised in BGP, only 79.4% of the aggregate address space is actually reachable.

The 20.6% shortfall is heavily concentrated in large, dormant legacy allocations and carrier-grade NAT backbones, with the disparity hitting hardest across the Global South. Cross-validation against live scanning data confirmed 28.1 million distinct responsive hosts sitting in monitored prefixes. For network engineers, the takeaway is stark: registry-based IP utilization metrics massively overstate active routing density, complicating capacity planning and automated geolocation setups.

* **Universal Variant Shredding:** Shredded Variant writes - breaking nested sub-columns out into typed physical Parquet leaves while retaining the unified semi-structured query interface - now extend past Spark to Apache Flink, Kafka Connect, and generic writers. Uniform typing across records is now strictly enforced during shredding.
* **Native Geospatial Columns:** Iceberg tables now support native `geometry` and `geography` types stored as Well-Known Binary (WKB) inside Parquet and Avro, with Spark 4.1 providing the reference read/write engine.
* **Flink Vector Deletes:** Apache Flink can now resolve equality deletes directly into row-position deletion vectors, cutting down the compute needed for streaming changelog compaction.
