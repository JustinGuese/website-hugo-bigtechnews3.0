Airwallex walked away from a cross-border alliance with Ramp because its venture backers decided Ramp's lunch looked better to eat than to share. The failed deal exposes the fundamental instability of modern spend management: when every player's margins depend on capturing software subscription revenue, FX spreads, and interchange simultaneously, no partnership can survive feature convergence. 

At the same time, legacy card networks and tier-one banks are aggressively paring down their human payrolls while pointing to agentic commerce and automation. Between Barclays and American Express, roughly 8,500 jobs are disappearing - and engineering orgs inside the banking stack are finding out they are not exempt from the chopping block.

A planned technical partnership between corporate card platform Ramp and global rails provider Airwallex [reportedly collapsed](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/Jv6wQ2E78krjm78ikVbh8g/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMjYtZmludGVjaHxyYW1wLWFuZC1haXJ3YWxsZXgtdHVybi1mcm9tLWludGVncmF0aW9uLXBhcnRuZXJzLXRvLXplcm8tc3VtLXJpdmFsc3x8aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlaW5mb3JtYXRpb24uY29tL2FydGljbGVzL3JhbXAtaW52ZXN0b3JzLXdlbnQtbnVjbGVhci1haXJ3YWxsZXgtZG9vbWVkLWRlYWw_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t) after Airwallex investors pushed CEO Jack Zhang to kill the deal. Ramp had intended to leverage Airwallex's cross-border payment infrastructure and local clearing connections to scale its international spend capabilities. Airwallex's board calculated that handing Ramp easy global routing was effectively subsidizing its most dangerous competitive threat.

Ramp co-founder Eric Glyman tried to preserve the integration, but the discussions unraveled in April 2025. With Airwallex rolling out spend management and expense software, and Ramp building direct international payment pipelines, both platforms are pursuing the same enterprise accounts. For operations teams, the breakdown means evaluating these providers as monolithic, end-to-end rivals rather than expecting clean API interop between domestic expense workflows and international treasury pipes.

[Mesh partnered with Visa](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/k7g6TLbUMrtrZfU_TdcLew/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTAtMjYtZmludGVjaHxtZXNoLWFuZC12aXNhLW9yY2hlc3RyYXRlLXBvaW50LW9mLXNhbGUtc3RhYmxlY29pbi1jb252ZXJzaW9uc3x8aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cubWVzaHBheS5jb20vYmxvZy9tZXNoLXgtdmlzYS1zcGVuZC1jcnlwdG8tc3RhYmxlY29pbnMtYXQtMTc1bS1tZXJjaGFudHM_dXRtX3NvdXJjZT1icmllZi10ZWNoLW5ld3MuY29t) to allow wallet users to spend crypto and stablecoins across 175 million merchant locations in 200 countries without manual pre-funding or dedicated physical plastic. Instead of forcing users to top up a prepaid debit account hours in advance, the architecture relies on a just-in-time credential mechanism.

When a consumer taps to pay from a self-custodial or exchange-backed wallet, Mesh intercepts the transaction auth, liquidates the underlying crypto or stablecoin balance, and settles in local fiat across Visa's network in real time. For payment engineers, this pay-from-balance model collapses the multi-step bridge of swapping, transferring, and funding cards into a single synchronous API call, abstracting settlement risk away from the merchant terminal entirely.
