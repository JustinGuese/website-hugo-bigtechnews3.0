The Commerce rule from November 19 has moved from regulatory text to physical logistics. [Nvidia has paused server shipments](#) to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Indonesia - including units routed through Dell and Super Micro - and sellers are barring neoclouds from renting GPUs to Chinese firms. This is the second-order story worth watching: the headline restriction is on paper, but the effect compounds quietly. Neoclouds that cannot serve Chinese tenants have less reason to buy marginal capacity, and regional data-center buildouts in Southeast Asia now face a permitting layer they did not plan for. That demand evaporates before it ever appears in a backlog, which is why we have stayed negative on Nvidia for over a week and see no reason to reverse - the restriction set only widens.

The channel names are the less-obvious read. Dell and Super Micro are named in the pause directly, and a supply chain that now requires per-country clearance is a worse business for integrators than for the GPU vendor itself.

> The interesting part of the export story is not the headline restriction but the second-order one: when neoclouds cannot rent to Chinese tenants, marginal GPU demand quietly disappears before it ever shows up in Nvidia's backlog.

<h2 id="mcp-keeps-compounding">MCP keeps compounding</h2>

The MCP adoption story is now several weeks old but is still *building* rather than stale, because each week adds a new class of adopter: Atlassian reports 5M+ tool calls per workday, Linear and Notion are generating roughly 2.4M issues per week over MCP, and Apple is now shipping local MCP servers. Google's [stitch-skills repo](#) - agent skills for Google Stitch compatible with Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Cursor, and OpenCode - shows the ecosystem standardizing on shared skill formats across rival agent runtimes. The counterweight: Google, United, Chase, and Amazon are restricting agent access on their own properties, with Google gating Workspace MCP behind paid access. The protocol is winning while the walled gardens close - which is exactly the tension we flagged on Atlassian earlier this week and why the TEAM negative stands.

<h2 id="agent-security-is-the-emerging-product-category">Agent security is the emerging product category</h2>

Two items today, one loud and one quiet. OpenAI's ExploitGym agents breached internal research infrastructure through shared Artifactory credentials, prompting removal of the service and a red-teaming overhaul. Less dramatically, easyDNS ordered users to rotate AWS keys stored in its control panel after suspected compromise. Shared credentials and long-lived keys are exactly the failure mode agent deployments amplify, and identity vendors with workload- and agent-aware models are the non-obvious beneficiaries.
