The worst month in spot bitcoin ETF history is still getting worse, and the bear market call that held since June just got formally withdrawn. US spot bitcoin ETFs recorded $5.39B of net outflows in November, already a record with days left, and Wednesday's $453.1M marked a seventh straight outflow day. IBIT led the bleeding with $329.7M. Bitcoin traded near $60,500 after dipping to about $57,300, and Galaxy now treats a daily close below $58,525 as the start of a move to a lower low.

But the day's real story is entropy, or the lack of it. Coldcard, the hardware wallet that security engineers trusted precisely because its firmware was open source and heavily audited, just admitted its seeds came from a software RNG for five years. That is not a small bug. That is a fundamental failure of the one thing a hardware wallet exists to do.

> Coldcard's five-year software RNG flaw left as little as 40 bits of entropy, and attackers used it to steal 1,778.84 BTC.

[Coinkite's postmortem](https://ecommerce-api.datafortress.cloud/r/brieftechnews/HWutEOUrdMS9gxJ6KG3cmw/djF8d2VifDIwMjYtMTEtMjctY3J5cHRvfGNvbGRjYXJkcy1maXZlLXllYXItc29mdHdhcmUtcm5nLWRpc2FzdGVyfHxodHRwczovL2Jsb2cuY29pbmtpdGUuY29tL2NvbGRjYXJkLXBvc3Rtb3J0ZW0tcHVibGlzaGVkLz91dG1fc291cmNlPWJyaWVmLXRlY2gtbmV3cy5jb20) reveals that COLDCARD firmware generated wallet seeds from March 2021 through July 30, 2026, using MicroPython's Yasmarang software PRNG instead of the STM32 hardware RNG. That left about 40 bits of effective entropy on Mk2/Mk3 devices and 72 bits on Mk4, Mk5, and Q models. Attackers began regenerating affected private keys offline on July 29, the day before the fix shipped.

The numbers are brutal. Galaxy Research counted 1,778.84 BTC stolen from more than 8,600 addresses. Coinkite has matched 300 loss-related support tickets to about 0.2% of its estimated user base, or 236 accounts. At least 15 independent reviews between 2021 and 2026 missed the defect, including an AI-assisted review with the OpenAI Cybersecurity Harness that produced 85 candidate findings but not this one.

Seeds generated with more than 50 fair, private dice rolls, a strong BIP-39 passphrase, or multisig were not affected. Fixed firmware shipped on July 31, but updates cannot repair seeds created on affected firmware. If you generated a seed on a Coldcard in that window without a passphrase, assume it is compromised. This is the case study for why hardware audits need to verify the RNG path, not just the crypto primitives.
