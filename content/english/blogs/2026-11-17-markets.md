The only event today that changes anything structural is Nvidia's. The [obituary](#) confirms Jensen Huang resigned as CEO on November 12 after 33 years - the tenure that built CUDA in 2006 and bought Mellanox in 2019 - citing defense-contractor classification and espionage allegations. Then Trump publicly backed Huang's reinstatement while the board searches for a successor. Two things follow. First, this is a governance overhang, and it is now political: the successor search has a head of state as a participant, which means the board's choice will be read as a Washington outcome regardless of what it is. Second - and this is the part that is not in the headline - the moat that matters is CUDA, and CUDA is a moat of people and habits. Every hyperscaler already has a cost reason to diversify away from Nvidia. Today they acquired a governance reason too. We have been negative on Nvidia since late October, before any of this; what changed today is that the risk moved from valuation to execution continuity, and we are raising our conviction accordingly. The second-order beneficiaries are AMD and Broadcom's custom-silicon programs, not Nvidia itself - the displaced-name logic works in reverse here: the destabilized incumbent is the headline, the hedge is the trade.

> CUDA is a moat of people and habits, and moats of people do not survive a succession conducted in public with a head of state as a participant.

<h2 id="price-cuts-buy-share-not-premium">Price cuts buy share, not premium</h2>

OpenAI cut GPT-6 Luna pricing 90% and lifted its low-end share from 30% to 81%. Impressive, and close to meaningless: Anthropic still holds 94% of the premium tier. There are two readings - a structural cost advantage that lets OpenAI subsidize share indefinitely, or a margin-blind land grab - and the market cannot distinguish them yet. What the data does settle is the shape of the demand curve: the top of the market is not price-elastic, and the bottom is worthless as a business. That is bad news for Microsoft, whose OpenAI economics now include hosting a model given away at a 90% discount, and quietly validating for Google's premium-tier Gemini positioning. We stay negative on Microsoft and positive on Alphabet, now with better evidence for both.

<h2 id="metered-intelligence">Metered intelligence</h2>

Apple's iOS 27.3 beta puts daily caps on server-side Siri AI and sells headroom through iCloud+ tiers from $2.99/month at 2x to $59.99/month at 20x - the first time Apple has metered AI processing at all. Three effects, in descending order of interest. It converts a growing cost center into a tiered services revenue line. It pushes the marginal user back on-device, which is where Apple's own silicon lives. And it puts a governor on consumer cloud-inference growth - the first real-world experiment in whether consumer AI demand is elastic at the free tier. Anyone modeling consumer inference as a smoothly compounding curve should watch this beta closely.

<h2 id="berlin-arithmetic">Berlin arithmetic</h2>

Tesla confirmed the €3 billion Cybercab factory at Giga Berlin, up to 1 million units/year of capacity for 2029. The same announcement confirms EU rules cap driverless registrations at 1,500 per year, with no unsupervised-driving approval in sight. That is 0.15% of nameplate capacity against a €3 billion plant. Rules can change before 2029, but you do not underwrite a factory on a regulatory bet, and yesterday's neutral does not survive this arithmetic: we are reversing it to negative, on the specific new facts of the capacity figure and the cap arriving together. The long position in the book will be sized accordingly.
